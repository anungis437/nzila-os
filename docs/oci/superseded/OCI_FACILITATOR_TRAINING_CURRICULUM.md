# OCI Facilitator Training Curriculum

**Status:** Canonical training programme for facilitators of the OCI Method™.

**Audience:** Candidates entering OCI facilitator training, training stewards delivering the curriculum, and partner institutions enabling supervised facilitators.

**Method version this curriculum trains for:** **1.0** (effective 2026-05-21).

---

## 1. Purpose of the curriculum

The curriculum prepares a candidate to deliver the OCI Method™ inside a real institution under the discipline that the method requires. It does not prepare candidates to sell the method, brand the method, or extend the method. Those are separate stewardships, held elsewhere.

The curriculum has one institutional commitment: at the close of certification, the certified facilitator must be able to enter an institution, deliver the method without harm, and leave the institution operating better than it entered the engagement.

---

## 2. Eligibility

Candidates must, at minimum, hold:

- Demonstrated experience in institutional settings (union, healthcare, governance, public service, federation, regulated profession) — typically at least seven years,
- Reading fluency in governance documents (constitutions, bylaws, collective agreements, policy manuals, regulatory frameworks),
- Demonstrated discretion handling sensitive institutional information,
- Native or near-native fluency in at least one of the languages the candidate intends to deliver the method in,
- A standing professional reference from a person who has worked with the candidate in an institutional setting.

Candidates without these prerequisites may be admitted to preparatory study under explicit deferred-eligibility status, but are not eligible to begin Module 1 until the prerequisites are met.

---

## 3. Curriculum structure

The curriculum is delivered in eight modules. Total contact time is approximately 80 hours over a 12-week period, plus a supervised practicum.

| Module | Title | Hours | Required reading |

| 3 | Workshop choreography and the standard arc | 10 | Executive workshop flows catalogue, `OCI_WORKSHOP_OPENING_SCRIPT.md` |
| 4 | Discovery framework and the conversation library | 12 | Institutional discovery framework catalogue, continuity conversation prompts catalogue |
| 5 | Governance interpretation and continuity plan drafting | 10 | `OCI_BOARD_OVERVIEW.md`, `OCI_PILOT_SCOPE_TEMPLATE.md` |

Modules are delivered sequentially. Candidates may not enter a later module without completing the earlier modules and their assessments.

## 4. Module detail

### Module 1 — Method doctrine and voice register

Outcomes:

- The candidate can articulate the method's five phases and explain why the pilot collapses Phases 4 and 5 for delivery.
- The candidate can name the eight method principles and give an institutional example of each.
- The candidate can read a passage of marketing-register text and rewrite it in method register without loss of meaning.

Assessment: written reflection (1500–2500 words) on a real institutional reading, with explicit voice-register notes; reviewed by a training steward.

### Module 2 — Recognition and the facilitation guide

Outcomes:

- The candidate can lead a sponsor interpretation conversation that produces a usable summary without diagnostic framing.
- The candidate can identify, in a transcript, the moment recognition has been established and the moment it has been lost.
- The candidate can navigate the facilitation guide and select the correct entry for any given session type.

Assessment: live sponsor interpretation simulation with a training steward in the sponsor role; reviewed against the facilitation guide's success and failure signals.

### Module 3 — Workshop choreography and the standard arc

Outcomes:

- The candidate can deliver each of the five workshop flows in the order the method requires: opening reflection, operational mapping, stewardship recognition, governance realization, stabilization pathway.
- The candidate can recover a workshop in which diagnosis has been offered before recognition.
- The candidate can hold a workshop closing posture that does not commit the institution to actions outside its current consent.

Assessment: two recorded workshop deliveries with a peer cohort acting as institutional participants; reviewed against the workshop flow catalogue's facilitator notes and red lines.

### Module 4 — Discovery framework and the conversation library

Outcomes:

- The candidate can deploy the institutional discovery framework section by section, with synthesis starters returned to the institution rather than retained.
- The candidate can select conversation prompts from the continuity conversation library appropriate to the institution's category and tenure.
- The candidate can identify and decline conversation paths that would invite blame voice, behavioural inference, or surveillance signals.

Assessment: a written engagement plan for a fictional institution, drawing on the discovery framework and the conversation library; reviewed against the editorial register and the anti-surveillance position.

### Module 5 — Governance interpretation and continuity plan drafting

Outcomes:

- The candidate can read a governance document and identify points of interpretation drift without characterising the interpreters.
- The candidate can draft a continuity plan in governance-receivable language.
- The candidate can prepare a governance body ratification briefing that gives the body genuine room to amend, adopt, or decline.

Assessment: a complete continuity plan draft based on a synthetic governance brief; reviewed by a training steward with governance reading fluency.

### Module 6 — Anti-surveillance posture and the AI boundary

Outcomes:

- The candidate can identify, in a proposed engagement scope, any element that would breach the anti-surveillance position.
- The candidate can identify, in any computational tooling proposed for engagement use, any element that would breach the AI boundary.
- The candidate can decline an institution's request for surveillance-adjacent outputs without losing the engagement.

Assessment: a written boundary review of three synthetic engagement proposals, two of which contain seeded boundary breaches; reviewed against the anti-surveillance position and the AI boundary.

### Module 7 — Artefact production, archival, and severance

Outcomes:

- The candidate can produce every method artefact in the form the artefact register requires.
- The candidate can hand artefacts to the institution in a form the institution can archive in its own governance records.
- The candidate can sever operational dependency on the facilitator at engagement close.

Assessment: a closure simulation in which the candidate hands a complete artefact set to a training steward in the institutional witness role.

### Module 8 — Supervised practicum

Outcomes:

- The candidate has co-delivered, under direct supervision of a senior certified facilitator, at least one full pilot engagement or the equivalent in defined sub-engagement scope.
- The candidate has produced or co-produced the full artefact set for the practicum engagement.
- The candidate has received and integrated supervisory feedback recorded against the certification rubric.

Assessment: practicum review by the supervising senior facilitator and a second training steward; outcome recorded against the certification rubric.

---

## 5. Cadence and intake

Cohorts are intentionally small. A cohort is normally limited to between four and eight candidates so that every candidate receives direct feedback at every module.

Cohorts open at most twice per calendar year. There is no urgency-based intake. Candidates are admitted when the cohort can accept them under the cadence the method requires.

---

## 6. Training stewards

The curriculum is delivered by training stewards. A training steward must:

- Hold current OCI Lead Facilitator certification,
- Have delivered at least three full method engagements in distinct institutional sectors,
- Hold no commercial incentive in admitting any particular candidate,
- Have completed a training-steward orientation under a senior method steward.

The list of currently active training stewards is maintained in `docs/oci/OCI_TRAINING_STEWARDS.md` when published.

---

## 7. Withdrawal and removal

A candidate may withdraw from the curriculum at any time without prejudice.

A candidate may be removed from the curriculum if a training steward, with the agreement of one other training steward, records that:

- The candidate has shown a pattern of voice-register breach that has not yielded to feedback, or
- The candidate has shown a pattern of anti-surveillance breach, or
- The candidate has shown a pattern of disregard for institutional consent, or
- The candidate has misrepresented their eligibility.

A removed candidate may petition for re-admission to a future cohort after a stand-down period recorded by the training stewards.

---

## 8. Cost and access

Cost is set by the OCI doctrine maintainers and recorded in `docs/oci/OCI_TRAINING_COSTS.md` when published. The method explicitly funds a defined number of fee-waived seats per cohort for candidates serving institutions whose continuity exposure would otherwise be unaddressed. Fee-waived seats are filled before fee-paying seats.

---

## 9. Relationship to certification

Completion of the curriculum is necessary but not sufficient for certification. Certification is governed by the OCI Facilitator Certification Rubric. The curriculum prepares the candidate to be assessed against the rubric; the rubric determines whether the candidate is certified.
