# OCI Facilitator Certification Rubric

**Status:** Canonical rubric for certifying facilitators of the OCI Method™.

**Audience:** Certification assessors, training stewards, certified facilitators preparing for recertification, and institutions verifying a facilitator's standing.

**Method version this rubric assesses against:** **1.0** (effective 2026-05-21).

---

## 1. Purpose of the rubric

The rubric exists so that certification of an OCI facilitator is a reproducible, governance-receivable judgement made against named dimensions, rather than a relational judgement made by individual assessors. Certification grants a facilitator standing to deliver the method inside institutions. That standing must be earned against criteria the method can defend.

The rubric is binding. Assessors do not depart from it. Candidates are entitled to a written certification decision recorded against every dimension.

---

## 2. Certification levels

The method recognises three certification levels. Levels are not seniority labels; they are scope-of-standing labels.

| Level | Standing |
| --- | --- |
| Associate Facilitator | May co-deliver method engagements under direct supervision of a Lead Facilitator. May not hold sole facilitator responsibility for an engagement. |
| Facilitator | May hold sole facilitator responsibility for engagements in institutional sectors recorded in their certification. May not act as training steward. |
| Lead Facilitator | May hold sole facilitator responsibility for engagements in any institutional sector, may supervise Associate Facilitators, and may act as training steward upon completion of training-steward orientation. |

A facilitator may hold certification in one or more institutional sectors. Sector-scope is recorded explicitly on the certification record.

---

## 3. Assessment dimensions

Every certification decision is made against the following eight dimensions. Each dimension is scored on a four-level scale (see §4). A candidate's certification level is determined by their lowest dimension score, not by an average.

### 3.1 Voice register fidelity

The candidate's spoken and written language during the engagement holds the method's editorial register. Marketing register, productivity register, transformation register, and behavioural-inference register do not appear in the candidate's outputs or in their improvised speech under stress.

Evidence: workshop recordings, written artefacts, sponsor interviews, training-steward observation notes.

### 3.2 Workshop choreography fidelity

The candidate delivers each workshop flow in the order the method requires: opening reflection, operational mapping, stewardship recognition, governance realization, stabilization pathway. The candidate does not collapse, reorder, or skip steps under time pressure.

Evidence: at least three observed workshop deliveries across at least two distinct session types.

### 3.3 Governance interpretation safety

The candidate reads and discusses governance documents with the institution without characterising interpreters or stewards. The candidate produces interpretation-related artefacts in a form the institution's governance body can receive and amend.

Evidence: a governance interpretation register draft for a real or synthetic institution, with assessor commentary.

### 3.4 Anti-surveillance vigilance

The candidate identifies, declines, and explains the decline of any request — institutional, vendor, or internal — that would breach the anti-surveillance position or the AI boundary. The candidate does not let the request pass quietly out of relational deference.

Evidence: at least two recorded decline-and-explain instances, real or simulated, scored against the anti-surveillance position.

### 3.5 Stewardship recognition discipline

The candidate names stewardship density and stewardship burden as institutional facts. The candidate does not characterise individual stewards. The candidate does not produce inputs to personnel decisions, even when invited.

Evidence: stewardship density register draft, plus assessor observation of at least one stewardship recognition workshop step.

### 3.6 Reductive stabilization judgement

The candidate proposes only stabilization moves that reduce institutional fragility without displacing load to less senior or less recognised stewards. When such a move cannot be drafted, the candidate names the deferral honestly.

Evidence: stabilization pathway draft including at least one honest deferral, plus a written defence of one declined displacement proposal.

### 3.7 Artefact production quality

The candidate produces each method artefact in the form the artefact register requires, in the institution's language and governance register, and hands artefacts to the institution in a form the institution can archive without further work.

Evidence: a complete artefact set from a practicum engagement, signed off by the supervising senior facilitator.

### 3.8 Sponsor relationship integrity

The candidate maintains the sponsor relationship as a relationship of institutional consent, not as a relationship of personal loyalty, urgency-creation, or upsell. The candidate does not extend the engagement beyond its scoped boundary, does not propose post-engagement dependencies, and does not propose method amendments mid-engagement.

Evidence: sponsor interview at engagement close, conducted by an assessor outside the engagement.

---

## 4. Scoring scale

Each dimension is scored on the following scale. Scores are recorded with named evidence.

| Score | Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Emerging | The candidate is beginning to demonstrate the dimension. Falls below the threshold required to deliver the method without supervision. |
| 2 | Practising | The candidate demonstrates the dimension with effort and occasional supervision. Sufficient for Associate Facilitator standing. |
| 3 | Demonstrating | The candidate demonstrates the dimension consistently and without supervision. Sufficient for Facilitator standing. |
| 4 | Exemplifying | The candidate demonstrates the dimension consistently, can model it for other candidates, and can recover from situations in which the dimension is under stress. Sufficient for Lead Facilitator standing. |

A candidate's certification level is determined by their lowest dimension score:

- Lowest score is 2 → Associate Facilitator
- Lowest score is 3 → Facilitator
- Lowest score is 4 → Lead Facilitator

A candidate with any dimension scored 1 is not certified. The certification record names every dimension and its score. A candidate is entitled to written assessor commentary on every dimension scored below the candidate's target level.

---

## 5. Assessors

A certification assessment is conducted by two assessors. Both must be Lead Facilitators. At least one must hold training-steward standing. Neither may have a commercial or personal relationship with the candidate that would create dependency on the certification outcome.

Where the candidate has practised in a specific institutional sector, at least one assessor must hold certification in that sector. Where this is not possible, the certification is recorded as cross-sector provisional, and the candidate must complete a sector-specific re-assessment before delivering an engagement in that sector solo.

Assessor disagreement on any dimension is resolved by a third Lead Facilitator selected by the doctrine maintainers. The third assessor's score governs.

---

## 6. Certification record

Every certification decision produces a written record containing:

- Candidate name and contact,
- Certification level granted (Associate Facilitator, Facilitator, Lead Facilitator),
- Sector scope (one or more institutional sectors, or cross-sector provisional),
- Each of the eight dimension scores,
- Assessor commentary on every dimension scored below the candidate's target level,
- Effective date,
- Recertification due date,
- Names of both assessors and, where applicable, the third assessor.

The certification record is held by the doctrine maintainers. The candidate receives a copy. Institutions verifying a facilitator's standing may request confirmation of certification level and sector scope from the doctrine maintainers; assessor commentary is not released to third parties without the candidate's written consent.

---

## 7. Recertification

Certification is held for a defined period and then reassessed.

| Level | Recertification cadence |
| --- | --- |
| Associate Facilitator | Every 12 months until promotion to Facilitator |
| Facilitator | Every 24 months |
| Lead Facilitator | Every 36 months |

Recertification reuses the rubric. A Facilitator whose recertification produces any dimension scored 2 is reassigned to Associate Facilitator standing for the following cycle. A Lead Facilitator whose recertification produces any dimension scored 3 is reassigned to Facilitator standing for the following cycle. A facilitator whose recertification produces any dimension scored 1 is decertified.

---

## 8. Revocation

A facilitator's certification may be revoked outside the recertification cycle if a sponsoring institution, a co-facilitator, a training steward, or the doctrine maintainers record a substantiated breach in any of the following areas:

- Anti-surveillance posture (Dimension 3.4),
- Stewardship recognition discipline (Dimension 3.5),
- Sponsor relationship integrity (Dimension 3.8),
- Misrepresentation of certification level or sector scope.

Revocation is governed by a defined process:

1. The recorded concern is forwarded to the doctrine maintainers in writing.
2. The doctrine maintainers convene a review panel of two Lead Facilitators with no relationship to the concern.
3. The panel interviews the facilitator and the party recording the concern.
4. The panel issues one of: no further action, supervisory return-to-practice, downgrading of certification level, or revocation.
5. The decision is recorded against the facilitator's certification record.

A revoked facilitator may petition the doctrine maintainers for re-entry to the curriculum after a stand-down period determined by the panel's decision.

---

## 9. Holding the rubric itself

The rubric is itself reviewed by the doctrine maintainers at a cadence of no less than every 36 months. Material changes to the rubric apply to certifications and recertifications conducted after the change's effective date; they do not retroactively alter standing already granted.

The rubric is the method's commitment that certification is earned against criteria the method can defend. Institutions and facilitators alike are entitled to that commitment.
