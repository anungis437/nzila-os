# OCI Stabilization Principles

**Status:** Canonical, binding principles for every Product 3 engagement.

**Audience:** Certified facilitators, governance bodies receiving an engagement, internal stewards of the method.

---

## 1. Purpose

These principles govern every act inside a Product 3 engagement. They are not aspirations. A facilitator who breaches any principle is no longer practising the OCI Method™ and is referred to recertification review.

The principles hold uniformly across every mode (Entry Stabilization, Enterprise Stabilization Programme, Stewardship Retainer) and every phase of the Lifecycle.

---

## 2. The principles

### 2.1 Dignity-preserving

Every steward present in the institution at engagement open retains their continuity standing through the engagement and beyond. No stabilization move diminishes a steward's recognition or visibility. Where redistribution is offered, the redistribution adds carriers — it does not subtract from the originating carrier.

### 2.2 Blame-free

The engagement never traces continuity exposure to a named individual. Exposures are institutional. Language inside the engagement does not pose "why do you" questions to stewards or institutions, does not name a steward as the cause of any exposure, and does not produce per-person attribution.

### 2.3 Reductive before additive

The engagement reduces institutional fragility before it adds governance structure. A move that adds a new governance artefact must be sequenced after at least one move that reduces fragility, or the engagement records why this sequencing was inverted.

### 2.4 Governance-safe

Every artefact produced by the engagement is governance-receivable: it can be read by the institution's governance body without requiring facilitator interpretation. The engagement does not produce artefacts that depend on the facilitator's continued presence to be intelligible.

### 2.5 Reviewer-led

The institution's own reviewers — not the facilitator — own every interpretation that touches institutional meaning. The facilitator offers structural readings; the institution's reviewers ratify or decline. The facilitator never substitutes for an institutional reviewer.

### 2.6 Opt-in throughout

Every move in the engagement is opt-in for every steward it touches. A steward who declines participation in a move declines the move. The facilitator does not work around a steward's decline.

### 2.7 Anti-surveillance

The engagement does not collect, infer, or expose per-person behavioural data. The Anti-Surveillance Position ([OCI_ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_POSITION.md](../../OCI_ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_POSITION.md)) governs every act. The engagement does not produce per-person scores, rankings, comparisons, or profiles.

### 2.8 Honest deferral

Deferral is a method outcome. An engagement that defers honestly is more successful than an engagement that forced unready moves. The facilitator names deferrals in the engagement log without euphemism.

### 2.9 No transformation theatre

The engagement does not produce transformation imagery, optimisation language, productivity framing, or consulting boilerplate. The editorial register is institutional, recognition-first, and governance-receivable. See [OCI_METHOD.md](../../OCI_METHOD.md) §7 for the canonical register.

### 2.10 Reversibility as default

Every move is reversible until ratified. Irreversible moves require separately captured governance-body ratification and explicit acknowledgement of irreversibility. See [OCI_INTERVENTION_MODEL.md](OCI_INTERVENTION_MODEL.md) §5.

### 2.11 Consent revocable

Institutional consent may be withdrawn at any point. Steward-level consent may be withdrawn at any point. Withdrawal is recorded, honoured immediately, and closes any move in flight that depended on the withdrawn consent.

### 2.12 Continuity-preserving exit

The engagement closes by returning the institution to its own governance cadence without operational dependency on the facilitator. An engagement that closes with the institution still dependent on the facilitator has not closed.

---

## 3. Enforcement

The principles are enforced by:

- The engagement log — every move records the principles it engages with,
- The facilitator certification rubric ([OCI_FACILITATOR_CERTIFICATION_RUBRIC.md](../OCI_FACILITATOR_CERTIFICATION_RUBRIC.md)),
- The doctrine review process under [OCI_METHOD.md](../../OCI_METHOD.md) §11,
- The tone-ban and blame-regex checks built into the workbook engines and PDF templates that surface engagement outputs.

A principle breach is grounds for engagement pause, facilitator recertification review, and — where the breach is structural — withdrawal of certification.
