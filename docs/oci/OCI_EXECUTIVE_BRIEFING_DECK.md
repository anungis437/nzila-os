# OCI Executive Briefing Deck

**ARTIFACT TYPE:** Institutional Doctrine — Executive Presentation Outline
**DOCTRINE_VERSION:** 1.0.0
**STATUS:** Canonical
**INTENDED USE:** 30-minute executive briefing; rendered to PDF for in-room use; never sent in advance
**PARENT FRAMEWORK:** [OCI Pilot Framework™](./OCI_PILOT_FRAMEWORK.md)

> This document is the canonical outline of the OCI Executive
> Briefing. It is not the deck itself; it is the editorial spine
> from which the deck is rendered. The slides are sparse — at
> most three lines of text per slide — and the facilitator
> carries the substance verbally.

---

## How this briefing is used

The Executive Briefing is delivered in person, in a closed room,
to an executive sponsor and at most three other institutional
leaders. It runs thirty minutes including questions.

The deck is not circulated in advance. It is not left behind. A
written Executive Continuity Brief — produced from the OCRA, not
from this deck — is the artifact the institution keeps.

The briefing is a conversation. The deck is the scaffolding for
the facilitator's discipline, not a substitute for the
facilitator's presence.

---

## The twelve slides

### Slide 1 — Opening recognition

**Title:** What we are here to recognise.

**Body (verbal):** "Most institutions carry their continuity in
a small number of people. The shape of that continuity is rarely
named. Today we look at the shape, together."

**On slide:** the institution's name, the engagement date, and a
single sentence: *"A working session on operational continuity."*

---

### Slide 2 — The question

**Title:** The question we work with.

**On slide:** *"What does your institution carry, and who carries
it?"*

**Body (verbal):** The facilitator names the question and pauses.
The room is not asked to answer aloud. The question is the
posture of the next twenty-five minutes.

---

### Slide 3 — Three things we observe

**Title:** Three things institutions notice late.

**On slide:** three short lines —
*"Memory leaves before it is asked to."*
*"Decisions are remembered without their rationale."*
*"Continuity sits inside calendars, not records."*

**Body (verbal):** Each line is held for approximately one
minute. The facilitator does not embellish.

---

### Slide 4 — Continuity, defined

**Title:** Continuity is an operating posture.

**On slide:** *"Continuity is what the institution has when the
right person is unavailable."*

**Body (verbal):** Continuity is not a backup plan. It is the
capacity of the institution to remain itself through transitions.

---

### Slide 5 — The OCI Method™

**Title:** Five phases.

**On slide:** Recognition → Mapping → Stabilization →
Infrastructure → Intelligence.

**Body (verbal):** A short pass through each phase. No slide per
phase. The detail belongs to
[OCI Method™](./oci-method.md).

---

### Slide 6 — The Pilot Framework

**Title:** Four phases of the pilot.

**On slide:** Recognition → Mapping → Stabilization →
Embedding.

**Body (verbal):** "A pilot is a slice of the Method. It opens
the door to embedding. The door is the institution's to walk
through, or not."

---

### Slide 7 — What we look at first

**Title:** Stewardship density.

**On slide:** *"How much of your institution is carried by how
few people?"*

**Body (verbal):** A plain description of the Stewardship
Density Index. No numbers from comparable institutions. No
benchmarks. The institution's own measure will speak for itself
when the time comes.

---

### Slide 8 — What we look at next

**Title:** Governance lineage.

**On slide:** *"How are your decisions remembered, and by whom?"*

**Body (verbal):** A plain description of governance entropy —
the slow erosion of why decisions were made, even when the
decisions themselves are recorded.

---

### Slide 9 — How we work

**Title:** The cadence.

**On slide:** *"Twelve weeks. One steward. One sponsor. One
facilitator."*

**Body (verbal):** A brief account of the
[OCI Delivery Model](./OCI_DELIVERY_MODEL.md). The room is told,
once, that this is fewer meetings and fewer people than they
expect.

---

### Slide 10 — What we do not do

**Title:** The boundary.

**On slide:** three short lines —
*"We do not measure people."*
*"We do not score institutions."*
*"We do not act on the institution's behalf."*

**Body (verbal):** A short, level account of the anti-
surveillance posture and the AI boundary. Reference
[OCI Anti-Surveillance Position](./OCI_ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_POSITION.md)
and [OCI AI Boundary](./OCI_AI_BOUNDARY.md) by name.

---

### Slide 11 — What the institution receives

**Title:** What the institution holds at the end.

**On slide:** four short lines —
*"An Executive Continuity Brief."*
*"A stewardship cartography."*
*"A Governance Entropy Workbook."*
*"A governance continuity plan, ratified by your governance body."*

**Body (verbal):** Each artifact named. No artifact previewed
visually. The artifacts are the institution's own; their
appearance is theirs to define.

---

### Slide 12 — Closing posture

**Title:** What we ask of the institution today.

**On slide:** *"Read the Brief. Take the time you need.
Decide."*

**Body (verbal):** "We do not ask for a decision now. We ask
that you read the Brief when it arrives, that you take the time
your institution needs, and that you let us know when you have
decided. Either decision is honourable."

---

## Slide design rules

These rules are constitutive of the briefing voice.

- **No more than three lines of body text per slide.** If more
  is needed, it belongs in the verbal narrative or the Brief.
- **No charts of comparable institutions.** Comparison is
  outside the doctrine.
- **No vendor logos.** The institution is not being sold to. It
  is being addressed.
- **No animation.** Stillness reads as seriousness.
- **No slide of the facilitator's biography.** The facilitator
  is introduced verbally, briefly.
- **No closing call-to-action.** The closing is the
  institution's reading time, not a decision moment.

---

## What is never on the deck

- No screenshots of the product. The institution will see the
  product when it is needed; the briefing is upstream of product.
- No customer logos.
- No metrics that imply benchmarking.
- No language reserved for marketing surfaces (productivity,
  optimisation, transformation).
- No pricing.

Pricing, where applicable, is a separate written conversation
held after the institution has decided to proceed.

---

## Materials handed to the room

The room receives:

- **Nothing during the briefing.** The facilitator opens with
  empty hands.
- **The Executive Continuity Brief, after the OCRA.** Delivered
  printed, bound, numbered, to the sponsor and the readers the
  sponsor names.
- **A one-page summary of the briefing slides**, on request, no
  more than seventy-two hours after the session, posted by
  arrangement.

The deck itself is not handed out.
