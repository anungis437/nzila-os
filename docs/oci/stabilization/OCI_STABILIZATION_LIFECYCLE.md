# OCI Stabilization Lifecycle

**Status:** Canonical doctrine for the five-phase delivery lifecycle of every Product 3 engagement.

**Audience:** Certified facilitators delivering Product 3, governance bodies receiving an engagement, internal stewards of the method.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Lifecycle defines the canonical sequence of phases that every Product 3 engagement runs through. It is the delivered form of the framework's Intervention Model, expressed as a phased sequence with explicit entry and exit conditions per phase.

The Lifecycle is not a project schedule. Phase duration is institutional; some institutions complete a phase in days, others in months. The Lifecycle governs sequence and gating, not duration.

---

## 2. The five phases

### 2.1 Phase I — Recognition

**Purpose:** Re-establish institutional recognition specifically for the stabilization engagement (separate from the Product 1 recognition the institution already holds).

**Entry condition:** Product 2 ratified; sponsor and governance body present.

**Activities:** recognition reconfirmation, sponsor interpretation refresh, voice-and-register alignment, engagement scope ratification.

**Exit condition:** A signed recognition statement specifying the stabilization engagement is on file with the institution.

**Dignity rule:** No exposure is named before the recognition statement is signed.

### 2.2 Phase II — Mapping

**Purpose:** Produce a stabilization-specific reading of the exposures the institution has accepted as in scope.

**Entry condition:** Phase I exit met.

**Activities:** structured discovery against the in-scope exposures, severity-band assignment per [OCI_STABILIZATION_SEVERITY_MODEL.md](OCI_STABILIZATION_SEVERITY_MODEL.md), readiness reverification per [OCI_STABILIZATION_READINESS.md](OCI_STABILIZATION_READINESS.md), Continuity Debt naming per [OCI_CONTINUITY_DEBT.md](OCI_CONTINUITY_DEBT.md).

**Exit condition:** A stabilization map document returned to the institution for ratification, including severity bands and named Continuity Debt categories.

**Defensiveness control:** The map is offered, not asserted. The institution may decline any item in the map without explanation. Declined items are recorded and removed from working scope.

### 2.3 Phase III — Stabilization

**Purpose:** Apply the reductive moves the institution has ratified as in scope.

**Entry condition:** Phase II map ratified; readiness reverified at the move level.

**Activities:** sequenced application of stabilization moves per the relevant playbooks; consent capture per move; reversibility profile documented per move; engagement log maintained.

**Exit condition:** A stabilization pathway document recording every applied move, every deferred move with reason, and the residual exposure landscape.

**Gradual-load discipline:** No phase III session introduces more than the institution can integrate before the next session. Pace is set by the institution, not by the facilitator.

**Shock-avoidance guarantee:** No phase III move is presented to a steward without prior individual consent and prior briefing on the move's reversibility profile.

### 2.4 Phase IV — Operationalization

**Purpose:** Embed the stabilization pathway in the institution's own governance cadence so that successor stewards can inherit it.

**Entry condition:** Phase III exit met; governance body available to receive embedding.

**Activities:** governance body briefing, integration of the pathway into the institution's governance calendar, successor-briefing package preparation, archival of artefacts in the institution's records.

**Exit condition:** A governance-receivable embedding package the institution can operate without facilitator presence.

**Dignity rule:** Embedding does not introduce new measurement of stewards. The pathway is embedded as institutional commitment, not as steward performance metric.

### 2.5 Phase V — Intelligence

**Purpose:** Establish the longitudinal intelligence loop so the institution can read its own continuity evolution over time (downstream Product 5 entry point).

**Entry condition:** Phase IV exit met; institution has accepted an Intelligence cadence.

**Activities:** longitudinal cadence ratification, downstream intelligence hand-off per [OCI_STABILIZATION_EVOLUTION.md](OCI_STABILIZATION_EVOLUTION.md), retainer terms ratified where applicable.

**Exit condition:** A signed Intelligence cadence document or a signed declination. Either is a successful Phase V exit.

**Dignity rule:** Intelligence is read at the institutional level. It is never read against named individuals.

## 3. Cross-phase commitments

### 3.1 Defensiveness controls

Every phase carries explicit defensiveness controls — provisions that prevent the engagement from becoming an instrument of pressure on stewards or governance bodies. The controls are:

- Voluntary continuation at every phase boundary,
- Steward-level consent revocability throughout,
- Institutional consent revocability throughout,
- Recognition statement renewal at every phase boundary,
- Facilitator decline of any move that loses readiness mid-flight.

### 3.2 Gradual-load discipline

Across the Lifecycle, the engagement loads the institution gradually. No phase introduces more new commitment than the previous phase has embedded. Where load is exceeded, the engagement pauses until prior load is integrated.

### 3.3 Shock-avoidance guarantee

The Lifecycle does not produce institutional shock. Phase boundaries are previewed in advance. New exposures discovered mid-phase are surfaced through the recognition register, not through escalation to leadership without prior steward briefing.

### 3.4 Severity-band trajectory

The Lifecycle tracks severity-band trajectory across phases. A severity band that worsens during the engagement is itself a finding and is surfaced to the institution.

---

## 4. Relation to the OCI Method™

The Lifecycle is the Product 3 expression of the OCI Method's five phases (see [OCI_METHOD.md](../OCI_METHOD.md) §2). Where the Method names the third phase "Stabilization", the Lifecycle expands it across Phases II–V to make the delivered shape explicit.

Where the Lifecycle and the Method diverge in language, the Method governs vocabulary and the Lifecycle governs delivery shape.
