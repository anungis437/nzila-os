# OCI Intervention Model

**Status:** Canonical doctrine for the intervention methodology of the OCI Stabilization Framework™.

**Audience:** Certified facilitators delivering Product 3, governance bodies receiving an engagement, internal stewards of the method.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Intervention Model defines how a stabilization engagement is run from open to close. It is the method-level shape of every Product 3 delivery, regardless of mode (Entry Stabilization, Enterprise Stabilization Programme, Stewardship Retainer).

The model is not a project plan. It is not a workplan template. It is the disciplined sequence of moves a facilitator is authorised to make inside an engagement, and the conditions under which each move may be made.

---

## 2. Engagement structure

Every engagement has four structural components:

1. **Opening** — recognition reconfirmation, scope ratification, readiness verification, consent capture.
2. **Working phase** — the sequenced application of stabilization moves under the Lifecycle (see [OCI_STABILIZATION_LIFECYCLE.md](OCI_STABILIZATION_LIFECYCLE.md)).
3. **Embedding** — handover to the institution's own governance cadence, archival of artefacts in the institution's records, severance of facilitator operational dependency.
4. **Close** — closure note, recognition statement renewal or release, optional retainer continuation.

No engagement skips any structural component. An engagement that cannot complete any component is closed at the last completed component with an explicit closure note.

---

## 3. Sequencing rules

The model holds five sequencing rules, enforced across all engagements:

1. **Recognition before intervention.** No stabilization move is offered before the recognition statement of Phase 1 is reconfirmed and accepted.
2. **Mapping before action.** No action is taken on an exposure that has not been mapped under Product 2 or under the engagement's own Mapping phase.
3. **Reduction before addition.** Stabilization moves that reduce institutional fragility are offered before moves that add governance structure.
4. **Reversible before irreversible.** Reversible moves are sequenced before irreversible moves. Irreversible moves require explicit, separately captured governance-body ratification.
5. **Severity-band ordered.** Within a phase, moves are sequenced by severity band (see [OCI_STABILIZATION_SEVERITY_MODEL.md](OCI_STABILIZATION_SEVERITY_MODEL.md)) descending, then by reversibility ascending.

A facilitator who departs from these sequencing rules must record the departure in the engagement log with a stated reason. Repeated departures are a recertification matter.

---

## 4. Facilitator role

The facilitator is the named, certified practitioner accountable for the engagement under the OCI Method™. The facilitator's role inside the model is:

- To hold the recognition relationship,
- To run the sequenced moves of the Lifecycle,
- To name and decline scope outside the framework,
- To capture consent, reversibility, and ratification points in the engagement log,
- To return the engagement to the institution at close without operational dependency.

The facilitator is not a consultant. The facilitator does not direct institutional decisions. The facilitator does not act on behalf of the institution's governance body. The facilitator does not retain operational standing once the engagement closes.

---

## 5. Reversibility commitments

Every stabilization move offered under the model carries an explicit reversibility profile:

- **Fully reversible** — the institution may decline or reverse the move at any point before ratification with no residual institutional change.
- **Reversible with cost** — reversal is possible but incurs documented institutional cost (e.g., re-briefing successor stewards). Cost is named at the time the move is offered.
- **Irreversible** — the move cannot be reversed once ratified. Irreversible moves require separately captured governance-body ratification and explicit acknowledgement of the irreversibility profile.

A move offered without a stated reversibility profile is not a valid Product 3 move.

---

## 6. Deferral discipline

The model treats deferral as a first-class outcome. A move may be deferred when:

- The Readiness Thresholds for the move are not yet met,
- The institution declines the move,
- A steward affected by the move declines participation,
- Reversibility cannot be assured to the standard of section 5,
- The facilitator's certification scope does not cover the move.

Deferred moves are recorded with the deferral reason and the conditions under which they may be reopened. Deferral is not a failure. The framework holds explicitly that an engagement closing with significant deferred moves is a more successful application of the framework than an engagement that forced unready moves through.

See also [OCI_INTERVENTION_ETHICS.md](OCI_INTERVENTION_ETHICS.md) §4 on the ethics of deferral.

---

## 7. Engagement log

Every engagement maintains an engagement log. The log records, at minimum:

- The recognition statement and its reconfirmation date,
- The ratified scope,
- Every move offered, with severity band, reversibility profile, and consent capture,
- Every deferral, with reason,
- Every ratification, with the ratifying body and date,
- The closure note.

The engagement log is the institution's record. The facilitator holds a working copy during the engagement and transfers the canonical copy to the institution at close. Severance of any retained facilitator copy is governed by [OCI_DATA_HANDLING.md](../OCI_DATA_HANDLING.md).

---

## 8. Boundaries

The Intervention Model does not authorise:

- Acting on a steward's standing without that steward's direct consent,
- Capturing personal performance data,
- Running behavioural inferences,
- Operating after consent withdrawal,
- Continuing a move that has lost its readiness conditions mid-flight.

A facilitator who is asked to perform any of the above declines the request, names the boundary, and records the decline in the engagement log.
