# Continuity Debt™

**Status:** Canonical OCI executive vocabulary. Signature term reserved under trademark discipline.

**Audience:** Certified facilitators, governance bodies, executive sponsors, internal stewards of the method.

---

## 1. Purpose

Continuity Debt™ is the canonical OCI vocabulary for the accrued institutional fragility an institution carries when continuity work is deferred. The term is signature OCI executive vocabulary and is reserved as a trademark-discipline term. Its use outside the framework is governed by the OCI naming convention.

The term exists to give governance bodies and executive sponsors a single, governance-receivable word for what they already sense: the slow accumulation of institutional fragility that does not show up on any conventional ledger but that institutions pay for at moments of steward departure, governance transition, or modernization.

---

## 2. Definition

Continuity Debt™ is the cumulative institutional fragility that accrues when continuity-bearing work is deferred, undocumented, or concentrated in a single steward without successor preparation, lineage capture, or written practice.

Continuity Debt is read at the institutional level. It is not a per-person debt. A steward who carries undocumented practice is not in debt; the institution carrying that practice without succession or lineage capture is in debt.

Continuity Debt is paid down through stabilization. The Stabilization Framework™ exists as the canonical method for retiring Continuity Debt without displacing the stewards who carry it.

---

## 3. The five debt categories

Continuity Debt accrues in five named categories. Every Product 3 engagement reads the institution across these five categories.

### 3.1 Undocumented governance

Governance commitments — interpretations, precedents, policies-in-practice — that are operationally honoured but not documented in a form a successor governance body could inherit.

Accrual mechanism: governance bodies make interpretations that become institutional practice without being recorded in a governance-receivable form. Each unrecorded interpretation accrues debt against the next governance transition.

Surfaced in workbook readings via `governanceInterpretationMatrix` and `continuityLineageEngine`.

### 3.2 Reconstruction burden accumulation

The cost the institution would bear to reconstruct lapsed practice or lapsed interpretation. Distinct from undocumented governance because reconstruction burden measures the cost of recovery, not the absence of documentation per se.

Accrual mechanism: precedents fade from active practice; the cost to reconstruct them rises with time since last active reference.

Surfaced via `reconstructionBurdenAnalyzer` and `precedentContinuityMapper`.

### 3.3 Onboarding fragility

The degree to which a successor steward would be unable to absorb the load a current steward carries, given existing written practice, lineage capture, and shadowing arrangements.

Accrual mechanism: roles are held by stewards whose practice is not transferable without their direct presence. Each year of steward tenure without onboarding capture accrues debt against the eventual transition.

Surfaced via `onboardingFragilityAnalysis`.

### 3.4 Stewardship overload

The degree to which institutional load is concentrated on a small number of stewards beyond the institution's distributed carrying capacity.

Accrual mechanism: institutions add load to known-capable stewards faster than they distribute carrying capacity. Each load increment without redistribution accrues debt against the eventual loss of any one overloaded steward.

Surfaced via `stewardshipCartography`, `continuityDependencyGraph`, and `continuityRedistributionPlanner`.

### 3.5 Modernization without preservation

The degree to which planned or in-flight modernization moves do not preserve the institutional practice they are replacing.

Accrual mechanism: modernization initiatives substitute new tooling or new process without capturing the institutional interpretation embedded in the old. Each unpreserved substitution accrues debt against the post-modernization continuity surface.

Surfaced via `continuitySafeModernization`, `modernizationAlignmentEngine`, and `governanceModernizationReview`.

---

## 4. How debt accrues

Continuity Debt accrues through inaction, not through action. The institution accrues debt when:

- A steward holds undocumented practice and time passes without lineage capture,
- A governance body makes an interpretation and time passes without that interpretation being recorded in a governance-receivable form,
- A modernization moves through without preservation of the practice it replaced,
- A successor candidate is named without a shadowing arrangement being set,
- A precedent fades from active practice without being reconstructed in writing.

Accrual is institutional. No steward causes accrual; the institution accrues by deferring the moves that would have paid down debt.

---

## 5. How debt is paid down

Continuity Debt is paid down through stabilization moves under Product 3. The retirement mechanisms per category are:

| Category | Retirement mechanism |
|----------|---------------------|
| Undocumented governance | Governance lineage capture; interpretation matrix codification; ratified governance-receivable artefact. |
| Reconstruction burden | Precedent reconstruction; lineage briefing of successor stewards. |
| Onboarding fragility | Written practice capture; shadowing arrangement; onboarding playbook ratified per role. |
| Stewardship overload | Carrier redistribution; broadened practice; recognition of additional carriers with reciprocity terms. |
| Modernization without preservation | Continuity-safe modernization review; pre-substitution practice capture; post-substitution continuity confirmation. |

Each retirement mechanism is a stabilization move with a documented reversibility profile, severity band, and consent capture.

---

## 6. Reading Continuity Debt in workbook outputs

Workbook outputs surface Continuity Debt through the per-dimension severity bands of the Severity Model (see [OCI_STABILIZATION_SEVERITY_MODEL.md](OCI_STABILIZATION_SEVERITY_MODEL.md)). A higher band along a debt-relevant dimension indicates a higher accrued debt in that category.

The workbook does not produce a single composite Continuity Debt figure. A composite would obscure the structural reading the framework holds as canonical. Governance bodies reading the workbook see five debt-category readings, each with its own band and its own retirement candidate moves.

---

## 7. Trademark discipline

Continuity Debt™ is signature OCI executive vocabulary. The term appears in:

- Product 3 engagement materials,
- Executive briefing decks,
- Governance body briefings,
- The Diagnostic & Stabilization Program™ commercial documentation.

The term does not appear in:

- Marketing copy that would dilute its institutional register (e.g., advertising claim copy outside an OCI delivery context),
- Material that uses the term as a metaphor for unrelated organisational issues,
- Third-party material without OCI naming-convention authorisation.

A facilitator or partner using the term outside this discipline is referred to the OCI naming-convention review process.
