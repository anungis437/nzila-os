# OCI Onboarding Stabilization

**Status:** Canonical doctrine. Governs how a Product 3 engagement opens — from first conversation through Readiness reading, scoping, agreement, and the opening seven days of facilitated work. Binding for every certified facilitator.

**Audience:** Certified facilitators, executive sponsors entering a Product 3 engagement, governance bodies receiving an engagement proposal, internal stewards reviewing facilitator practice.

---

## 1. Purpose

OCI Onboarding Stabilization defines the disciplined opening of an engagement so the institution enters the work recognised, not assessed; informed, not sold to; and protected by Readiness thresholds before any stabilization move is taken.

Onboarding is itself a stabilization phase. It is not pre-work. It is the first surface of the framework the institution experiences, and it sets the tone register, the consent posture, and the receivability of every subsequent move.

---

## 2. Audience and entry surfaces

A Product 3 engagement may open from three surfaces:

1. **Continuation from Product 2** — the institution has completed a Governance Entropy Workbook™ engagement and the workbook surfaces continuity debt warranting stabilization.
2. **Direct request** — an institution approaches the framework asking for stabilization without a prior workbook. This path requires a workbook before any stabilization move can be made.
3. **Referral from peer institution or governance body** — a third party identifies a candidate institution. Consent of the institution's executive sponsor and at least one governance-body representative is required before any framework contact.

In every case, onboarding begins with recognition of the institution as it already exists, not with a problem statement.

---

## 3. The onboarding sequence

The sequence is fixed. A facilitator who reorders, compresses, or skips a stage is no longer practising the framework and is referred to recertification review.

### 3.1 Stage one — Recognition conversation

A single conversation (typically 60–90 minutes) in which the facilitator listens to how the institution describes itself, names what is already being carried, and confirms whether the institution wishes to proceed to a Readiness reading. No assessment is performed. No proposal is offered. No commitment is requested.

### 3.2 Stage two — Workbook prerequisite verification

If the institution has not completed a Governance Entropy Workbook™ in the prior 18 months, a workbook is conducted (Product 2) before any Product 3 conversation continues. The workbook is the empirical surface from which Readiness can be honestly read.

### 3.3 Stage three — Readiness reading

The facilitator reads the institution against the five Readiness conditions documented in `OCI_STABILIZATION_READINESS.md`. The reading is shared in full with the institution. If any condition is below sufficient band, the engagement is deferred and the institution is offered the appropriate alternative continuation.

### 3.4 Stage four — Scoping conversation

Conducted only if Readiness is sufficient across all five conditions. The facilitator and the institution discuss which playbooks (see `playbooks/README.md`) the institution recognises itself in, and which stabilization moves are most receivable in the institution's present state. The scope is bounded by what the institution can carry without exhausting its stewards.

### 3.5 Stage five — Engagement agreement

A written agreement that names: the playbooks in scope, the stabilization moves committed to, the Readiness thresholds that gate continuation, the recertification triggers that gate facilitator continuation, the data-handling posture (see `OCI_DATA_HANDLING.md` referenced in `OCI_METHOD.md`), and the institution's right to pause or end the engagement at any point without penalty or interpretation.

### 3.6 Stage six — The opening seven days

The first seven days of facilitated work are reserved for recognition surfaces only. No diagnostic claims. No move recommendations. No tracking instrument is introduced. The opening seven days exist so the institution experiences the framework's tone register before any stabilization move is offered.

---

## 4. Tone discipline during onboarding

Onboarding tone is recognition-first. The facilitator does not name pathologies, does not score, does not benchmark the institution against peers, and does not offer transformation language. The institution is named as it already understands itself, and the framework's vocabulary is introduced only when the institution asks for it.

Forbidden tone during onboarding includes: urgency framing, scarcity framing, comparison to other institutions, framing of the institution's current state as failure, and any language that positions the framework as rescuing the institution.

---

## 5. Consent posture

Consent is read continuously, not signed once. The institution may pause or end the engagement at any stage without justification and without consequence to its standing with the framework. Consent to enter Stage Three is not consent to enter Stage Five. Each stage carries its own consent surface.

If the executive sponsor or governance-body representative changes during onboarding, consent is re-read from Stage One. The engagement does not proceed on inherited consent.

---

## 6. Honest non-engagement during onboarding

If at any stage the facilitator reads that the institution would not be served by continuing — even if Readiness is technically sufficient — the facilitator offers honest non-engagement. The framework treats this as a successful onboarding outcome. The institution is offered a Product 1 recognition continuation or an invitation to return when conditions have shifted.

---

## 7. Onboarding closure

Onboarding closes when one of three dispositions is recorded:

- **Proceed** — Readiness sufficient, scope agreed, engagement opens with the seven-day recognition window.
- **Deferred** — Readiness insufficient on one or more conditions; alternative continuation offered.
- **Withdrawn** — Institution or facilitator records honest non-engagement.

In all three cases the institution receives a written closure note naming the disposition, the conditions read, and the continuation offered. The closure note is the institution's record of its onboarding, not the framework's record of the institution.
