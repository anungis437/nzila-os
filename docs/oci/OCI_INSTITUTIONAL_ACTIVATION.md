# OCI Institutional Activation

**ARTIFACT TYPE:** Institutional Doctrine
**DOCTRINE_VERSION:** 1.0.0
**STATUS:** Canonical
**PARENT FRAMEWORK:** [OCI Pilot Framework™](./OCI_PILOT_FRAMEWORK.md)

> Activation is the moment an institution decides to host an OCI pilot.
> It is not a launch event. It is a quiet, deliberate alignment of
> sponsor, governance, and operational stewardship before the
> Recognition phase begins.

---

## What this document is

This document names the conditions, decisions, and minimum
institutional posture required before an OCI pilot can begin in good
order. It is intended for two readers:

- the executive sponsor who is preparing to host the pilot, and
- the facilitator who is preparing to enter the institution.

If activation is not done well, Recognition begins with the wrong
people in the room, with unstated expectations, and the pilot's
protective frame is compromised before it begins.

---

## Activation, defined

Activation is the bounded period — typically two to four weeks before
the Recognition phase opens — during which:

- the executive sponsor confirms institutional sponsorship in writing,
- the governance liaison is named,
- the continuity steward is identified and informed,
- the engagement's scope, cadence, and artifacts are agreed in a
  written scope document,
- the institution names the conditions under which it will pause or
  end the engagement.

Activation closes when these conditions are satisfied. The
Recognition phase opens immediately afterward.

---

## Pre-activation readiness signals

An institution is ready to consider activation when:

- a continuity question has surfaced inside the institution, named or
  unnamed (a recent senior departure, a board succession, a regulator
  inquiry, a stewardship near-miss);
- an executive leader carries the question seriously enough to host
  the engagement personally;
- the institution has the capacity to release approximately four to
  six hours per week of the steward's time over twelve weeks;
- the institution accepts that the engagement will not produce
  immediate operational change, and is comfortable with that pacing.

An institution is **not** ready when:

- the engagement is being sought to satisfy a deadline (regulatory
  filing, board reporting cycle, funder review);
- no executive sponsor is named;
- the steward identified is unable or unwilling to participate;
- the institution expects a vendor relationship, not a facilitation
  relationship.

A facilitator declining to begin an engagement on these grounds is
honouring the doctrine, not refusing the work.

---

## The activation conversation

The activation conversation is a single 90-minute working session,
held in person where possible, with the executive sponsor, the named
governance liaison, the continuity steward, and the facilitator. It
is a working session, not a presentation.

The conversation covers:

1. **The continuity question.** What has the institution noticed?
   What is unresolved? The sponsor speaks first; the facilitator
   listens.
2. **The institutional posture.** Is this engagement to be visible
   inside the institution, or held quietly? The answer is the
   institution's to make.
3. **Scope and cadence.** The default scope is the four phases of the
   Pilot Framework over twelve weeks. Variations are noted.
4. **Artifact handover.** Who receives the Executive Continuity
   Brief? Who reads the Governance Entropy Workbook? What stays
   inside the institution and what may be referenced in anonymised
   sector intelligence (with consent, never by default)?
5. **The off-ramp.** Under what conditions does the institution pause
   or end the engagement? The answer is written down.

The conversation closes with a draft scope document the sponsor
signs within seven days. Until the scope is signed, the engagement
has not begun.

---

## Activation artifacts

Activation produces three small artifacts:

- **Sponsorship letter.** A one-page letter, signed by the executive
  sponsor, naming the engagement, the sponsor, the steward, the
  governance liaison, and the conditions under which the engagement
  may pause or close. Filed with the institution's records, not
  publicised.
- **Scope document.** The
  [OCI Pilot Scope Template](./OCI_PILOT_SCOPE_TEMPLATE.md), completed
  and ratified by sponsor and facilitator.
- **Governance notification.** A short note to the governance body
  (board, council, executive committee) informing them that a
  continuity engagement is beginning, naming the sponsor, and naming
  the cadence at which the body will receive the eventual governance
  continuity plan.

No public announcement is made. No press release is drafted. No
website is updated. The institution may, after Phase 3, choose to
reference the engagement in its annual report or governance
communications. That decision belongs to the institution and is
made later, not now.

---

## Activation roles, restated

| Role | Activation responsibility |
|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | Signs sponsorship letter; chairs activation conversation |
| Continuity steward | Confirms participation; clears calendar at the agreed cadence |
| Governance liaison | Drafts governance notification; carries it to the body |
| Facilitator | Drafts scope document; declines engagement if readiness is absent |
| Institutional witness | Not present at activation; introduced during Mapping |

---

## What activation is not

- **Not a kickoff.** No event. No ceremony. No deck.
- **Not a discovery sprint.** Discovery happens during Recognition,
  with proper instruments. Activation only confirms the conditions
  under which Recognition will be entered.
- **Not a procurement step.** Procurement, if any, is handled
  separately under the institution's own purchasing rules. Activation
  presumes procurement is either resolved or unnecessary.
- **Not reversible without notice.** Once the scope is signed, the
  facilitator and the institution have both committed. Withdrawing
  requires a written note from the sponsor and a brief closing
  conversation. The engagement leaves no residue.

---

## Activation conduct rules

The activation conversation operates under the same conduct rules
that govern the pilot itself. In particular:

- **No diagnosis.** The facilitator does not characterise the
  institution's continuity posture during activation. Recognition
  has not yet begun.
- **No selling.** The facilitator does not advocate for embedding,
  for additional scope, or for sector intelligence participation.
  Those questions belong to Phase 4 (Embedding), not now.
- **No urgency.** The activation conversation is unhurried. If the
  conversation is rushed, activation has not happened; only
  scheduling has.
