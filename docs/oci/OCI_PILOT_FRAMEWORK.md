> This document defines the institutional pilot. It is not a marketing
> framework. It is the canonical methodology under which an institution
> moves from first contact to embedded continuity infrastructure.

---

## What this document is

The Pilot Framework names the four phases of an OCI institutional pilot
and binds each phase to its instruments, its institutional posture, and
its exit criteria.

Where the [OCI Method™](./OCI_METHOD.md) names the long arc of an
institution's continuity maturity (five phases, including the steady-state
phases of Infrastructure and Intelligence), the Pilot Framework names the
delivery arc — the bounded engagement during which an institution and
its facilitators recognise, map, and stabilise their continuity, and
during which the conditions for embedding are prepared.

> The Pilot Framework is a slice of the OCI Method™.
> Pilot Phase 4 (Embedding) opens the door to Method Phases 4 and 5
> (Infrastructure and Intelligence), which the institution operates from
> then on. Kill criteria and the labour information boundary are defined once,
> in [`OCI_METHOD.md` §6.3–6.4](./OCI_METHOD.md#63-information-boundary-labour-engagements),
> and apply to every phase below without restatement.

## The four pilot phases

### Phase 1 — Recognition

**What happens.** The institution sees the shape of its continuity for
the first time. The conversation moves from anecdote ("we lost a lot
when Marie retired") to articulation ("our continuity sits in a small
number of carriers without identified successors, and the institution
has never named this").

**Instruments.**

- [OCI Continuity Risk Assessment (OCRA)](../../apps/union-eyes/lib/icra/) —
  the entry instrument; produces an Executive Continuity Brief.
- Executive interpretation session — a facilitator walks the institution
  through the Brief without diagnosis.

**Posture.** Calm. Observational. Recognition before diagnosis.
The facilitator does not tell the institution what is wrong. The
facilitator helps the institution see what is already true.

**Exit criteria.**

- The executive sponsor can articulate the institution's continuity
  shape in plain language.
- The institution has named at least one continuity dependency it had
  not previously articulated.
- The Executive Continuity Brief has been read by the sponsor and at
  least one other institutional leader.
- The institution has chosen, without pressure, to enter Phase 2.

**Typical duration.** Two to four weeks from intake to executive
interpretation.

---

### Phase 2 — Mapping

**What happens.** The institution maps its continuity terrain. It
identifies its institutional memory holders, the responsibilities they
carry, the lineage of its governance, and the breakpoints where loss
would be most consequential.

**Instruments.**

- [Governance Entropy Workbook™](../../apps/union-eyes/lib/workbook/) —
  the canonical mapping instrument.
- Stewardship Density Index™ — the deterministic concentration measure.
- Continuity lineage exploration — facilitated session with stewards.
- Governance archaeology — review of decisions whose rationale has been
  partially lost.

**Posture.** Fieldwork. Editorial. Dignified.
The mapping is not an audit. It is a record. The people whose labour
appears in the map are recognised, not measured.

**Exit criteria.**

- The Stewardship Density Index has been computed and read by the
  sponsor.
- At least one institution-critical role without an identified
  successor has been named.
- The workbook's Memory Holders module is complete.
- The institution can describe, in its own words, where its continuity
  is thin.

**Typical duration.** Three to six weeks of working sessions, paced to
the institution's rhythm.

---

### Phase 3 — Stabilization

**What happens.** The institution reduces its continuity burden where
that burden is most fragile. Stewardship is redistributed where
appropriate. Lineage is recorded where it was carried orally. The work
is operational, not symbolic.

**Instruments.**

- Continuity breakpoint review — facilitated working session.
- Operational survivability priorities — a written, time-bounded list
  of stabilisation moves.
- Stewardship redistribution agreements — documented, sponsor-approved.
- Governance continuity plan — the institution's own document, not the
  facilitator's.

**Posture.** Facilitative. Collegial. Reductive.
Stabilisation does not add work. It removes fragility. Where a steward
has been quietly carrying institution-critical knowledge, the goal is
relief, not transfer of blame.

**Exit criteria.**

- At least one institution-critical responsibility has either gained an
  identified successor or has been documented to a standard that
  reduces its single-point-of-failure exposure.
- The governance continuity plan has been ratified by the institution's
  governance body (board, council, executive committee).
- The institution has decided whether to embed OCI infrastructure
  (Phase 4) or to operate the plan without ongoing platform support.

**Typical duration.** Four to eight weeks of stabilisation work,
overlapping with Phase 2.

---

### Phase 4 — Embedding

**What happens.** Continuity becomes embedded in the institution's
operational systems. The work done in Phases 1–3 is no longer a project;
it is the platform. This phase opens onto the long-arc steady state
defined in [OCI Method™](./OCI_METHOD.md) as Infrastructure (Method
Phase 4) and Intelligence (Method Phase 5).

**Instruments.**

- Runtime continuity systems (audit-grade records, decision lineage,
  stewardship density tracking over time).
- Governance continuity infrastructure (board-level continuity
  reporting, succession reviews on a documented cadence).
- Longitudinal continuity intelligence (the institution's own data,
  used by the institution; sector-level anonymised intelligence
  available to participating institutions on consent).

**Posture.** Embedded. Structural. Durable.
At this stage, OCI is no longer something the institution is doing. It
is something the institution operates from.

**Exit criteria.**

- The pilot is over.
- The institution either continues as an embedded-OCI institution
  (Method Phases 4–5) or returns to standalone operation with its
  stabilisation plan in hand.
- Either outcome is honourable. No institution is pressured to embed.

**Typical duration.** Embedding decisions are made within the pilot
arc; the embedding itself extends beyond the pilot.

## Cross-walk to the OCI Method™

| Pilot Phase | OCI Method™ Phase |
|---|---|
| 1 — Recognition | 1 — Recognition |
| 2 — Mapping | 2 — Mapping |
| 3 — Stabilization | 3 — Stabilization |
| 4 — Embedding | 4 — Infrastructure + 5 — Intelligence |

The Pilot Framework collapses Method Phases 4 and 5 into a single
delivery phase (Embedding) for readability. The substantive doctrine
is unchanged: an embedded institution operates both Infrastructure
and Intelligence over time, but the pilot only opens the door.

---

## What the Pilot Framework is not

- It is not a consulting engagement methodology. The facilitator is not
  a consultant in the conventional sense; they are an institutional
  fieldworker.
- It is not a transformation programme. There is no transformation
  promised, planned, or measured.
- It is not a software deployment plan. Software is downstream of
  doctrine.
- It is not a maturity model. Institutions are not graded.

---

## Voice and conduct rules

The Pilot Framework operates under the doctrine's standing rules. In
particular:

- **Recognition before diagnosis.** No phase opens with a finding. Each
  phase opens with a recognition.
- **Non-blaming language.** Institutional fragility is described, never
  attributed to individuals.
- **Stewardship validation.** The labour that carries the institution
  is named and honoured, never measured for productivity.
- **Calm pacing.** The pilot moves at the institution's rhythm, not at
  the facilitator's calendar.

These are not stylistic preferences. They are conditions of the work.
