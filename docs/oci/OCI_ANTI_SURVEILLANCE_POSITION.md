# OCI Anti-Surveillance Position

**ARTIFACT TYPE:** Institutional Doctrine — Conduct Boundary
**DOCTRINE_VERSION:** 1.0.0
**STATUS:** Canonical
**PARENT DOCTRINE:** [docs/doctrine/ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_DOCTRINE.md](../doctrine/ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_DOCTRINE.md)
**RELATED:** [OCI Privacy Position](./OCI_PRIVACY_POSITION.md), [OCI AI Boundary](./OCI_AI_BOUNDARY.md)

> This document restates, for institutional reviewers, the anti-
> surveillance posture under which OCI operates. The parent
> doctrine is binding on the entire Nzila product family; this
> document specifies how the posture applies to the OCI work.

---

## The position, stated plainly

OCI exists to help an institution recognise and preserve the
continuity it already carries. It does not exist to observe,
measure, or characterise the people who carry it.

Surveillance — in any of its operational forms — is incompatible
with the work. This is not a configuration. It is a constitutive
limit.

---

## What this rules out

The following classes of feature are out of scope, by doctrine,
and are not on any roadmap:

### Worker observation

- attention tracking,
- focus measures,
- activity timing,
- communication metadata aggregation,
- presence inference.

### Behavioural inference

- behavioural analytics,
- engagement scoring,
- predictive conduct modelling,
- inferred psychological traits,
- relationship-graph inference from communication metadata.

### Productivity measurement

- per-individual productivity scoring,
- per-team productivity comparison,
- workforce productivity dashboards,
- "operator efficiency" measures,
- comparative leaderboards of any kind.

### Concealed observation

- observation without disclosure to the observed,
- observation of institutional members for purposes other than
  those they have consented to under the institution's privacy
  regime,
- observation of facilitation participants for purposes outside
  the agreed engagement scope.

The list is not exhaustive. The principle is: OCI does not look
at people for purposes of measurement or prediction.

---

## What this preserves

The exclusions above protect three institutional goods that the
doctrine considers prior to product:

1. **Stewardship dignity.** The people whose labour carries the
   institution are recognised, not measured. The Memory Holders
   map names a steward; it does not score them.
2. **Governance trust.** Members of an institution must be able
   to trust that their continuity infrastructure is not also a
   monitoring infrastructure. Without that trust, the
   institution cannot speak frankly about its own continuity.
3. **Doctrinal coherence.** Continuity intelligence cannot live
   inside a surveillance posture. The two postures contradict
   each other; embedding both would compromise the work.

---

## How this binds the work

The anti-surveillance position binds every layer of OCI delivery:

### Bound in product

- The Stewardship Density Index is institutional, not personal.
- The Memory Holders map names roles and responsibilities; it
  does not score the people who hold them.
- The Governance Entropy Workbook records governance lineage,
  not behavioural patterns.
- Telemetry is operational, not behavioural (see
  [OCI Data Handling](./OCI_DATA_HANDLING.md)).

### Bound in conduct

- Facilitators do not characterise individuals. The recognition
  posture is institutional throughout.
- Operators do not query institutional data outside the agreed
  scope.
- Engineers do not propose features that would relax this
  boundary; if proposed, such features are rejected at design
  review.

### Bound in disclosure

- Procurement disclosure (this document and its companions)
  states the position before any contract is signed.
- The institution receives the anti-surveillance position in
  writing as part of activation, not after.

### Bound in contract

- The standard contract names the anti-surveillance position as
  a binding undertaking, not as a marketing claim. Material
  departure from the position is grounds for institutional
  termination without penalty.

## Instrument disposition

The method carries a small set of analytical instruments (named in
[`OCI_METHOD.md`](./OCI_METHOD.md) and [`oci-product-ladder.md`](./oci-product-ladder.md)). Each is
reviewed here against the anti-surveillance position. None report a number that stands alone;
every reading is paired with the institutional context that justifies it, and none name individuals.

| Instrument | Disposition | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stewardship Density Index | **Keep** | Institutional concentration reading; reports a band, not a person |
| Governance Entropy Scale (1–5) | **Keep, renamed as ordinal, not a score** | An ordinal drift reading paired with the precedent register that produced it — never presented as a grade |
| Continuity Burden Map | **Keep** | Institutional topology; rendered as bands and zones, never a heat map of named people |
| Continuity Survivability Matrix | **Keep** | Reconstruction-burden reading under breakpoint scenarios; institutional, not personal |
| Reconstruction Burden Index | **Keep** | Institutional reconstruction-cost estimate; speaks to the institution's load, not any individual's |
| OCI Operational Profile | **Keep, facilitated-engagements only** | Cross-instrument synthesis; requires the breadth of input only a facilitated session produces |
| P5 Intelligence Network / sector baselines | **Keep, opt-in, non-ranked, not sold** | Permitted only with k≥5 contributing institutions per published band, and only once two completed paid engagements exist and a second certified facilitator is active (see [`OCI_METHOD.md` §6.5](./OCI_METHOD.md#65-p5-intelligence-network-gate)) |

No instrument in this list produces a ranked institutional comparison. If a future instrument
would, it is rejected at design review under this position, not shipped and relabelled later.

## What happens at the edge

A pilot occasionally encounters a moment where an institutional
question shades toward observation of an individual — for example,
when a steward's decisions are under post-hoc review by the
governance body, or when a continuity transition involves a
contested departure.

The doctrine in these moments:

- **The institution's own processes lead.** OCI does not become
  the venue for personal review. The institution conducts that
  review under its own rules, with its own counsel, in its own
  records.
- **The facilitator declines characterisation.** If asked to
  characterise an individual's conduct from OCI records, the
  facilitator declines. The records are not built for that
  purpose and would be misread if used that way.
- **The records remain institutional.** OCI records are
  available to the institution's authorised reviewers under the
  institution's privacy regime. They are not delivered to third
  parties to support personal-conduct conclusions.

These moments are rare and are handled in writing, not in
working sessions.

## How a member of the institution can verify the position

A member, employee, or steward who wishes to verify that the
anti-surveillance position is being honoured may:

- read this document and its parent
  [Anti-Surveillance Doctrine](../doctrine/ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_DOCTRINE.md),
- request, through the governance liaison, a written description
  of the OCI records held about the named role,
- request, through the governance liaison, a written description
  of the AI-assisted operations performed on those records (see
  [OCI AI Boundary](./OCI_AI_BOUNDARY.md)),
- raise a concern with the institution's privacy officer; the
  facilitator and operator are required to cooperate fully with
  the institution's privacy review.

The verification path is institutional, not adversarial. The
posture is designed to be inspectable.
