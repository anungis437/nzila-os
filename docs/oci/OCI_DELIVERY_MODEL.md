# The OCI Delivery Model

**ARTIFACT TYPE:** Institutional Doctrine
**DOCTRINE_VERSION:** 1.0.0
**STATUS:** Canonical
**PARENT FRAMEWORK:** [OCI Pilot Framework™](./OCI_PILOT_FRAMEWORK.md)

> This document defines who is in the room, when they are in the room,
> what they hold, and what passes between them. It is the choreography
> of an OCI engagement.

---

## What this document is

The Delivery Model names the institutional roles, the cadence of an
OCI engagement, the artifact handover sequence, and the default
duration of a pilot. It exists so that an institution preparing to host
an OCI pilot, and a facilitator preparing to deliver one, are working
from the same understanding before either commits.

The model is deliberately small. OCI engagements involve fewer people,
fewer meetings, and fewer artifacts than comparable institutional work.
Compression is the goal.

---

## Roles

OCI engagements name five institutional roles. None of these are
created for the engagement; all of them already exist inside the
institution, even when they are not labelled.

### Executive sponsor

The institutional leader who carries the continuity question on behalf
of the institution. Typically a CEO, executive director, secretary-
general, general manager, or president. The sponsor's role is to:

- read the Executive Continuity Brief and decide whether the
  institution proceeds beyond Recognition,
- ratify the governance continuity plan at Phase 3 close,
- protect the engagement from premature operationalisation.

The sponsor does not facilitate. The sponsor authorises.

### Continuity steward

The person inside the institution who carries the most operational
memory. Often the operations director, chief of staff, executive
assistant of long tenure, or a programme director who has been with
the institution through multiple leadership transitions.

The steward is the institution's primary fieldwork partner. They are
recognised by the engagement, never measured by it.

### Governance liaison

The person responsible for the engagement's interface with the
institution's governance body (board, council, executive committee).
Often the board secretary, governance counsel, or chief of governance.
Their role is to:

- carry the institution's governance language into the engagement,
- carry the engagement's continuity language into the governance body,
- ensure the governance continuity plan is ratified by the correct
  body, in the correct form, at the correct cadence.

### Facilitator

The OCI-trained fieldworker who carries the methodology. The
facilitator is not a consultant. They do not produce findings. They
help the institution articulate what is already true and choose what
to stabilise.

A facilitator may be internal to the institution (after training) or
external. The methodology is the same.

### Institutional witness

One or two additional institutional voices invited to specific
sessions. The witness is not a decision-maker. The witness is a
recognised observer whose presence helps the institution hold itself
accountable to what it has named.

The witness is invited, never embedded. Their presence is consensual
and bounded.

## Cadence

The default cadence of an OCI pilot is twelve weeks. The cadence is
not a deadline. Institutions move at their own rhythm. Twelve weeks is
the median; an unhurried pilot may take sixteen.

| Week | Phase | Session | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recognition | Sponsor intake | 60 min |
| 2 | Recognition | OCRA delivery | self-paced |
| 3 | Recognition | Executive interpretation | 90 min |
| 4 | Mapping | Workbook orientation | 60 min |
| 5–7 | Mapping | Working sessions (steward + facilitator) | 90 min each |
| 8 | Mapping | Stewardship density review | 90 min |
| 9 | Stabilization | Continuity breakpoint review | 120 min |
| 10 | Stabilization | Operational survivability working session | 120 min |
| 11 | Stabilization | Governance continuity plan drafting | 120 min |
| 12 | Stabilization | Sponsor + governance ratification | 90 min |
| 13+ | Embedding | Decision and (optional) embedding cadence | — |

No session is mandatory in its scheduled week. The cadence is
illustrative. Pacing belongs to the institution.

## Artifact handover sequence

The engagement produces a small, deliberate set of artifacts. Each is
handed to the institution at a specific moment and remains the
institution's property thereafter.

### Phase 1 artifacts

- **Executive Continuity Brief** — produced from the OCRA. Bound,
  numbered, distributed in printed form to the sponsor and a small
  number of named institutional readers.

### Phase 2 artifacts

- **Stewardship cartography (digital + printed)** — the Memory Holders
  map, produced by the workbook.
- **Stewardship Density Index** — a single deterministic figure with
  an interpretive paragraph, never a leaderboard.
- **Governance Entropy Workbook (PDF)** — the institution's mapping
  workbook, exported as a bound document at Phase 2 close.

### Phase 3 artifacts

- **Operational survivability priorities** — a one-page document
  drafted by the steward and the facilitator, ratified by the sponsor.
- **Governance continuity plan** — the institution's own document,
  drafted under facilitation, ratified by the governance body.

### Phase 4 artifacts

- **Embedding decision memo** — a short document recording the
  institution's decision to embed, defer, or operate independently.

No artifact contains individual performance information. No artifact
ranks institutions. No artifact is shared beyond the institution
without explicit, documented consent.

## What the engagement does not include

The OCI pilot deliberately excludes practices common to comparable
engagements:

- No formal kickoff event. The engagement begins with a quiet sponsor
  intake.
- No public announcement. Institutions choose if and when to name the
  engagement publicly. Most do not.
- No interim status reports. The sponsor and facilitator speak when
  there is something to say.
- No vendor presentations. The facilitator does not pitch additional
  scope mid-engagement.
- No success theatre. The engagement closes with a ratified plan, not
  a celebratory presentation.

These exclusions are intentional and constitutive.

## What is delivered, and by whom

| Deliverable | Produced by | Delivered to |
|---|---|---|
| Executive Continuity Brief | Facilitator (from OCRA) | Executive sponsor |
| Stewardship cartography | Steward + facilitator | Sponsor + governance liaison |
| Governance Entropy Workbook PDF | Workbook engine | Sponsor (printed and digital) |
| Operational survivability priorities | Steward + facilitator | Sponsor |
| Governance continuity plan | Institution (facilitated) | Governance body |
| Embedding decision memo | Sponsor | Institution + facilitator |

The facilitator is named on artifacts as a facilitator, not as an
author. The institution is the author of its own continuity.

---

## Engagement constraints

- **One pilot per institution at any given time.** Continuity work
  requires the institution's full attention to one inquiry.
- **One sponsor per pilot.** Distributed sponsorship erodes the
  pilot's protective frame.
- **Facilitator-to-institution ratio of one-to-one.** A single
  facilitator carries a single pilot at a time. Scaling happens by
  certifying additional facilitators, not by stacking pilots.
