**ARTIFACT TYPE:** Institutional Doctrine — Board-Facing Written Brief
**DOCTRINE_VERSION:** 1.0.0
**STATUS:** Canonical
**INTENDED USE:** Four-page written brief circulated to a governance body (board, council, executive committee) before or after the engagement is named to them
**LENGTH TARGET:** four printed pages

> This document is the board-facing companion to the Executive
> Briefing. Where the Executive Briefing speaks in a closed room,
> the Board Overview speaks on paper, to readers who were not in
> the room, on a cadence the governance body controls.

## Page 1 — Recognition

### What this document is

This is a four-page institutional brief on the OCI engagement
that the executive sponsor is hosting on behalf of the
institution. It is written for the governance body — for board
members, council members, or executive committee members — who
will not be present in working sessions and who need a written
account they can read at their own pace.

The brief is not a project report. It is not a status update. It
is a statement of what the institution is doing, why, and under
what conditions.

### The institutional question

Most institutions carry their operational continuity in the
calendars and memories of a small number of people. When those
people are unavailable — through transition, retirement,
illness, or the ordinary movement of careers — the institution
discovers what was being carried, and at what cost.

The discovery is usually late. The OCI engagement is the
institution's deliberate decision to make the discovery early
and on the institution's own terms.

### Why this work, and why now

The sponsor's reasons are recorded in the engagement's scope
document. Common institutional reasons include: a recent
leadership transition that exposed continuity dependencies; a
governance succession that requires lineage to be preserved; a
sector context in which continuity has become a governance
concern; or simply the institution's own decision to operate
with greater continuity awareness than it has previously
required of itself.

The governance body is asked to read this brief and to receive,
in due course, the governance continuity plan that will emerge
from the engagement.

## Page 2 — The work

### The four phases

The OCI pilot moves through four phases. Each phase has a
purpose, a posture, and an artifact the institution receives.

**Phase 1 — Recognition.** The institution looks at the shape of
its own continuity, with the support of a single instrument (the
Institutional Continuity Risk Assessment) and a facilitator.
The phase closes with an Executive Continuity Brief, read by
the sponsor.

**Phase 2 — Mapping.** The institution maps its memory holders,
its governance lineage, and its continuity breakpoints. The
Governance Entropy Workbook™ is the working instrument. The
Stewardship Density Index™ — a single deterministic measure of
how concentrated the institution's continuity is in a small
number of people — is reviewed in this phase.

**Phase 3 — Stabilization.** The institution reduces continuity
burden where it is most fragile. The phase closes with a
governance continuity plan, drafted by the institution under
facilitation and ratified by the governance body.

**Phase 4 — Embedding decision.** The institution decides whether
to embed OCI infrastructure into its long-term operations or to
operate the plan independently. Either outcome is honourable.

### Cadence

Twelve weeks is the median. Sixteen is unhurried. The
institution's rhythm takes precedence over the schedule.

### Who is in the room

One executive sponsor. One continuity steward. One governance
liaison. One facilitator. Occasionally one institutional witness
for specific sessions. No larger group is needed.

## Page 3 — The boundary

### What this work does not do

Three boundaries are constitutive and are documented in writing.

**This is not a surveillance engagement.** The work does not
measure individuals. It does not score stewards. It does not
characterise institutional staff. The Stewardship Density Index
is an institutional figure. The Memory Holders cartography names
roles and responsibilities; the stewards who hold those roles are
recognised, never measured. See
[OCI Anti-Surveillance Position](./OCI_ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_POSITION.md).

**This is not an autonomous-AI engagement.** Where AI assists the
facilitator's reasoning, the work is reviewer-led under explicit
human oversight. No AI surface concludes on the institution's
behalf. No AI surface acts on the institution's behalf. See
[OCI AI Boundary](./OCI_AI_BOUNDARY.md).

**This is not a software deployment.** The institution may decide
to embed OCI runtime infrastructure at Phase 4. The decision is
the institution's. The pilot does not require embedding to have
been useful.

### What the institution holds at the end

- An Executive Continuity Brief.
- A stewardship cartography.
- A Governance Entropy Workbook.
- A ratified governance continuity plan.
- An embedding decision recorded in writing.

Each artifact is the institution's property and remains in the
institution's records.

### What leaves the institution

Nothing without consent. Where the institution chooses to
contribute to sector-level continuity intelligence (the long-arc
Phase 5 of the OCI Method™), contribution is aggregated,
anonymised, reviewed by the governance liaison before each
publication, and withdrawable at any time. The default is no
participation. See
[OCI Data Handling](./OCI_DATA_HANDLING.md).

## Page 4 — What the governance body is asked to do

### During the engagement

- **Read this brief.** Once, at the cadence the body prefers.
- **Receive a short notification** from the governance liaison
  confirming that the engagement has begun, the cadence agreed,
  and the date at which the governance continuity plan will
  arrive for ratification.
- **Hold the cadence.** No interim status reports will arrive.
  The governance body is not asked to participate in working
  sessions. The sponsor and the facilitator will not seek the
  body's attention between activation and the plan's arrival.

### At the close of Stabilization

- **Receive the governance continuity plan.** Drafted by the
  institution under facilitation. Presented to the body by the
  sponsor, with the governance liaison's support, for ratification
  under the body's normal procedures.
- **Ratify, defer, or amend.** The body's authority over the
  plan is unaltered by the engagement. Ratification is a
  governance act, not a facilitator's request.

### At the embedding decision

- **Be informed** of the sponsor's recommendation and the
  institution's choice. Embedding is an operational decision; it
  does not typically require governance ratification unless the
  institution's own rules require it.

### Conditions under which the engagement may close

The engagement closes at the institution's instruction at any
time, without penalty. The conditions the sponsor has recorded
are documented in the engagement's scope document and are
available to the governance body on request.
