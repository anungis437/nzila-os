# OCI Method™

**Status:** Canonical doctrine reference for the Organizational Continuity Intelligence Method.

**Audience:** Internal stewards of the method, certified facilitators, sponsoring institutions evaluating the methodology, and partners enabled to deliver it under supervision.

**Convention used in this document:** "the method" refers to the OCI Method™ as a whole. "the pilot" refers to a specific institutional engagement run under the method. "the institution" refers to any organisation engaging the method.

---

## 1. Purpose of the method

The OCI Method™ exists to help institutions recognise, map, and steward their continuity exposures before those exposures become institutional failures.

The method is not a productivity programme. It is not a modernization framework. It is not an organisational change methodology in the conventional sense. It is a disciplined practice of:

- Recognising the institutional knowledge an organisation already carries,
- Mapping where that knowledge is concentrated, fragile, or exposed,
- Stabilising continuity without displacing the stewards who carry it,
- Embedding the resulting governance commitments so that successor stewards can inherit them honestly.

The method is human-led, reviewer-led, and governance-aware by design. It is delivered under explicit institutional consent and bounded by the OCI Anti-Surveillance Position and the OCI AI Boundary.

---

## 2. The method in five phases

The method consists of five canonical phases. The four-phase pilot framework (see `OCI_PILOT_FRAMEWORK.md`) is a delivery view of these five phases, with the final two phases consolidated into a single embedding phase for institutional handover.

### Phase 1 — Recognition

Purpose: establish that the institution and its stewards are recognised on their own terms before any continuity work begins.

Activities: institutional reading, sponsor interpretation conversation, stewardship pre-acknowledgement, voice and register alignment with the institution's own language.

Outputs: an institutional reading note, a sponsor interpretation summary, and an explicit recognition statement that the institution may keep or discard at the close of Phase 5.

### Phase 2 — Mapping

Purpose: produce an honest, narrow map of continuity exposures the institution already senses but has not yet named institutionally.

Activities: structured discovery using the Institutional Discovery Framework, stewardship density review, governance interpretation drift survey, modernization pressure inventory.

Outputs: a continuity map, a stewardship density register, a governance interpretation register, and a modernization-pressure note. All outputs are returned to the institution for review before any wider circulation.

### Phase 3 — Stabilization

Purpose: identify reductive moves that lower institutional fragility without shifting stewardship load to less senior or less recognised stewards.

Activities: continuity breakpoint working sessions, reductive redistribution drafting, governance interpretation clarification, stewardship recognition formalisation.

Outputs: a stabilization pathway document containing only changes the institution has explicitly accepted, a redistribution register with reciprocity terms named, and a list of items deliberately deferred.

### Phase 4 — Continuity Plan

Purpose: codify the institution's continuity commitments in a governance-receivable form.

Activities: continuity plan drafting, governance body ratification preparation, stewardship recognition wording finalisation, succession horizon naming.

Outputs: a continuity plan ratified by the institution's governance body, a stewardship recognition appendix, and a succession horizon register.

### Phase 5 — Embedding

Purpose: ensure successor stewards can inherit the method's results without re-engaging the method.

Activities: handover briefing to incoming stewards, integration of the continuity plan into the institution's own governance cadence, archival of method artefacts in the institution's own records, severance of operational dependency on the facilitator.

Outputs: a successor briefing package, an institutional archive index, and a closure note recording that the institution can now operate the continuity plan without facilitator presence.

The pilot framework collapses Phases 4 and 5 into a single embedding phase for delivery convenience. The method retains the five-phase form for doctrine, training, and certification.

## 3. Method principles

The method is governed by a small number of principles that hold across every phase. Drift from any of these principles is grounds for facilitator recertification review.

### 3.1 Recognition precedes diagnosis

The institution must feel recognised on its own terms before any continuity exposure is named. Diagnosis offered before recognition is, in the method's understanding, a category mistake. Stewards who feel diagnosed before they feel recognised will withhold the institutional context the method requires to function.

### 3.2 Stewardship is institutional, not personal

Stewardship density, stewardship burden, and stewardship recognition are institutional facts. The method does not characterise individual stewards. The method does not evaluate steward performance. The method does not produce inputs to personnel decisions. Stewards whose load is named in the method's outputs are named as institutional facts, not as personal subjects.

### 3.3 Reductive stabilization

Stabilization moves must reduce fragility without shifting load elsewhere. Moves that quietly transfer load to less senior or less recognised stewards are not stabilization; they are displacement. The method explicitly prefers deferring a move to displacing the load.

### 3.4 Governance-aware language

The method produces governance-receivable documents. Continuity plans are drafted for the institution's governance body in the institution's own register, with explicit room for the governance body to amend, adopt, or decline.

### 3.5 Reviewer-led use of any computation

The method permits computational support — discovery synthesis, language consistency review, document drafting assistance — only under reviewer-led use. No computation produces institutional output without a named human reviewer accepting that output on the institution's behalf. The OCI AI Boundary governs this principle in detail.

### 3.6 Opt-in aggregate intelligence

Any aggregation across institutions — sector baselines, stewardship-burden pattern frequencies, continuity-fragility distributions — is permitted only with the explicit, written, revocable opt-in of each contributing institution, and only at a level of aggregation that protects every contributing institution from re-identification. The OCI Benchmark Intelligence Layer documents the technical guarantees that support this principle.

### 3.7 Anti-surveillance

The method does not score individuals, does not infer behaviour, does not produce metrics about stewards or members, and does not produce ranked institutional comparisons that could function as surveillance signals. The OCI Anti-Surveillance Position is binding on every facilitator at every phase.

### 3.8 Honest deferral

The method explicitly recognises items that cannot be stabilised within the engagement. Honest deferral, with the reason named, is treated as a successful method outcome — not as a failure. Stabilization pretended where none was possible is a method violation.

## 4. Method artefacts

Each phase produces a defined set of artefacts. The method enforces artefact integrity rather than artefact volume.

| Phase | Artefact | Steward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition | Institutional reading note | Facilitator |
| Recognition | Sponsor interpretation summary | Facilitator + sponsor |
| Recognition | Recognition statement | Facilitator |
| Mapping | Continuity map | Facilitator + institution |
| Mapping | Stewardship density register | Facilitator + institution |
| Mapping | Governance interpretation register | Facilitator + governance liaison |
| Mapping | Modernization-pressure note | Facilitator + institution |
| Stabilization | Stabilization pathway document | Institution (facilitator drafts) |
| Stabilization | Redistribution register | Institution |
| Stabilization | Deferred items list | Institution |
| Continuity Plan | Continuity plan | Governance body |
| Continuity Plan | Stewardship recognition appendix | Governance body |
| Continuity Plan | Succession horizon register | Governance body |
| Embedding | Successor briefing package | Institution |
| Embedding | Institutional archive index | Institution |
| Embedding | Closure note | Institution |

Artefacts produced by the institution are owned by the institution. Artefacts produced by the facilitator on the institution's behalf are owned by the institution from the moment they are accepted. The facilitator retains no working copies beyond the engagement's stated retention window.

## 5. Method roles

The method is delivered by a small, named team. Role separation is structural, not nominal.

| Role | Responsibility | Held by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive sponsor | Institutional authorisation, recognition standing, scope boundary | Institution |
| Continuity steward | Day-to-day partnership with the facilitator, continuity context surfacing | Institution |
| Governance liaison | Governance body interface, interpretation register stewardship | Institution |
| Facilitator | Method delivery, artefact drafting, voice discipline | OCI-certified |
| Institutional witness | Observation, evidence of consent, continuity of memory | Institution or independent |

The facilitator is not a consultant in the conventional sense. The facilitator does not drive the institution toward outcomes the institution has not chosen. The facilitator does not retain ongoing operational presence beyond the engagement.

---

## 6. Method boundaries

The method explicitly does not do certain things. These boundaries are part of the method's definition.

The method does not:

- Adjudicate personnel matters or performance,
- Resolve labour disputes or grievances,
- Produce inputs to compensation or promotion decisions,
- Replace legal counsel, regulatory counsel, or auditor opinion,
- Function as a substitute for the institution's own governance process,
- Produce ranked comparisons between institutions,
- Operate without explicit, written institutional consent,
- Continue operating after the institution's consent is withdrawn.

Facilitators encountering requests that fall outside these boundaries are required to decline the request, name the boundary, and offer to refer the institution to an appropriate counterpart.

## 7. Method language register

The method holds a specific editorial register. The register is enforced in the facilitation guide, the workshop flows, the discovery framework, the conversation library, and the invariant tests that guard them.

The register favours:

- Continuity, stewardship, recognition, mapping, stabilization, embedding,
- Reviewer-led, reviewer-assisted, under human oversight,
- Governance-aware, institutional, governance of record,
- Honest deferral, irreducible loss, succession horizon.

The register refuses:

- Transformation vocabulary, productivity vocabulary, optimization vocabulary,
- Automation vocabulary applied to institutional work,
- Behavioural inference vocabulary, scoring vocabulary, surveillance vocabulary,
- Disruption vocabulary, modernization-as-virtue vocabulary,
- Marketing vocabulary that flattens institutional difference.

The register is not stylistic preference. It is institutional posture made operational.

## 10. What success looks like

The method has succeeded in an engagement when the institution can credibly state, in its own governance register, that:

1. It has recognised, in its own language, the continuity exposures it carries,
2. It has made stewardship recognition decisions it had previously deferred,
3. It has ratified a continuity plan that survives the departure of any single steward,
4. Its successor stewards can operate the continuity plan without facilitator presence,
5. Its governance body considers the engagement closed.

Success is institutional. Success is not measured by the facilitator. Success is not measured by activity volume. Success is not measured by artefact count.

The institution declares closure. The method then ends.
