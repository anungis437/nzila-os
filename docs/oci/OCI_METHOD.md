# OCI Method™

**Status:** Canonical. This is the single method file. `oci-method.md` is a redirect stub — see [`SUPERSEDED.md`](./SUPERSEDED.md).

**Audience:** Institutional sponsors, governance liaisons, certified facilitators, procurement evaluators.

**OCI** stands for **Organizational Continuity Intelligence**. This is the one expansion used everywhere in this repository. "Operational Continuity Intelligence" is a naming error to be corrected on sight.

**OCRA** stands for **Organizational Continuity Recognition Assessment**. OCRA is **Product 1** of OCI — the Recognition-phase instrument. OCI is the method and the brand; OCRA is the first, entry-layer product built on it. OCRA does not come "before" OCI — it is a layer inside OCI, the same way the Governance Entropy Workbook is a layer inside OCI. See [`docs/oci/CANON.md`](./CANON.md) for the one-paragraph answer.

> **Public-service front door.** For public-service audiences, this method is presented
> as **CIVIC by Nzila** (framing) and **CLEAR** (evidence-discipline articulation). CIVIC/CLEAR
> are not a second method — see [CIVIC ↔ OCI Alignment](../CIVIC_OCI_ALIGNMENT.md).

## 1. Purpose

OCI exists to help an institution recognise, map, and steward the continuity exposures it already carries — before those exposures become institutional failures.

It is not a productivity programme, a modernization framework, or an audit. It is a disciplined, facilitator-led practice: recognise what the institution already carries, map where it is concentrated or exposed, stabilise it without displacing the people who carry it, and hand the result to the institution's own governance so a successor can inherit it honestly.

The method is human-led, reviewer-led, and governance-aware. It is delivered under explicit institutional consent and bounded by the [OCI Anti-Surveillance Position](./OCI_ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_POSITION.md) and the [OCI AI Boundary](./OCI_AI_BOUNDARY.md).

**What OCI is, in one sentence a local president can repeat:** *OCI is a facilitator-led practice that helps an institution see and protect the knowledge it depends on, without scoring the people who hold it.*

## 2. The five phases

There is one clock. Every product and every SKU sits on it.

| # | Phase | What happens | Product layer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Recognition** | The institution sees the shape of its continuity for the first time. | P1 — OCRA |
| 2 | **Mapping** | The institution maps memory holders, governance lineage, and breakpoints. | P2 — Governance Entropy Workbook™ |
| 3 | **Stabilization** | Continuity burden is reduced where it is most fragile; the governance continuity plan is drafted and ratified. | P3 — OCI Diagnostic & Stabilization |
| 4 | **Infrastructure** | Continuity is embedded in the institution's own operating systems; it stops being a project. | P4 — OCI Runtime |
| 5 | **Intelligence** | (Opt-in, not sold today — see §6.5) The institution may join an anonymised, non-ranked sector network. | P5 — OCI Intelligence Network |

The [pilot delivery view](./OCI_PILOT_FRAMEWORK.md) collapses this into four delivery phases (Recognition, Mapping, Stabilization, Embedding), where "Embedding" is the decision point that opens onto Phases 4–5 above. That collapse is a delivery convenience, not a second method — see the cross-walk table in that document.

## 3. Principles

### 3.1 Recognition precedes diagnosis
The institution must feel recognised before any continuity exposure is named. Diagnosis offered before recognition is a category mistake.

### 3.2 Stewardship is institutional, not personal
Stewardship density, burden, and recognition are institutional facts. The method does not characterise individual stewards, evaluate performance, or feed personnel decisions.

### 3.3 Reductive stabilization
Stabilization moves reduce fragility. Moves that quietly shift load to a less senior or less recognised steward are displacement, not stabilization. The method prefers deferring a move to displacing the load.

### 3.4 Governance-aware language
Continuity plans are drafted for the institution's governance body, in the institution's own register, with explicit room to amend, adopt, or decline.

### 3.5 Reviewer-led use of any computation
Computational support (drafting, synthesis, classification) never produces institutional output without a named human reviewer accepting it on the institution's behalf. Governed in full by the [OCI AI Boundary](./OCI_AI_BOUNDARY.md).

### 3.6 Opt-in aggregate intelligence
Any cross-institution aggregation is opt-in, written, revocable, and aggregated at a level that protects every contributor from re-identification (minimum five contributing institutions per published band). See the [Anti-Surveillance Position](./OCI_ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_POSITION.md) for the instrument-by-instrument disposition and the P5 sale gate.

### 3.7 Anti-surveillance
No individual scores, no behavioural inference, no ranked institutional comparisons. Binding on every facilitator at every phase — governed in full by the [Anti-Surveillance Position](./OCI_ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_POSITION.md).

### 3.8 Honest deferral
An item the engagement cannot stabilise is named and deferred, not stabilised in appearance only. Deferral is a successful outcome, recorded on the Deferred Items List (§4). Pretending to stabilise what was not stabilised is a method violation.

## 4. Method artefacts

The method enforces artefact integrity, not artefact volume. **The facilitator drafts. The institution adopts or declines. A draft is not a finding until the institution adopts it.** This is binding on every row below — see §5 for the role it depends on.

| Phase | Artefact | Drafted by | Adopted by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition | Institutional reading note | Facilitator | Sponsor |
| Recognition | Recognition statement | Facilitator | Sponsor |
| Mapping | Continuity map | Facilitator | Institution |
| Mapping | Stewardship density register | Facilitator | Institution |
| Mapping | Governance interpretation register | Facilitator | Governance liaison |
| Stabilization | Stabilization pathway document | Facilitator | Institution |
| Stabilization | Redistribution register | Facilitator | Institution |
| Stabilization | **Deferred items list** — item, reason, owner, review date, explicit note that deferral is not a failure | Facilitator | Institution |
| Stabilization | **Governance continuity plan** | Facilitator drafts; governance body owns the ratified text | Governance body |
| Stabilization | **Closure minute** — one page recording that the institution declares closure, what was stabilised, what was deferred, and the date | Facilitator | Sponsor |
| Infrastructure | Successor briefing package | Institution | Institution |
| Infrastructure | Institutional archive index | Institution | Institution |

Artefacts are owned by the institution from the moment they are adopted. The facilitator retains no working copies beyond the engagement's stated retention window (see [OCI Data Handling](./OCI_DATA_HANDLING.md)).

## 5. Method roles

| Role | Responsibility | Held by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive sponsor | Authorises the engagement, ratifies the continuity plan, protects against premature operationalisation | Institution |
| Continuity steward | Primary fieldwork partner; carries operational memory | Institution |
| Governance liaison | Interface to the governance body | Institution |
| Facilitator | Drafts artefacts, holds voice discipline, never the author of record | OCI-certified |
| Institutional witness (optional) | Observes; not a decision-maker | Institution or independent |

**The facilitator drafts; the institution adopts or declines.** A facilitator is not a consultant driving the institution toward outcomes it has not chosen, and does not retain ongoing operational presence beyond the engagement.

## 6. Method boundaries

### 6.1 What OCI is
*OCI is a facilitator-led practice that helps an institution see and protect the knowledge it depends on, without scoring the people who hold it.* That sentence is the category. It replaces any ban-list ("not SaaS," "not governance software," "not an assessment vendor") — a ban-list is not a category, and OCRA and OCI Runtime are, plainly, an assessment and a hosted system respectively. Naming what something is settles more than naming what it is not.

### 6.2 What the method refuses to do
- Adjudicate personnel matters, performance, or labour disputes.
- Produce inputs to compensation, promotion, or discipline decisions.
- Replace legal counsel, regulatory counsel, or auditor opinion.
- Replace the institution's own governance process.
- Produce ranked comparisons between institutions.
- Operate without explicit, written institutional consent, or continue after consent is withdrawn.

A facilitator who crosses these is no longer practising the method.

### 6.3 Information boundary (labour engagements)
This rule is part of the method, not a privacy appendix. Default is **refuse**.

| Category | Examples | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In scope** | Governance lineage, role structure, succession, operational memory, decision records the institution offers | Facilitator may discuss, map, and record it |
| **Out of scope** | Member casework, bargaining strategy content, active grievance detail, staff performance | Facilitator declines and names the boundary |
| **Privileged** | Legal-privileged communications, active litigation strategy | Facilitator refuses without exception; refers to counsel |
| **Political** | Internal political strategy, election positioning | Facilitator refuses; this is not a continuity question |

If a fact volunteered by a steward falls into an out-of-scope, privileged, or political category, the facilitator does not use it, does not record it, and tells the steward it will not be used.

### 6.4 Kill criteria — when an institution is not assessable
The facilitator stops the engagement, in writing, without describing it as a failed sale, when any of the following is true:

1. **No named executive sponsor.** No single accountable leader can ratify a continuity plan.
2. **Active adversarial process.** The institution is in active litigation, a contested labour action, or a governance crisis where continuity work would be read as taking a side.
3. **Consent cannot be sustained.** The institution cannot commit to steward time, or withdraws consent, before Recognition closes.

Stopping under any of these is the method working correctly, not a failure.

### 6.5 P5 / Intelligence Network gate
P5 aggregation is opt-in, non-ranked, and **not sold** until two completed paid engagements exist and a second certified facilitator is active. See the [Anti-Surveillance Position](./OCI_ANTI_SURVEILLANCE_POSITION.md) and the [Product Ladder](./oci-product-ladder.md).

## 7. Facilitation infrastructure

### 7.1 Session types
Recognition session, discovery/mapping session, stabilization working session, governance ratification session, embedding/closure session. A session that does not match one of these five is not part of the method.

### 7.2 Voice discipline
The register favours continuity, stewardship, recognition, mapping, stabilization, honest deferral, governance-aware. It refuses transformation/productivity/optimization vocabulary, behavioural-inference and scoring vocabulary, and "world-class," "unique," or "only platform" claims. Enforced in artefact drafting; audited at certification.

## 8. Deployment model

| Form | What it is | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot engagement | A bounded first engagement | Institution may conclude without proceeding |
| Workshop | A single recognition/discovery session | Introduces the method; not a sale |
| Executive session | Senior-leader-only preparation or ratification support | Not an abbreviated pilot |
| Board reporting | A governance-receivable reading | Amended, adopted, or declined by the body |
| Ongoing practice | Institution operates its own continuity cadence; facilitator on periodic review | Presumes successful Phase 4 embedding |

No other deployment form is offered under the OCI name.
