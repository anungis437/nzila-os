# OCI Workshop Opening Script

**ARTIFACT TYPE:** Institutional Doctrine — Facilitation Scripts
**DOCTRINE_VERSION:** 1.0.0
**STATUS:** Canonical
**INTENDED USE:** Facilitator opening lines for the five workshop session types
**PARENT FRAMEWORK:** [OCI Delivery Model](./OCI_DELIVERY_MODEL.md)

> An opening is a posture. The first ninety seconds of a working
> session establishes whether the room is in recognition mode or
> in diagnosis mode. These scripts hold the facilitator to
> recognition.

---

## How to use these scripts

Each script is one to two minutes spoken aloud, by the
facilitator, at the opening of the session type indicated.
Scripts are not memorised. They are read once before the
session, internalised, and delivered in the facilitator's own
voice — but the structure and the boundary statements remain.

Variation is permitted in cadence and word choice; the opening
recognition, the boundary statement, and the closing posture
must remain. These three elements are constitutive.

The scripts are written for in-person delivery. For remote
sessions, the facilitator opens with the same words after
confirming everyone is present and the room is quiet.

---

## Script 1 — Executive Interpretation Session

**When delivered:** End of Phase 1 (Recognition), 90 minutes,
sponsor and the facilitator (and at most one institutional
reader of the Brief). The session at which the Executive
Continuity Brief is read together.

**Opening recognition (60 seconds):**

> "Thank you for the time today. We have one document in the
> room — the Executive Continuity Brief — and we will read it
> together at the pace you set. Before we open it, I want to
> say one thing.
>
> The Brief is a record of what your institution has been
> carrying. It is not a finding about your leadership, and it
> is not a recommendation about your operations. It is an
> articulation of a shape that has been there. Our work today
> is to look at that shape together."

**Boundary statement (30 seconds):**

> "There are three things I will not do today. I will not
> characterise individuals. I will not propose actions. I will
> not ask you for a decision about what comes next. After we
> read the Brief together, you will take it with you, and you
> will decide in your own time whether to move into mapping."

**Closing posture before opening the Brief (15 seconds):**

> "There are no wrong questions in this room. Take the pages at
> your pace. We can stop wherever you want to stop."

## Script 2 — Workbook Orientation Session

**When delivered:** Opening of Phase 2 (Mapping), 60 minutes,
sponsor, continuity steward, and facilitator. The session that
introduces the Governance Entropy Workbook™ to the institution.

**Opening recognition (60 seconds):**

> "We are beginning the mapping phase. Mapping is the slowest
> phase of the engagement, and it is the phase whose record
> the institution keeps the longest. The workbook in front of
> us is the instrument; the substance is what you and your
> colleagues hold in memory and in practice.
>
> Today we orient. We open the workbook together. We name the
> sections. We agree the cadence at which we will fill it. We
> do not fill it today."

**Boundary statement (45 seconds):**

> "The workbook will name memory holders by role and by name.
> A steward who appears in the workbook is being recognised.
> They are not being measured. The Stewardship Density Index
> is a single institutional figure. It is not a leaderboard.
> It is not shared outside this engagement without your
> explicit approval, per publication, per audience.
>
> Anything we record today, you can have removed at any time
> without explanation."

**Closing posture (15 seconds):**

> "We open the workbook now. We will read the first section
> together, and we will stop wherever it is right to stop."

## Script 3 — Stewardship Density Review

**When delivered:** Mid-to-late Phase 2, 90 minutes, sponsor,
governance liaison, continuity steward, and facilitator. The
session at which the Stewardship Density Index is read and
interpreted with the institution.

**Opening recognition (60 seconds):**

> "Today we read the Stewardship Density Index together. The
> Index is a single figure. It describes how much of your
> institution's continuity is held by how few of its people.
>
> Before we look at the figure, I want to name what the figure
> is, and what it is not. It is a measure of concentration. It
> is not a measure of fragility in absolute terms, and it is
> not a measure of the people who carry the load. The people
> who carry the load are the reason the institution has
> functioned. The Index names a situation; it does not assign
> responsibility."

**Boundary statement (45 seconds):**

> "There is no comparable institution's figure on the page. I
> will not show you a benchmark. The reason is that comparison
> across institutions of different sizes, sectors, and ages
> distorts the interpretation. Your figure is your figure.
>
> If at any point the conversation moves toward characterising
> a specific steward, I will return it to the institutional
> level. That is the discipline of this method."

**Closing posture (15 seconds):**

> "We will turn the page now."

## Script 4 — Continuity Breakpoint Working Session

**When delivered:** Opening of Phase 3 (Stabilization), 120
minutes, sponsor, continuity steward, facilitator, and the
governance liaison if available. The session at which the
institution identifies which continuity dependencies to
stabilise first.

**Opening recognition (75 seconds):**

> "We move from mapping to stabilisation today. The mapping
> phase has produced a picture; the stabilisation phase
> produces actions. We will not act on everything. We will act
> on the few things that, if they fail, the institution would
> notice within a quarter.
>
> Stabilisation is a reductive discipline. It removes
> fragility. It does not add work. If a stabilisation move
> looks like it adds work, we re-shape it or we do not do it.
> The institution's operating load is the operating load. Our
> task is to reduce one form of fragility within it, not to
> stack additional initiatives on top of it."

**Boundary statement (30 seconds):**

> "I will not recommend a stabilisation move that requires a
> specific named steward to take on additional load. If a move
> implies that, we will redesign it, or we will defer it."

**Closing posture (15 seconds):**

> "We have the cartography in front of us. We start with the
> question: which dependency, if it failed in the next
> quarter, would the institution most regret?"

## Script 5 — Governance Continuity Plan Ratification Session

**When delivered:** Close of Phase 3, 90 minutes, sponsor,
governance liaison, continuity steward, facilitator, and (at
the institution's choice) representatives of the governance
body. The session at which the draft governance continuity
plan is reviewed in preparation for ratification by the
governance body.

**Opening recognition (60 seconds):**

> "We are reading the institution's governance continuity plan
> today. The plan was drafted by you, under facilitation. The
> facilitator's role today is to read the plan with you, to
> note where the language could be clearer, and to confirm
> that the plan is yours.
>
> The ratification, when it happens, is the governance body's
> act under your own procedures. Today's session prepares the
> document for that act; it does not replace it."

**Boundary statement (45 seconds):**

> "The plan does not name individuals as single points of
> stewardship without naming the institutional response to
> that fact. The plan does not name fragility without naming
> the move the institution intends to make. The plan does not
> commit the governance body to anything beyond what the body
> itself will ratify.
>
> If the body chooses not to ratify, that is a governance
> decision. The plan is still yours."

**Closing posture (15 seconds):**

> "We read the plan from the first page now."

## Cross-session rules

These rules apply to every opening, irrespective of script.

- **The facilitator opens the session.** Not the sponsor. The
  facilitator's opening establishes the working posture. The
  sponsor's contributions follow.
- **The facilitator does not open with thanks for the
  institution's "trust." Trust is not asserted; it is earned by
  conduct.
- **The facilitator does not refer to the institution as a
  "client." The institution is the institution.
- **The facilitator does not open with the engagement's
  branding.** No reference to the Method, the Framework, or the
  product family by name unless the session script names it.
- **The facilitator does not ask "how is everyone today?" as an
  opening.** The session is professional and the opening is
  professional.

---

## What is never in an opening

- No characterisation of individuals.
- No assertion of urgency.
- No promise of outcomes.
- No reference to comparable institutions.
- No reference to AI assistance as the source of the work. The
  facilitator is the source; AI is the assistance.
- No reference to "transformation," "optimisation," or
  "productivity." None of those words belong to this work.
