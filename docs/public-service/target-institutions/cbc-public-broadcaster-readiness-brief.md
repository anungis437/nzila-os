# CBC/Radio-Canada — Public Broadcaster Readiness Brief

> **Internal only.** This is a pre-outreach readiness analysis. It is not a public page, a pitch, a
> one-pager, or a document to be sent to CBC/Radio-Canada. Do not publish, route, or forward it.

## 1. Executive verdict

CBC/Radio-Canada is a strong conceptual fit for CIVIC/CLEAR, but not yet ready for direct
institutional outreach without a tailored public-broadcaster framing. Warm discovery is acceptable;
formal pitch, product discussion, procurement language, and SAGE discussion are not.

Readiness snapshot:

- Generic Canadian public-sector readiness: strong.
- CBC/Radio-Canada-specific readiness: not yet — needs a public-broadcaster adaptation.
- Ready for Richard to mention a public broadcaster like CBC as a possible fit: yes, cautiously.
- Ready for a warm, feedback-oriented intro to someone CBC-adjacent: yes, if framed around
  accessibility / continuity / digital modernization evidence.
- Ready to send the generic CIVIC package directly to CBC: no.
- Ready for a formal CBC pitch, or any procurement/vendor conversation: no.

## 2. Why CBC/Radio-Canada is a fit

The fit is about public-trust continuity for a mandate-driven public media institution — not
"government modernization." Framed around:

- institutional memory
- public-trust continuity
- mandate traceability
- accessibility implementation traceability
- Official Languages operating continuity
- digital modernization reviewability
- workforce transition
- evidence confidence
- responsible modernization

CBC/Radio-Canada's strategy emphasizes proximity, digital agility, local and regional presence,
official-language minority communities, Indigenous Peoples, youth and newcomers, and public value —
all of which live in the operating layer between commitment and delivery, exactly where CLEAR's
visibility-and-traceability lens applies.

## 3. Why CBC/Radio-Canada is sensitive

CBC is not just "government." It is simultaneously a national public broadcaster, Crown corporation,
cultural institution, employer, digital platform, journalistic organization, accessibility actor,
Official Languages actor, Indigenous/EDI actor, and public-trust institution. Sensitivities:

- editorial independence
- newsroom independence
- journalistic standards
- programming and creative independence
- public trust / political scrutiny
- procurement/vendor-code sensitivity
- ATIP / privacy / records complexity (journalistic/creative/programming carve-outs)
- Indigenous strategy and consultation sensitivity
- EDI and accessibility commitments
- unionized / workforce context

## 4. Institutional context

- CBC/Radio-Canada is a national public broadcaster and Crown corporation.
- It operates at arm's length from government.
- Editorial, journalistic, creative, and programming independence must be protected — it is a legal
  and institutional red line, not a preference.
- CBC/Radio-Canada has public commitments around accessibility, EDI, Indigenous strategy, Official
  Languages, digital agility, public value, and trust.
- Its records/access/privacy posture differs from ordinary departments where journalistic, creative,
  or programming material is implicated (access rights do not apply to information controlled by CBC
  relating to journalistic, creative, or programming activities other than general administration).
- Governance differs too: the corporate plan is tabled with the Minister for information rather than
  government approval, unlike many other Crown corporations.
- CBC/Radio-Canada already has mature accountability structures — journalistic standards, an
  ombudsman, a corporate code of conduct, a vendor code of conduct, accessibility and EDI plans, an
  Indigenous strategy, annual reports, and board governance. Do not imply any of these are missing.

2. Corporate strategy / planning
3. Enterprise risk / assurance
4. Internal audit / evaluation

6. Digital transformation / service design
7. Information management / records governance

## 6. No-go zones

- Do not approach through newsroom/editorial.
- Do not discuss content decisions.
- Do not discuss journalistic standards.
- Do not discuss programming choices.
- Do not mention SAGE in first contact.
- Do not frame CIVIC as software.
- Do not use procurement, pilot, demo, SOW, or pricing language.
- Do not mention UnionEyes or CourtLens.
- Do not describe this as an assessment, audit, score, or compliance check.

## 7. Required CBC-specific boundary language

Both statements must be present in any CBC-facing framing:

> CIVIC and CLEAR do not review, assess, influence, score, govern, or provide evidence judgments on
> editorial decisions, journalistic judgment, newsroom independence, programming choices, creative
> decisions, content selection, or ombudsman processes.

> For CBC/Radio-Canada, any evidence work must avoid journalistic, creative, programming,
> source-protection, or newsroom material unless CBC/Radio-Canada's own authorized functions
> explicitly define the scope and controls.

Additional handling:

- **Indigenous strategy:** Where Indigenous strategy, relationships, services, content, or data may be
  implicated, CIVIC/CLEAR would defer to CBC/Radio-Canada's Indigenous Office, consultation
  practices, legal/policy authorities, and community-specific protocols. Do not frame this as an
  "evidence audit" of Indigenous commitments — that reads as extractive.
- **EDI:** Frame only as visibility and traceability of stated commitments through operations and
  review. Never as EDI compliance, anti-racism gap detection, bias detection, or content diversity
  scoring.
- **Public trust:** Use public-trust continuity, mandate traceability, reviewability through change,
  evidence confidence, and institutional memory. Avoid trust scoring, legitimacy review, or public
  confidence assessment.
- **Language:** Use public broadcaster, Crown corporation, public media institution, mandate-driven
  institution, national public broadcaster. Avoid department, departmental authority, government
  department, program authority, or bureaucracy in CBC-facing materials (these remain fine in the
  canonical CLEAR/SAGE docs).

## 8. Best first wedge

**Accessibility implementation traceability + institutional continuity + digital modernization
evidence.**

Why: accessibility is public, strategic, cross-functional, and evidence-oriented, and is tied to
employee experience, content and platform accessibility, digital services, procurement, and barrier
removal. CBC's accessibility plan spans employment, built environment, ICT, communication,
procurement, program/service design and delivery, and transportation, with emphasis on digital
accessibility and inclusive employee experience. That makes it the safest, most concrete surface for
a first conversation.

Not: newsroom governance, editorial quality, content bias, or journalistic accountability.

## 9. Safe first-send posture

The first send must be:

- feedback-oriented
- not a pitch
- not a procurement ask
- not a product introduction
- not a platform demonstration
- not a pilot proposal

Recommended framing language:

> We are testing whether the CIVIC framing is useful for mandate-driven public institutions facing
> workforce transition, accessibility commitments, digital modernization, and public-trust
> continuity. This is not about editorial work, programming decisions, or newsroom independence.

CBC-safe first thesis:

> Public broadcasters face a specific continuity problem: they must preserve institutional memory,
> mandate traceability, public trust, accessibility commitments, bilingual operating capacity,
> inclusion commitments, and responsible modernization while audiences, technology, workforce
> expectations, and accountability pressures are changing at the same time.

CBC-safe boundary:

> This is not about editorial work, content decisions, journalistic standards, newsroom independence,
> or programming choices. The question is narrower: how a mandate-driven public institution preserves
> continuity, evidence, accountability, and reviewability through organizational and digital change.
