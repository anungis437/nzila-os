## 1. Institutional question

How can the institution preserve mandate traceability, institutional memory, accessibility
implementation evidence, and digital modernization reviewability during workforce and technology change
without replacing board governance, corporate accountability, source systems, or accountable human
decision-making?

## 2. Institution type and adaptation row

Crown corporation — adaptation-matrix row "Crown corporation" (arm's-length governance; corporate
records authoritative; primary sensitivity: arm's-length governance and independence).

## 3. Scope and exclusions

- **In scope:** organizing public and (if authorized) internal evidence about mandate traceability,
  institutional memory, accessibility implementation, and modernization reviewability.
- **Excluded:** board governance, corporate accountability, arm's-length independence, legal/HR/audit
  determinations, compliance certification, source-system replacement, direct data access.

## 4. Accountable functions

Executive sponsor; mandate/corporate strategy owner; service or operating owner; digital/information
management lead; privacy/access/records; legal; internal audit/risk; accessibility/Official
Languages/EDI; people and culture. The institution owns every conclusion.

## 5. Evidence source register summary

Ten sources registered (see evidence-source-register.md): public corporate plan, annual report,
accessibility plan, digital strategy, governance charter, service standards, and public risk/audit
summary (references); plus three authorized-only internal sources (owner interview notes,
implementation tracker, records inventory) which remain pending authorization. Reference-and-link-first
preserved; no direct system access assumed.

## 6. CLEAR pillar findings

- **Continuity** — Institutional memory for the mandate appears partly dependent on a small number of
  experienced roles; durable documentation exists publicly at the strategy level but operating-level
  memory is the open question. *(Observation; confidence: low–moderate.)*
- **Legitimacy** — Mandate and authority are publicly traceable via the governance charter and
  corporate plan; the open question is whether that authority remains visible at the operating layer.
- **Evidence** — Public evidence is strong at the commitment level; operating evidence of
  implementation is fragmented across public documents and (pending) internal trackers.
- **Accountability** — Accountable functions are identifiable; ownership of mandate traceability and
  accessibility implementation *through change* is the gap.
- **Readiness** — Digital modernization is active; the open question is whether modernization decisions
  are reviewable before they become defaults, and whether accessibility evidence survives the change.

## 7. Evidence confidence

Overall **low–moderate**, based mainly on public sources; internal authorized sources are pending.
Dimensions: provenance moderate (public), durability uncertain, corroboration partial, recency mixed,
completeness partial, decision relevance moderate, sensitivity low for public sources.

## 8. Risk tier

**Level 2 — Moderate institutional continuity question, applied for this fictional dry run. In a real
engagement, risk tiering would require accountable human confirmation.**

## 9. Human-review path

Strategy owner reviews framing → accessibility and digital leads review implementation/readiness →
privacy/records confirms no protected material → internal audit/risk notes evidence gaps → executive
sponsor holds overall accountability. No decision is made by the operator.

## 10. Key gaps and uncertainty

- No authorized internal owner confirmation yet.
- Operating-layer evidence beyond public documents is not yet available.
- Whether modernization review points exist before defaults are set is unconfirmed.

## 11. Next evidence needed

- Authorized owner interview on where mandate/accessibility memory actually lives.
- Traceability of one accessibility commitment from strategy → operation → review.
- Confirmation of a review checkpoint before a modernization change becomes the default.

## 12. Boundary check

- No prohibited conclusions (no legal/HR/equity/accessibility/Official Languages/audit/compliance
  determination).
- No independence-bearing-function interference (board governance, corporate accountability, audit
  independence untouched).
- No product/procurement/pricing/pilot/demo/SOW language.
- Source-system authority preserved; personal information minimized; SAGE not described as launched.

## 13. Recommended decision posture

Proceed only as an internal evidence-organization exercise. Do not create public copy, product claims,
procurement language, or institution-specific outreach from this proof run.
