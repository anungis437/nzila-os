# Feedback Integration Decision Record

> Internal governance material. Copy this template whenever feedback leads to a change (or a decided
> non-change) in the public-service stack. It creates an auditable trail from signal to change and
> confirms boundaries were held.

## 1. Decision summary

*One or two sentences: what was decided and why.*

## 2. Feedback source

*Who/what the feedback came from, the conversation-note reference, and the date.*

## 3. Signal classification

*Feedback categories, signal quality level, and rubric total (per the signal-classification rubric).*

## 4. Affected layer

Select the layer(s) touched:

- outreach language
- public-service front door
- article / insight
- FAQ
- conversation guide
- adaptation framework
- target-institution brief
- CLEAR method
- SAGE architecture
- exported package
- no change

## 5. Alternatives considered

*Other integration paths weighed (including "no action" and "parking lot") and why they were not
chosen.*

## 6. Decision

*The chosen action, at the chosen layer, and the rationale tied to the rubric threshold.*

## 7. Files changed

*Exact files, or "none" for a no-change / parking-lot decision.*

## 8. Boundaries checked

Confirm each remains intact (all must be true to proceed):

- [ ] no automated legal / HR / equity / clinical / enforcement / adjudicative decisioning
- [ ] no findings of wrongdoing, racism, discrimination, misconduct, or breach
- [ ] no scoring of people, groups, programs, or institutions
- [ ] no compliance certification
- [ ] no procurement / pricing / pilot / demo / SOW language
- [ ] no source-system replacement
- [ ] no direct data-access implication
- [ ] no SAGE availability claim
- [ ] institution-specific red lines respected

## 9. Validation run

*For any material change: `pnpm validate:docs`, `pnpm final:go`, the copy-governance scan, and (for
CBC-adjacent or high-risk material) the containment scans. Record results.*

## 10. Follow-up needed

*Anything still open: monitoring for a repeated pattern, a promised follow-up, a second-touch asset to
prepare, or a re-review trigger.*
