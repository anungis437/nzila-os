# Signal Classification Rubric

> Internal governance material. Defines how to judge whether feedback is strong enough to change the
> stack, and at which layer. Used together with the feedback intake protocol and the decision-record
> template.

## 1. Purpose

Not all feedback deserves a change, and strong-sounding reactions can be weak signals. This rubric
scores a piece of feedback across defined dimensions so that integration decisions are consistent,
defensible, and proportionate — never driven by the loudest or most recent voice.

## 2. Signal dimensions

- **role relevance** — is the person's role actually connected to the question?
- **institution relevance** — does it apply to a real, in-scope institution type?
- **specificity** — is it concrete and actionable, or vague?
- **repeated pattern** — has the same signal appeared from independent sources?
- **risk severity** — does it flag a real reputational, political, or institutional risk?
- **evidence value** — does it teach us something durable about framing or fit?
- **actionability** — can we actually act on it without overreaching?
- **alignment with boundaries** — can it be acted on without weakening non-negotiable boundaries?

## 3. Signal strength scoring

Score each of the eight dimensions on a 0–3 scale:

- **0 — absent / not useful**
- **1 — weak**
- **2 — useful**
- **3 — strong**

Total range: 0–24.

## 4. What each score means

- **0** on a dimension means it contributes nothing; do not inflate scores to justify a change.
- **alignment with boundaries** is special: a low score here is a stop signal, not just a low number
  (see the hard rule).
- **repeated pattern** is what most often moves feedback from "internal guidance" into "canonical
  change." A single high-context reaction rarely reaches the top band on its own.

## 5. Integration threshold

| Total score | Response |
| --- | --- |
| **0–7** | Park or ignore |
| **8–12** | Monitor / clarify |
| **13–17** | Update internal guidance (adaptation framework, conversation guide, or a target-institution brief) |
| **18–21** | Update public copy or FAQ, with review |
| **22–24** | Consider a canonical method (CLEAR) or architecture (SAGE) update |

The threshold sets the *ceiling* for a change, not an obligation. A qualifying score permits a change;
human judgment still decides whether to make it.

## 6. Examples

- *"Nice framing, felt clear"* from a casual contact → role relevance 1, institution relevance 0,
  specificity 0 → low total → **park or ignore**.
- *An accessibility lead says the accessibility wedge is exactly right but "continuity" reads as IT
  disaster-recovery* → role relevance 3, institution relevance 3, specificity 3, boundaries 3, others
  moderate → mid-high total → **update internal guidance / conversation guide** (clarify the
  continuity meaning), not a canonical rewrite.
- *Three independent public-sector reviewers each say the CLEAR confidence levels are unclear without
  the rubric* → repeated pattern 3, role relevance 3, evidence value 3, specificity 3, boundaries 3 →
  top band → **consider a canonical CLEAR clarification**, with review.

## 7. Anti-patterns

- **Score inflation** — nudging numbers up to justify a change you already wanted.
- **Recency bias** — treating the latest comment as the strongest signal.
- **Enthusiasm bias** — mistaking excitement for role/institution relevance.
- **Single-source canonical change** — moving CLEAR or SAGE on one reaction, without a repeated
  pattern.
- **Boundary erosion** — acting on a high total while quietly weakening a non-negotiable boundary.

## Hard rule

**No score can justify weakening non-negotiable boundaries.** Human review stays authoritative; no
automated legal/HR/equity/clinical/enforcement/adjudicative decisioning; no findings of wrongdoing; no
scoring of people, groups, programs, or institutions; no compliance certification; no
procurement/pricing/pilot/demo/SOW language; no source-system replacement; no direct data-access
implication; no SAGE availability claim; institution-specific red lines respected. If acting on
feedback would breach any of these, the answer is no — regardless of the total score.
