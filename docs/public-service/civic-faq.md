# CIVIC FAQ

*Cautious, government-native answers to likely questions. Written for a public-service
audience evaluating whether the CIVIC framing is useful.*

### Is this a product?

Not in this conversation. CIVIC is introduced first as a public-service continuity
framing and a briefing conversation. Any tooling or offering would come much later, if
a real institutional need is confirmed.

### Is this an assessment?

No. There is no paid assessment being offered here. CLEAR, the evidence discipline
behind CIVIC, can *later* support structured briefs, but the first touchpoint is a
feedback conversation, not an assessment engagement.

### Is this equity consulting?

No. CIVIC does not provide equity determinations or consulting findings. It treats
equity implementation as one area where commitments need to stay traceable through
workflows, ownership, evidence, and recourse — supporting human decision-makers rather
than replacing them.

### Is this AI?

CIVIC is a framing, not an AI system. Where technology is involved later, it is designed
to organize evidence, surface uncertainty, and support human-reviewed decisions — never
to make autonomous determinations. Human review remains authoritative.

### Is this "UnionEyes for government"?

No. UnionEyes is separate labor-sector work and prior experience. CIVIC is a distinct
public-service front door so that continuity and modernization framing can be evaluated
on its own terms, without being routed through labor-sector branding.

### Does this detect racism?

No. CIVIC is not a racism detector and does not produce automated findings of
discrimination or racism. Anti-racism / ABR is used only as a visibility and traceability
lens — helping human reviewers see whether equity and anti-racism commitments remain
visible in workflows and evidence.

### Does this replace HR, equity, legal, or internal audit?

No. It is explicitly designed not to. CIVIC supports governance and accountability;
it does not replace HR, equity offices, legal review, internal audit, or program
accountability. Those functions remain the decision-makers.

### Who would own this in government?

That is one of the questions we are exploring. Depending on the institution, it could
touch service continuity and operations, modernization / responsible-AI, enterprise risk
and internal audit, or equity implementation. Part of the conversation is learning where
it naturally fits.

### Why now?

Workforce transition, modernization and automation pressure, rising accountability
expectations, and institutional-memory loss are arriving at the same time. The risk is
that undocumented knowledge and fragmented evidence get embedded into redesigned services
before anyone can see it.

### What would a first conversation cover?

Whether the CIVIC framing is useful, where it would fit institutionally, and what kind of
evidence brief would be valuable to an executive managing workforce and modernization
pressure. It is a short conversation — you can **request a briefing** without any
commitment.
