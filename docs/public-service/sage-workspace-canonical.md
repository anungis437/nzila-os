# The SAGE Workspace

## Service Assurance & Governance Evidence — the future workspace layer behind CIVIC

SAGE is the future workspace direction that could turn CLEAR from a repeatable method into an
operational evidence environment. Where CIVIC opens the conversation and CLEAR defines the evidence
discipline, SAGE is the possible operating layer that would help public institutions organize
service continuity, governance evidence, review routing, decision context, and assurance-ready
outputs — without replacing departmental systems of record or accountable human decision-making.

**SAGE = Service Assurance & Governance Evidence.**

This document is the canonical architecture for that future direction. It is not a product, a
procurement offer, a compliance engine, or an automated decision system, and nothing here is
available or launched today. SAGE inherits every boundary CLEAR established; it does not weaken or
expand them.

---

## 1. What SAGE is

SAGE is the future workspace direction for turning CLEAR from a repeatable method into an operational
evidence environment. It helps public institutions organize service continuity, governance evidence,
review routing, decision context, and assurance-ready outputs without replacing departmental systems
of record or accountable human decision-making.

It is best understood as:

- a **future workspace direction**, not a current system;
- a **possible operating layer** beneath CIVIC and CLEAR;
- a **human-reviewed evidence environment**, not an autonomous engine;
- a producer of **assurance-ready outputs** for accountable humans to review and decide upon.

SAGE would organize what accountable humans need to review, explain, and preserve through change. It
would never draw the conclusion itself.

---

## 2. What SAGE is not

- SAGE is not a compliance engine.
- SAGE is not a legal, HR, equity, accessibility, Official Languages, privacy, ATIP, or audit
  authority.
- SAGE is not an automated decision system.
- SAGE is not a system of record.
- SAGE is not a replacement for departmental information-management or IT systems.
- SAGE is not a scoring dashboard or automated assurance engine.
- SAGE does not certify compliance.
- SAGE does not make findings of racism, discrimination, wrongdoing, misconduct, or legal breach.
- SAGE does not score, rank, or rate people, groups, programs, or institutions.

SAGE is a controlled evidence workspace that organizes what accountable humans need to review,
explain, and preserve through change — nothing more.

---

## 3. Relationship to CIVIC and CLEAR

The three layers are deliberately simple and sequential:

- **CIVIC** opens the conversation — the public-service continuity front door.
- **CLEAR** defines the evidence discipline — the repeatable, human-reviewed method.
- **SAGE** is the future workspace layer that could make CLEAR repeatable at institutional scale.

Two rules keep SAGE in its lane:

- **SAGE inherits CLEAR's boundaries** — human review authoritative, no automated legal/HR/equity
  decisions, no automated findings of wrongdoing, evidence confidence and uncertainty stated plainly,
  privacy and minimization from the start, and the department owns the conclusion.
- **SAGE does not expand CLEAR** into automated decisions or compliance certification. It makes the
  method easier to run; it does not change what the method is allowed to do.

---

## 4. Why SAGE exists

Public institutions often have evidence — but it is fragmented. It lives across systems, inboxes,
people, records, audit files, HR files, service systems, policy documents, and operational
workarounds. During workforce transition and modernization, the evidence needed to explain decisions,
preserve service logic, and maintain accountability becomes difficult to find, trust, and review.

CLEAR provides the discipline to organize that evidence into decision-grade material. But running the
method by hand, one brief at a time, does not scale across a large institution facing many
simultaneous continuity questions.

SAGE exists to organize that evidence into reviewable institutional workspaces — so the CLEAR method
can be applied repeatably, with provenance and uncertainty preserved, while systems of record and
human accountability remain exactly where they are.

---

## 5. SAGE workspace principles

- **Human review remains authoritative** — SAGE organizes; humans decide.
- **Evidence provenance is visible** — every item traces to its source.
- **Uncertainty is preserved, not hidden** — confidence and gaps travel with the evidence.
- **Minimum personal information** — collect only what the question requires.
- **Systems of record remain authoritative** — SAGE references; it does not supplant.
- **Role-based access by accountable function** — need-to-know by design.
- **Exportable evidence packages** — outputs leave cleanly for human review.
- **No shadow governance** — SAGE never becomes a parallel decision authority.
- **No hidden scoring** — no concealed ratings of people, programs, or institutions.
- **No automated conclusions** — SAGE preserves the review path, not the verdict.
- **Bilingual and accessible by design** — Official Languages and accessibility are built in, not
  added later.

## 6. Core workspace capabilities

These are future-facing capability groups, not current features. Each is described by its purpose, the evidence
it would handle, its human owner, and what it must never decide.

### 1. Institutional question workspace
- **Purpose:** Frame the specific continuity, memory, evidence, accountability, or modernization
  question to be examined.
- **Evidence handled:** The question statement, scope, and exclusions.
- **Human owner:** Program or service owner.
- **Must not decide:** Whether the answer is acceptable — that is for accountable humans.

### 2. Evidence source register
- **Purpose:** Record where relevant evidence lives and how strong each source is.
- **Evidence handled:** Source references, provenance, sensitivity flags.
- **Human owner:** Service owner, with privacy/ATIP input where personal information appears.
- **Must not decide:** The truth of a claim — only where the evidence comes from.

### 3. CLEAR brief workspace
- **Purpose:** Assemble CLEAR pillar findings into a human-reviewed brief.
- **Evidence handled:** Findings mapped to Continuity, Legitimacy, Evidence, Accountability,
  Readiness.
- **Human owner:** Accountable function relevant to the question.
- **Must not decide:** The institutional decision itself.

### 4. Risk-tier and review-routing panel
- **Purpose:** Record the CLEAR risk tier and proposed review path for human confirmation.
- **Evidence handled:** Risk-tier classification and proposed routing.
- **Human owner:** Service owner and executive sponsor for higher tiers.
- **Must not decide:** The risk classification or required reviewers without accountable human confirmation.

### 5. Accountable-function map
- **Purpose:** Show which functions own which aspects of the question.
- **Evidence handled:** Function ownership and involvement records.
- **Human owner:** Executive sponsor.
- **Must not decide:** Any function's determination within its own domain.

### 6. Decision-context log
- **Purpose:** Preserve the rationale and context around a decision for later review.
- **Evidence handled:** Decision records, rationale, approvals.
- **Human owner:** Program owner.
- **Must not decide:** Whether the decision was correct.

### 7. Continuity memory map
- **Purpose:** Show where institutional knowledge is concentrated and at risk.
- **Evidence handled:** Knowledge-concentration and succession signals.
- **Human owner:** HR / workforce planning with the service owner.
- **Must not decide:** Staffing or employment actions.

### 8. Implementation traceability view
- **Purpose:** Trace a commitment from authority through operation to review.
- **Evidence handled:** Traceability links across policy, operation, and review.
- **Human owner:** Relevant accountable function (e.g., equity/GBA Plus, accessibility, Official
  Languages).
- **Must not decide:** Whether a commitment is met — only whether it remains traceable.

### 9. Modernization readiness view
- **Purpose:** Show whether a change is safe and reviewable before it becomes the default.
- **Evidence handled:** Readiness findings and automated-decision trigger outcomes.
- **Human owner:** CIO / digital lead with the service owner.
- **Must not decide:** Whether to approve an automated-decision system.

### 10. Human-review risk register
- **Purpose:** Track surfaced signals that warrant human attention.
- **Evidence handled:** Risk entries with tier, confidence, owner, and next evidence need.
- **Human owner:** Executive sponsor.
- **Must not decide:** The resolution — only that a human must consider it.

### 11. Assurance package export
- **Purpose:** Produce a clean, human-reviewed evidence package for a decision or audience.
- **Evidence handled:** Compiled findings, confidence, provenance, review record.
- **Human owner:** Accountable function requesting the package.
- **Must not decide:** The outcome the package informs.

### 12. Follow-up evidence tracker
- **Purpose:** Track the specific evidence that would raise confidence next.
- **Evidence handled:** Open evidence gaps and their owners.
- **Human owner:** Service owner.
- **Must not decide:** Whether the gap is acceptable — that is a human judgment.

## 7. Evidence object model

SAGE would organize a small set of evidence objects. Each is defined by what it represents, its
minimum fields, and its sensitivity considerations. The **minimum field set** shared across objects
is: source, owner, date, provenance, confidence, sensitivity, related CLEAR pillar, risk tier, and
human-review status.

| Object | What it represents | Object-specific minimum fields | Sensitivity considerations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **InstitutionalQuestion** | The specific question being examined. | question text, scope, exclusions, owner, risk tier | May imply a program's vulnerabilities; access-controlled. |
| **EvidenceSource** | A place evidence lives. | source, owner, provenance, sensitivity | May point to systems holding personal information. |
| **EvidenceItem** | A single piece of evidence. | source, date, provenance, confidence, sensitivity, related CLEAR pillar | May contain personal or protected information; minimize and reference. |
| **EvidenceSourceRegister** | The catalogue of sources for a question. | list of sources, owners, provenance, sensitivity | Aggregate view; access by need-to-know. |
| **CLEARFinding** | A finding against a CLEAR pillar. | related CLEAR pillar, confidence, owner, date, human-review status | May characterize a practice; never a verdict on people. |
| **ConfidenceRating** | The confidence attached to a finding. | level, rubric dimensions, provenance | Must state uncertainty honestly. |
| **RiskTier** | The CLEAR risk level of a question. | tier, rationale, owner | Higher tiers imply tighter review and access. |
| **AccountableFunction** | A function that owns part of the question. | function, owner, involvement date | Routing metadata; low sensitivity. |
| **ReviewFlag** | A trigger for a required review. | flag type, function, date, status | May indicate legal, privacy, or automated-decision exposure. |
| **DecisionContext** | The rationale/context of a decision. | decision, rationale, approvals, date, owner | May be subject to access/privacy obligations. |
| **ContinuityRisk** | A knowledge/continuity vulnerability. | risk, concentration signal, owner, confidence | May implicate individuals; aggregate and minimize. |
| **TraceabilityLink** | A link from authority to operation to review. | from, to, relationship, owner | Low sensitivity; reference-based. |
| **HumanReviewRecord** | Evidence that a human reviewed the material. | reviewer, function, date, disposition | Establishes accountability; retain per records rules. |
| **AssurancePackage** | A compiled, reviewed evidence package. | question, scope, findings, confidence, risk tier, review record | May aggregate sensitive material; export-controlled. |
| **NextEvidenceNeed** | The evidence that would raise confidence. | gap, owner, target, priority | Low sensitivity unless it names protected data. |

Sensitivity, provenance, and human-review status are never optional. If an object cannot carry its
provenance and confidence, it does not belong in a SAGE workspace.

SAGE should avoid storing authoritative copies where a source-system reference is sufficient. The
preferred pattern is reference, link, and provenance first; copy only when necessary and controlled.

### Evidence object lifecycle

SAGE evidence objects should follow a controlled lifecycle:

1. **Draft** — a question, source, or evidence item has been identified but not reviewed.
2. **Registered** — provenance, owner, sensitivity, and source-system reference have been recorded.
3. **Mapped** — the object has been mapped to a CLEAR pillar, risk tier, or review flag.
4. **Reviewed** — an accountable human or function has reviewed the object.
5. **Exported** — the object has been included in an assurance package or reviewed output.
6. **Closed / archived** — the object has been retained, archived, or disposed of according to the
   institution's own records rules.

SAGE does not determine official record status, retention, disposition, or disclosure. Those remain
with the institution's records, privacy, ATIP, legal, and program authorities.

## 8. CLEAR-to-SAGE workflow

1. **Define the institutional question.**
2. **Open the SAGE workspace** for that question.
3. **Register evidence sources** in the evidence source register.
4. **Map evidence to CLEAR pillars** (Continuity, Legitimacy, Evidence, Accountability, Readiness).
5. **Apply the evidence confidence rubric** (provenance, durability, corroboration, recency,
   completeness, decision relevance, sensitivity).
6. **Record the risk tier** applied through the CLEAR method; risk tiering is confirmed by accountable
   humans.
7. **Record proposed review routing** to accountable functions; accountable humans confirm the
   required review path.
8. **Produce the CLEAR brief.**
9. **Record human review.**
10. **Export the assurance package.**
11. **Track next evidence needed.**

SAGE does not produce the conclusion. It preserves the review path — the evidence, its provenance and
confidence, the routing, and the human-review record — so accountable people can decide and so the
decision can be explained later.

---

## 9. Human-review and accountable-function routing

SAGE records proposed review routing and evidence packages for accountable-function review.
Accountable humans confirm the required review path. SAGE uses the CLEAR role map. For each function:
when SAGE would propose routing evidence to them, what they own, and what SAGE never decides for them.

| Accountable function | When SAGE proposes routing to them | What they own | What SAGE does not decide for them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Program owner** | Continuity, evidence, or accountability of the program is in question. | The program's mandate, outcomes, and authority. | Whether the program meets its mandate. |
| **Service owner** | Service logic, ownership, or continuity is under pressure. | Design and delivery of the service. | How the service should be delivered. |
| **CIO / digital lead** | A modernization or automation change is in scope. | Information, data, IT, and cyber management. | Technology and digital management decisions. |
| **Privacy / ATIP** | Personal information or records/access questions appear. | Privacy practices, PIAs, access obligations. | Any privacy or disclosure determination. |
| **Legal** | Authority, rights, or legal exposure is implicated. | Legal risk and interpretation. | Legal advice or conclusions. |
| **Internal audit / evaluation** | Evidence or accountability gaps are surfaced. | Independent assurance and evaluation. | Audit findings or assurance opinions. |
| **Equity / GBA Plus / accessibility / Official Languages** | A change may affect equity, accessibility, or Official Languages obligations. | Their respective inclusion and obligation domains. | Any equity, accessibility, or Official Languages determination. |
| **HR / workforce planning** | Workforce transition or knowledge concentration is in question. | Workforce, staffing, and knowledge transfer. | HR, staffing, or employment decisions. |
| **Executive sponsor** | Level 3–4 questions and any high-trust change. | Overall accountability for the decision. | The decision itself. |

## 10. Canadian public-sector assurance requirements

SAGE supports evidence organization for Canadian public-sector obligations. It does not certify
compliance or replace the institution's review processes. Government of Canada instruments serve as a
recognizable federal baseline; in provincial, territorial, municipal, Crown, or broader public-sector
contexts, SAGE would map to the applicable local requirements.

| Requirement area | How SAGE supports it | What SAGE does not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Service and digital management** | Organizes service-continuity and decision-context evidence. | Manage services or make digital decisions. |
| **Information and data governance** | Keeps provenance and references to authoritative data. | Become a system of record. |
| **Cyber / security classification** | Flags sensitivity and supports access segmentation. | Set or enforce security policy. |
| **Privacy and ATIP** | Minimizes personal information; flags where review may be needed. | Make privacy or disclosure determinations. |
| **Records and retention** | Distinguishes working material from reviewed outputs; supports exportable records. | Replace records authorities or retention schedules. |
| **Accessibility** | Tracks accessibility commitments and barrier-mitigation evidence. | Certify accessibility conformance. |
| **Official Languages** | Tracks whether Official Languages obligations remain visible through change. | Make Official Languages determinations. |
| **GBA Plus** | Keeps GBA Plus considerations visible and traceable. | Perform the GBA Plus analysis. |
| **Automated-decision review** | Flags when automated-decision review may be required. | Approve automated-decision systems. |
| **Auditability** | Preserves provenance, confidence, and human-review records. | Issue audit findings. |
| **Values and ethics** | Organizes evidence supporting transparency and public confidence. | Adjudicate conduct. |
| **Program authority** | Routes questions to the accountable program authority. | Exercise program authority. |
| **Departmental accountability** | Keeps the conclusion with the accountable authority. | Assume accountability. |

## 11. Privacy, ATIP, records, and evidence control

- **Use existing institutional evidence where possible** rather than collecting anew.
- **Minimize personal information** — gather only what the question requires.
- **Avoid uncontrolled side records** — no shadow inbox or spreadsheet evidence stores.
- **Distinguish working material from reviewed outputs** — drafts are not conclusions.
- **Maintain evidence provenance** — every item traces to its source.
- **Preserve source-system authority** — reference and link rather than duplicate.
- **Support exportable records** so evidence can move cleanly into official processes.
- **Respect retention and disposition rules** for anything SAGE holds.
- **Flag sensitivity and access controls** on every object that needs them.

SAGE does not determine retention, disposition, disclosure, or official record status. It supports
controlled evidence handling and export, while records authorities and institutional processes remain
authoritative.

SAGE's default posture is minimization and control. It recognizes that anything it holds may be
subject to institutional access and privacy obligations.

---

## 12. Responsible modernization and automated-decision boundaries

> **Trigger:** If a service change involves automated support for decisions affecting rights,
> interests, benefits, access, eligibility, recourse, or service outcomes, SAGE must flag the need for
> applicable automated-decision, privacy, legal, security, accessibility, Official Languages, policy,
> and program-authority review.

To be unambiguous:

- SAGE does not perform automated decisions.
- SAGE does not approve automated-decision systems.
- SAGE does not replace an Algorithmic Impact Assessment or departmental review.

SAGE helps surface when those processes should be engaged and helps organize the evidence they will
rely on — nothing more.

## 13. Role and access model

This describes a future access posture, not an implementation. No access-control code is defined here.

- **Least privilege** — access limited to what a role requires.
- **Role-based access** — permissions follow accountable-function roles.
- **Need-to-know** — sensitive evidence is not broadly visible.
- **Sensitive evidence segmentation** — protected material is compartmentalized.
- **Audit logs** — access and changes are recorded.
- **Reviewer separation where needed** — independence preserved for review functions.
- **Read-only source references where possible** — reference over duplication.
- **Export controls** — packages leave under controlled, recorded conditions.

## 15. Integration posture

SAGE should integrate with, not replace, the systems institutions already rely on:

- Microsoft 365 / SharePoint / Teams
- GCdocs or departmental records systems where applicable
- ServiceNow or service-management tools
- case and workflow systems
- audit and evaluation repositories
- HR and workforce systems
- data catalogues
- identity and access systems

The integration rule is simple: **read / reference / link first; copy only when necessary; the source
system remains authoritative.**

## 16. Future-state roadmap

SAGE is future-facing. Any evolution would be staged and deliberate:

- **Stage 0 — Method-only:** CIVIC + CLEAR docs and manual, human-written briefs. *(Where things
  stand today.)*
- **Stage 1 — Template workspace:** structured documents and evidence registers.
- **Stage 2 — Controlled workspace:** role-based evidence packages and review routing.
- **Stage 3 — Integrated workspace:** source-system references and controlled exports.
- **Stage 4 — Institutional assurance environment:** a repeatable, auditable, bilingual, and
  accessible workspace for continuity and governance evidence.

Nothing beyond Stage 0 exists today, and no stage changes the boundaries in Section 17.

---

## 17. Non-negotiable boundaries

- No automated legal, HR, equity, accessibility, Official Languages, privacy, ATIP, audit, or program
  decisions.
- No findings of racism, discrimination, wrongdoing, or misconduct.
- No compliance certification.
- No scoring of people, groups, programs, or institutions.
- No replacement of departmental systems of record.
- No shadow governance.
- No procurement or availability claims.
- No suggestion that SAGE exists or is available today.

SAGE is a future workspace architecture that would make CLEAR repeatable at institutional scale while
keeping human review authoritative and departmental authority intact. That restraint is the point.
