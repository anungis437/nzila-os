# Public-Institution Adaptation Framework

> Canonical framework for tailoring CIVIC, CLEAR, and SAGE across different public-institution types
> without changing the method, weakening the boundaries, or turning the message into a product pitch.

## 1. Purpose

CIVIC, CLEAR, and SAGE were shaped first against the reality of federal departments. That is a
strong baseline, but the public-institution surface is far wider — Crown corporations, regulators,
tribunals, municipalities, public broadcasters, health and education institutions, utilities, and
more. Each carries its own mandate, governance, independence, and sensitivity profile.

This framework exists to keep the CIVIC / CLEAR / SAGE hierarchy intact while allowing
**institution-specific adaptation** of language, role mapping, boundaries, and first-contact posture.
It prevents the public surface from being accidentally overfit to federal departments, and it keeps
every adaptation inside the same guardrails the canonical method already establishes.

## 2. Core principle

**Adapt the framing; never change the method or weaken the boundaries.**

- The method (CLEAR) and the future architecture (SAGE) do not change by institution type.
- What changes is language, role names, the first safe wedge, and the specific red lines that apply.
- Human review stays authoritative everywhere. The institution always owns the conclusion.
- No adaptation ever introduces scoring, automated findings, compliance certification, procurement
  language, or a claim that SAGE is available today.

CBC/Radio-Canada is used as the stress test because it exposes the widest set of blindspots (Crown
corporation and public-broadcaster language, editorial independence, public trust, accessibility
implementation traceability, Official Languages, Indigenous and EDI sensitivity, ATIP/records nuance,
procurement/vendor sensitivity, and arm's-length governance). It hardens the adaptation model — it
does **not** make the public surface CBC-specific.

## 3. Public-institution surface

The surface includes, but is not limited to:

- federal departments
- provincial and territorial ministries
- Crown corporations
- agencies, boards, and commissions
- Officers of Parliament and independent oversight bodies
- central agencies and treasury-board-type bodies
- regulators
- tribunals and ombuds offices
- courts and judicial-administration bodies
- police services and law-enforcement bodies
- corrections, parole, and detention institutions
- child welfare and youth-protection bodies
- social benefits and income-support administrators
- elections and democratic institutions
- municipalities and regional authorities
- public broadcasters and cultural institutions
- archives, libraries, museums, and heritage institutions
- public health and health-system institutions
- education-sector public institutions
- public safety and emergency-management institutions
- public utilities and infrastructure authorities
- port, airport, transit, and transportation authorities
- statistical and data agencies
- service-delivery partners operating under public mandate
- Indigenous governments, institutions, and service organizations — only where relationship-led,
  jurisdictionally appropriate, and protocol-respecting

These are not one generic "government" audience. The default noun is **public institution**; specific
nouns apply per type (see Section 5).

| **Elections / democratic institution** | An elections body or democratic institution | Continuity of institutional memory and readiness | CLEAR excluding electoral administration judgment | Future workspace, electoral systems excluded | Electoral integrity and independence | Administrative continuity + records traceability | voter eligibility, counting, boundaries, compliance |

| **Indigenous government / institution / service organization** | A First Nations, Inuit, or Métis government, institution, or service organization | Only relationship-led, jurisdictionally appropriate, protocol-respecting engagement | Defer to community data governance and authorities | Defer entirely to community authority and protocols | Sovereignty, jurisdiction, data governance, protocol | None assumed — relationship and invitation first | external governance, imposed evidence models, extractive framing |

**Indigenous governments, institutions, and service organizations** are not ordinary public-sector
targets. Engagement is only relationship-led, jurisdictionally appropriate, and protocol-respecting.
Defer to the relevant nation, community, organization, legal authority, data-governance expectations,
and consultation protocols. Do not initiate outreach as if to a standard institution.

## 5. Language rules

Default and specific nouns:

- Use **"public institution"** as the broad default.
- Use **"department"** only when the institution is actually a department.
- Use **"ministry"** only for provincial/territorial contexts.
- Use **"Crown corporation"** when applicable.
- Use **"public broadcaster"** or **"public media institution"** for CBC-like institutions.
- Use **"mandate-driven institution"** when independence, arm's-length governance, or public trust
  matters.
- Use **"service-delivery partner"** only where a non-departmental body delivers public services
  under public mandate.

Avoid broad misuse of (acceptable only inside explicit no-go language, or where technically
unavoidable and justified):

- government department
- departmental authority
- program authority
- bureaucracy
- client
- customer
- vendor
- platform
- software
- demo
- pilot
- pricing
- SOW
- procurement

Canonical CLEAR/SAGE docs may retain "department", "departmental authority", and "program authority"
where they are the federal baseline being described. Public-facing CIVIC copy should default to
"public institution" and add a specific noun only when the audience is known.

**On "client":** "client" may be acceptable only where it is the institution's own service terminology
or appears in a cited policy context (for example, the Directive on Automated Decision-Making refers
to decisions affecting clients). Do not use it as commercial customer language.

## 6. Role-mapping rules

CLEAR and SAGE role names adapt by institution type. The underlying accountable-function logic is
unchanged; only the labels shift.

**Federal department:**
- deputy head / executive sponsor
- program owner
- service owner
- CIO / digital lead
- privacy / ATIP
- legal
- internal audit / evaluation
- accessibility / Official Languages / GBA Plus
- HR / workforce planning

**Crown corporation:**
- executive sponsor
- mandate / corporate strategy owner
- service or operating owner
- digital / information management lead
- privacy / access / records
- legal
- internal audit / risk
- accessibility / Official Languages / EDI
- people and culture

**Public broadcaster:**
- corporate strategy / public value owner
- accessibility / inclusive design
- digital / service / technology stewardship lead
- information management / records
- privacy / access
- legal
- enterprise risk / audit
- people and culture
- Indigenous strategy / EDI functions, where authorized
- **explicit exclusion:** newsroom / editorial / content / programming decision authority

For other institution types, map to the nearest equivalent accountable functions, always adding the
institution's independence-bearing function (adjudicators, regulators, clinicians, educators) to the
**exclusion** list rather than the routing list.

## 7. Boundary rules by institution type

Institution-specific red lines. These are in addition to the universal boundaries (human review
authoritative; no automated legal/HR/equity/clinical/enforcement decisions; no findings of
wrongdoing; no scoring; no compliance certification; the institution owns the conclusion).

| Institution type | Institution-specific red line |
| --- | --- |
| **Public broadcaster** | Do not review, assess, influence, score, govern, or provide evidence judgments on editorial decisions, journalistic judgment, newsroom independence, programming choices, creative decisions, content selection, or ombudsman processes. |
| **Cultural / heritage institution** | Do not influence curatorial judgment, collections governance, acquisition, accession, deaccession, repatriation, restitution, donor-restricted material, archival restriction decisions, cultural-property determinations, Indigenous cultural protocols, or historical interpretation. |
| **Tribunal / ombuds office** | Do not interfere with adjudicative independence, case outcomes, investigations, findings, or reasons. |
| **Regulator** | Do not influence enforcement decisions, licensing outcomes, compliance findings, investigations, or regulated-party treatment. |
| **Public health / health system** | Do not make clinical, eligibility, triage, diagnostic, privacy, consent, or care decisions. No patient-specific review, no health-record extraction, no care-pathway recommendations, no quality-of-care findings, and no substitute for clinical governance. |
| **Education** | Do not make student assessment, discipline, accommodation, child-protection, or employment decisions. No student-risk scoring, no accommodation determinations, no child-safety determinations, no special-education placement decisions, and no parent/guardian rights determinations. |
| **Public safety / emergency management** | Do not make operational, enforcement, or emergency-response decisions. No intelligence, surveillance, threat assessment, dispatch, enforcement prioritization, use-of-force, detention, parole, corrections, or emergency-command decisions. |
| **Public utility / infrastructure** | Do not make operational control, safety, or dispatch decisions. |
| **Crown corporation** | Do not substitute for board governance, corporate accountability, or arm's-length independence. |
| **Agency / board / commission** | Do not substitute for statutory mandate, board governance, delegated authority, appointments, investigation powers, licensing powers, or public accountability. |
| **Independent oversight body** | Do not influence investigations, findings, reports, recommendations, protected disclosures, complaint handling, oversight priorities, or independent opinions. |
| **Central agency / treasury-board-type body** | Do not substitute for expenditure authority, policy authority, management-board authority, central-agency challenge function, or public-administration oversight. |
| **Court / judicial-administration body** | Do not influence judicial independence, case outcomes, reasons, judicial assignment, court-administration decisions affecting cases, or access-to-justice determinations. |
| **Police / law-enforcement body** | Do not influence investigations, enforcement priorities, intelligence, surveillance, threat assessment, use-of-force, dispatch, detention, or individual treatment decisions. |
| **Corrections / parole / detention institution** | Do not influence custody, classification, parole, release, discipline, risk assessment, detention, or individual liberty decisions. |
| **Child welfare / youth-protection body** | Do not influence child safety, placement, protection, custody, family reunification, investigation, or individual case decisions. |
| **Social benefits / income-support administrator** | Do not influence eligibility, benefit amount, recourse, enforcement, debt, suspension, or individual entitlement decisions. |
| **Elections / democratic institution** | Do not influence electoral administration, voter eligibility, candidate treatment, compliance investigations, vote counting, electoral boundaries, or democratic-process determinations. |
| **Statistical / data agency** | Do not influence statistical independence, methodology, releases, confidentiality, disclosure control, survey design, or official statistics. |
| **Transportation authority** | Do not influence safety-critical operations, dispatch, incident response, inspection, enforcement, or operational control decisions. |
| **Municipality / regional authority** | Do not substitute for council authority or local democratic accountability. |
| **Indigenous government / institution** | Do not impose external governance, evidence models, data assumptions, or consultation substitutes. |

## 8. Evidence and records posture

The canonical CLEAR/SAGE posture (minimize personal information, preserve provenance, keep
source-systems authoritative, avoid uncontrolled side records, respect retention/disposition) applies
everywhere. Adaptation adds institution-specific carve-outs:

- **Public broadcasters:** avoid journalistic, creative, programming, source-protection, or newsroom
  material unless the institution's own authorized functions explicitly define scope and controls.
  Access/records rights differ where journalistic/creative/programming material is implicated.
- **Cultural / heritage institutions:** avoid restricted collections, archival, donor-restricted,
  culturally sensitive, repatriation/restitution, or Indigenous cultural-protocol material unless the
  institution's authorized functions and applicable communities or rights-holders explicitly define
  scope and controls.
- **Health institutions:** treat health information as highly sensitive; defer to health-privacy and
  consent frameworks; never touch clinical decision content.
- **Tribunals / regulators:** exclude case files, investigation material, and adjudicative reasons.
- **Education:** exclude student records and child-protection material.
- **Indigenous institutions:** defer entirely to community data-governance expectations and protocols.

In all cases SAGE does not determine retention, disposition, disclosure, or official record status.

**Service-delivery partners.** For service-delivery partners, CIVIC/CLEAR must distinguish between the
partner's operational evidence and the public authority's mandate, accountability, funding, statutory,
and policy obligations. The partner may hold operating evidence, but the public authority may still
own the mandate and public accountability.

**Data access and source systems.** First-contact conversations should never imply direct data
access, data extraction, system integration, case-file access, health-record access, student-record
access, investigation-file access, source-material access, or newsroom/editorial material access. The
starting point is always framing, evidence discipline, and human-reviewed traceability — not data
access.

## 9. First-contact posture

Every first contact, regardless of institution type, is:

- feedback-oriented, not a pitch
- not a procurement ask, product introduction, platform demonstration, or pilot proposal
- framed around continuity, institutional memory, accessibility, and responsible modernization
- explicit about the relevant institution-specific boundary before anything else

SAGE is not mentioned in first contact for any institution type. It is introduced only if the
institution asks whether the method could become an operating workspace later.

## 10. When not to proceed

Hold or decline outreach when:

- the only available entry is through an independence-bearing function (newsroom, adjudicator,
  regulator's enforcement arm, clinician, educator's assessment authority);
- the institution is an Indigenous government/institution and there is no relationship-led,
  protocol-respecting invitation;
- the framing cannot avoid procurement, product, scoring, audit, or compliance-certification language;
- the required institution-specific boundary cannot be honoured;
- the contact expects an assessment, audit, score, or automated finding rather than a
  human-reviewed evidence conversation.

**Independence-bearing-function stop rule.** If the first safe wedge cannot be separated from an
independence-bearing function, do not proceed. For example: newsroom/editorial for public
broadcasters, adjudication for tribunals and courts, enforcement for regulators or police, clinical
care for health institutions, student assessment or child protection for education and youth
institutions, and electoral administration for elections bodies.

## 11. Relationship to CIVIC, CLEAR, and SAGE

- **CIVIC** opens the conversation — the public-service continuity front door. Adaptation changes the
  *language and wedge*, not the invitation.
- **CLEAR** defines the evidence discipline. Adaptation changes *role labels and carve-outs*, not the
  method, the confidence rubric, or the boundaries.
- **SAGE** is the future workspace architecture. Adaptation changes *which systems are authoritative
  and which material is excluded*, never its future-facing, non-product status.

This framework is the connective tissue that lets one method serve many mandate-driven public
institutions credibly — including public broadcasters like CBC/Radio-Canada — without becoming
specific to any one of them.

**Internal governance material.** This framework is internal governance material. It may inform public
copy, but it is not itself an outreach asset. Institution-specific readiness briefs are internal
unless deliberately converted into a separate external asset after review.
