# Public-Service Continuity Brief

## Context

Public institutions are being asked to modernize services, adopt AI responsibly, maintain trust, and deliver continuity while workforce structures shift.

## Pressure pattern

- workforce transition, including retirements and attrition
- modernization and automation pressure
- institutional-memory fragility
- accountability and implementation traceability expectations
- equity implementation scrutiny
- fragmented evidence across HR, service operations, audit, complaints, and modernization streams
- public trust dependence on explainable institutional action

## Core continuity risk

The risk is not only personnel turnover.

The risk is that undocumented knowledge, weak ownership, fragmented evidence, and unclear accountability become embedded into redesigned services or automated workflows.

## CIVIC framing

CIVIC by Nzila addresses this continuity problem as a public-service initiative:

- Continuity
- Implementation
- Visibility
- Integrity
- Capacity

## Evidence discipline behind the brief

The CLEAR Method is used as the evidence discipline behind CIVIC to help structure signals into decision-grade public-service briefs for human review.

## Boundaries

- no automated legal advice
- no autonomous HR or equity decisions
- no automated findings of discrimination or wrongdoing
- uncertainty and evidence confidence are reported explicitly
- privacy and minimization remain in scope from the start
