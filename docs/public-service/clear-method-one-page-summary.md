# CLEAR Method — One-Page Summary

*The evidence discipline behind CIVIC by Nzila. This is a method for producing human-reviewed
public-service continuity briefs — not a product, assessment, scorecard, or procurement offer.*

## What CLEAR is

CLEAR is a repeatable evidence discipline for public-service continuity. It gives leaders a
consistent way to ask a hard institutional question, gather what can actually be observed, judge how
strong that evidence is, and produce a human-reviewed brief that states both what is known and what
remains uncertain. It is used to examine institutional memory, governance evidence, responsible
modernization, equity and inclusion implementation, and service continuity — the things that tend to
weaken quietly during workforce transition and digital change. CLEAR organizes evidence for
accountable people; it does not decide for them.

## Five pillars

- **Continuity** — Will critical institutional knowledge and service logic survive change?
- **Legitimacy** — Does the operating practice appear traceable to authority, policy, procedural
  fairness, accessibility, official-language obligations, and review expectations? (CLEAR does not judge whether
  a policy choice itself is legitimate.)
- **Evidence** — Can the institution show its work, not just assert it?
- **Accountability** — Is it clear who owns the decision and how it can be reviewed?
- **Readiness** — Is the institution prepared to modernize — including using AI where appropriate —
  without losing continuity, control, or reviewability?

## Confidence and risk

**Evidence confidence** is labelled on every finding — High, Moderate, Low, or Insufficient —
grounded in seven dimensions (provenance, durability, corroboration, recency, completeness, decision
relevance, sensitivity) and weighted by source quality. Low confidence is not failure; it tells the
institution where to invest in better evidence before deciding.

**Risk tiering** scales the rigour to the stakes:

- **Level 1** — Low institutional sensitivity.
- **Level 2** — Internal operational sensitivity.
- **Level 3** — Public / client impact.
- **Level 4** — Rights, eligibility, equity, privacy, legal, or high-trust impact.

Higher tiers require more evidence, more accountable review, and a defined minimum evidence package.

## Canadian public-sector fit

CLEAR is designed to sit inside federal operating reality. It *supports*, *helps surface*, and
*helps organize evidence for* obligations under the Policy and Directive on Service and Digital, the
Directive on Automated Decision-Making, the Directive on Privacy Practices, the Values and Ethics
Code, GBA Plus, the Accessible Canada Act, Official Languages, and access-to-information / records
expectations — without claiming compliance. When a change involves automated support for decisions
affecting rights, benefits, access, eligibility, recourse, or service outcomes, CLEAR flags the need
for the institution's automated-decision, privacy, legal, and policy review. Government of Canada
instruments are used as a recognizable federal baseline; in provincial, territorial, municipal,
Crown, or broader public-sector contexts, CLEAR is mapped to the applicable local requirements.

## Human-review boundary

Human review is authoritative at every step. CLEAR outputs are decision-grade inputs, never
verdicts. Privacy and data minimization are in scope from the start, and CLEAR minimizes personal
information and avoids creating uncontrolled side records. The department, agency, Crown corporation,
public body, or accountable authority remains responsible for interpreting evidence, applying law and
policy, consulting required functions, and deciding through its own authorities.

## What CLEAR does not replace

CLEAR does not provide legal advice; does not make HR, equity, employment, or disciplinary decisions;
does not produce automated findings of racism, discrimination, wrongdoing, or misconduct; does not
score or rank people or groups; and does not replace internal audit, legal review, privacy / ATIP,
equity, accessibility, Official Languages functions, or executive accountability. It supports human
review by organizing evidence, uncertainty, and traceability.
