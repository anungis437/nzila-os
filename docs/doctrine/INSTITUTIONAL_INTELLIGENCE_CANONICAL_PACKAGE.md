## 1. Category Name

**Institutional Intelligence**

No abbreviation in public-facing materials. Abbreviated form for internal technical use only: II.

## 2. Core Problem Statement

> Organizations are becoming more digitally capable while becoming more institutionally fragile.

This sentence is the canonical opening for all category communications. It must not be paraphrased or replaced in primary public materials.

---

## 3. Category Definition

> Institutional Intelligence is the governed capacity of institutions to remember, decide, adapt, and remain legitimate over time.

### Word-by-word precision

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Governed | Operates under explicit human accountability and stewardship — not autonomous |
| Capacity | An organizational capability that is built, measured, and can erode — not a product |
| Remember | Preserved context: decisions, rationale, obligations, exceptions, and lineage |
| Decide | Consequential choices that are explainable and attributable to accountable human authority |
| Adapt | Change that preserves institutional identity, obligations, and trust relationships |
| Legitimate | Authority recognized and accepted by those who depend on the institution |
| Over time | Durable across transitions, pressure, and scale — not just in steady state |

## 4. Canonical Causal Model

```

  Memory
    ↓
  Continuity
    ↓
  Governance
    ↓
  Trust
    ↓
  Institutional Intelligence
    ↓
  Resilience

```

This sequence is frozen. Governance precedes Trust. Trust is the product of observable, accountable governance — not its precondition.

### Layer definitions

| Layer | Definition |
|---|---|
| Memory | Preserved institutional context: decisions, rationale, obligations, exceptions, and lineage |
| Continuity | Operational coherence through transitions, disruption, and change |
| Governance | Decisions consistent with past commitments, accountable to explicit authority, and explainable to those affected |
| Trust | Operational state: accumulated result of consistent, explainable, accountable behavior — not sentiment |
| Institutional Intelligence | Active, adaptive, governed intelligence of the institution operating as a coherent entity |
| Resilience | Capacity to absorb shocks and continue functioning with integrity |

### Diagnostic use

The model is both a design framework and a failure diagnostic. Institutional failure traces back to a breakdown one or two layers above where it becomes visible.

## 5. The Six Institutional Epochs

| Epoch | Name | Memory Location | Primary Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Oral Institutions | People | Fragile under disruption |
| II | Written Institutions | Records | Interpretation fragmentation |
| III | Bureaucratic Institutions | Roles and procedures | Rigidity and accountability distance |
| IV | Computational Institutions | Systems | Fragmentation and loss of context |
| V | AI-Augmented Institutions | Models and systems | Legitimacy gap |
| VI | Institutional Intelligence | Governed institutional layer | Still being built |

Institutional Intelligence is not the endpoint of history. It is the response to the current operating environment. This framing positions it as evolution, not invention.

## 6. Legitimacy as Load-Bearing Concept

Legitimacy is the outcome the entire model exists to produce and protect.

> Legitimacy is the recognition and acceptance of institutional authority by the people who depend on it.

It is not compliance. It is not reputation. It is not branding.

It is the operational condition of being trusted, credible, and seen as accountable.

Legitimacy cannot be claimed. It can only be earned — through consistent, explainable, accountable behavior over time.

An efficient institution that is not legitimate will lose the cooperation of its members.
An institution that is compliant but not explainable will lose trust under scrutiny.

## 7. Category Boundaries (What It Is Not)

| Category | What it does | What Institutional Intelligence adds |
|---|---|---|
| Artificial Intelligence | Assists decision-making and automates tasks | Accountability, explainability, continuity across time |
| Business Intelligence | Describes operational and financial performance | Governance legitimacy and institutional coherence |
| Knowledge Management | Captures and organizes information | Accountable decision lineage with rationale and attribution |
| Governance Software | Manages controls, approvals, workflows | Memory, trust, continuity, and stewardship integration |
| Workflow Automation | Executes processes | Constitutional integrity through process — speed without accountability loss |

## 10. Canonical Signals of Institutional Fragility

Reference these in assessments, articles, and policy positions.

1. Executive and leadership tenure shortening.
2. Knowledge retirement wave — experienced practitioners leaving without structured transfer.
3. Declining institutional trust across sectors (longitudinal surveys).
4. Governance complexity outpacing governance capacity.
5. AI adoption creating legitimacy gaps without governance design.
