# 1. Purpose and Positioning

## What the ICRA Is

The ICRA is a structured institutional diagnostic that produces a defensible measurement of an organization's exposure to continuity failure, governance fragility, operational memory loss, and trust debt.

It is designed to be administered:

- as a self-guided executive assessment,
- as a facilitated discovery workshop,
- as a procurement-readiness review,
- or as a governance committee instrument.

## What the ICRA Is Not

- It is not a lead capture quiz.
- It is not a productivity benchmark.
- It is not a personality or culture instrument.
- It is not a vendor scoring tool.
- It is not a workforce surveillance mechanism.

## Functional Roles

The ICRA serves five concurrent purposes:

1. **Operational diagnostic** — gives leadership a real measurement of continuity exposure.
2. **Institutional intelligence capture** — over time, generates anonymized benchmark data.
3. **Doctrine embodiment** — every question maps to a named doctrine pillar.
4. **Procurement evidence** — produces documentation suitable for governance review.
5. **Engagement entry point** — establishes a measurement baseline that supports ongoing institutional partnership.

# 2. Assessment Structure

## Nine Diagnostic Sections

| # | Section | Pillar Alignment | Weight |
|---|---------|------------------|--------|
| 1 | Operational Dependency | Continuity | 15% |
| 2 | Governance Visibility | Governance | 12% |
| 3 | Institutional Memory | Continuity, Evidence | 12% |
| 4 | Onboarding Continuity | Continuity | 8% |
| 5 | Transition Readiness | Continuity, Governance | 12% |
| 6 | Operational Traceability | Evidence, Explainability | 12% |
| 7 | Federated Coordination | Federation | 8% |
| 8 | Explainable Governance | Explainability, Trust | 11% |
| 9 | Operational Sovereignty | Sovereignty | 10% |

**Total weighted score:** 0–100.

---

## Section Objectives

1. **Operational Dependency** — measures how much operation depends on specific individuals.
2. **Governance Visibility** — measures whether governance decisions can be observed and reconstructed.
3. **Institutional Memory** — measures preservation of operational knowledge and historical context.
4. **Onboarding Continuity** — measures whether new members can reach operational competency without mentor heroics.
5. **Transition Readiness** — measures organizational ability to absorb leadership and structural change.
6. **Operational Traceability** — measures whether operational activity generates evidence as a byproduct.
7. **Federated Coordination** — measures cross-organizational alignment without centralization risk.
8. **Explainable Governance** — measures whether decisions and AI-assisted recommendations can be explained.
9. **Operational Sovereignty** — measures institutional independence from vendor, platform, and tool lock-in.

# 3. Scoring Methodology

## Question Response Scale

Each question uses a calibrated 0–4 ordinal scale, with descriptive anchors rather than abstract numbers.

| Score | Anchor | Operational Meaning |
|-------|--------|---------------------|
| 0 | Absent | The capability does not exist in any form |
| 1 | Informal | The capability exists only as individual practice |
| 2 | Partial | The capability is documented but inconsistently applied |
| 3 | Structured | The capability is systematized and consistently applied |
| 4 | Institutional | The capability is institutionalized, governed, and resilient |

## Section Scoring

Each section's raw score is computed as:

```
Section Score = (Sum of question scores / Maximum possible) × 100
```

## Composite Score

The composite ICRA score is the weighted average of all section scores using the weights above.

## Maturity Band Assignment

| Composite Score | Band | Continuity Risk Level |
|-----------------|------|----------------------|
| 85–100 | Continuity Intelligence | Low |
| 70–84 | Continuity-Aware Operations | Moderate |
| 50–69 | Structured Governance | Elevated |
| 30–49 | Fragmented Documentation | High |
| 0–29 | Personality Dependent | Critical |

---

## Cross-Cutting Indicators

In addition to the composite score, three cross-cutting indicators are computed:

### Governance Fragility Indicator
Derived from sections 2, 5, 8. High when governance is informal, transition-fragile, or non-explainable.

### Trust Debt Indicator
Derived from sections 3, 6, 8. High when operational ambiguity, traceability gaps, or unexplainable decisions accumulate.

### Operational Memory Indicator
Derived from sections 1, 3, 4. High when knowledge is trapped in individuals and onboarding depends on mentorship rather than system.

Each indicator is reported on the same 0–100 scale with band labels:

| Score | Indicator Band |
|-------|---------------|
| 0–25 | Strong posture |
| 26–50 | Manageable exposure |
| 51–75 | Elevated exposure |
| 76–100 | Critical exposure |

# 4. Assessment Instrument — Full Question Bank

> 32 questions total. All questions use the 0–4 anchored scale described above.

## Section 1 — Operational Dependency (5 questions)

1. **Key person concentration.** If three of your most operationally central people left in the next 90 days, what would continue functioning?
2. **Tribal knowledge prevalence.** What percentage of critical processes exist primarily in individual memory rather than documentation?
3. **Single point of failure mapping.** Has the organization formally identified and documented its single points of failure?
4. **Decision bottlenecks.** How many operationally significant decisions cannot be made without one specific person?
5. **Cross-coverage capability.** Can at least two people independently execute every operationally critical workflow?

## Section 2 — Governance Visibility (4 questions)

6. **Decision documentation.** What percentage of significant governance decisions have documented rationale accessible to authorized members?
7. **Approval traceability.** Can approvals be reconstructed historically without contacting the original approver?
8. **Escalation consistency.** Are escalation outcomes consistent across teams for equivalent situations?
9. **Governance event visibility.** Can governance committee or leadership review historical decisions on demand?

## Section 3 — Institutional Memory (4 questions)

10. **Historical reconstructability.** Can the organization reconstruct why a major decision was made three years ago?
11. **Knowledge preservation through departures.** When a long-tenured member departs, how much institutional knowledge is preserved?
12. **Operational rationale accessibility.** Is the reasoning behind current procedures accessible to current operators?
13. **Historical pattern recognition.** Can the organization identify recurring operational patterns from its history?

## Section 4 — Onboarding Continuity (3 questions)

14. **Time to operational competency.** How long does it take a new coordinator or steward to reach independent operational competency?
15. **Mentorship dependency.** How dependent is onboarding on the availability of specific mentors?
16. **Onboarding documentation completeness.** Does onboarding documentation cover not only what to do but why it is done that way?

## Section 5 — Transition Readiness (3 questions)

17. **Leadership transition history.** When the most recent leadership transition occurred, how disruptive was it to operations?
18. **Succession documentation.** Does each leadership role have documented succession context and operational handoff materials?
19. **Restructuring resilience.** When organizational restructuring occurs, is operational continuity actively preserved?

## Section 6 — Operational Traceability (4 questions)

20. **Activity-to-record ratio.** What percentage of operationally significant activity produces a traceable record?
21. **Audit readiness without preparation.** Could the organization pass an unannounced audit with confidence today?
22. **Record integrity confidence.** How confident are you that operational records are accurate, complete, and tamper-resistant?
23. **Lineage of significant decisions.** For any significant operational decision, can the full decision lineage be reconstructed?

## Section 7 — Federated Coordination (3 questions)

24. **Cross-organizational visibility.** In federated structures (chapters, locals, divisions, affiliates), is operational visibility available without centralizing data?
25. **Consistency without uniformity.** Can governance standards be applied consistently across federated entities while respecting local autonomy?
26. **Federated continuity.** When a federated entity experiences leadership transition, is operational continuity preserved at both local and federated levels?

## Section 8 — Explainable Governance (3 questions)

27. **Decision explainability.** When a significant decision is made, can the reasoning be explained to affected stakeholders in clear terms?
28. **AI-assisted decision transparency.** If AI or automated systems contribute to operational decisions, are those contributions traceable and explainable?
29. **Replay capability.** Can past governance decisions be replayed for review, learning, or accountability purposes?

## Section 9 — Operational Sovereignty (3 questions)

30. **Data exportability.** Could you export all your operational data, governance records, and institutional history without vendor assistance?
31. **Vendor independence.** If a primary platform vendor became unavailable, how long could operations continue without disruption?
32. **Operational understanding of own systems.** Does the organization independently understand how its own operational systems work?

# 5. Interpretation Logic

## Composite Score Interpretation

The composite score communicates overall institutional continuity posture. It is not a vendor scorecard — it is an institutional self-portrait.

### 85–100: Continuity Intelligence
The organization has institutionalized operational memory, governance is explainable and traceable, and continuity is a system property rather than a personal property. Risk concentration is low. Recommended posture: maintain hygiene, contribute to benchmark.

### 70–84: Continuity-Aware Operations
The organization has structured governance with identifiable fragility zones. Most operational risk is known and managed. Recommended posture: targeted hardening of identified weak sections.

### 50–69: Structured Governance
The organization has documented operations but inconsistent integration. Governance exists but coordination risk is meaningful. Recommended posture: integration program targeting lowest-scoring sections.

### 30–49: Fragmented Documentation
Knowledge is preserved in fragments but not institutionalized. Operations are vulnerable to personnel and structural change. Recommended posture: comprehensive continuity program.

### 0–29: Personality Dependent
Operations depend heavily on specific individuals. Continuity failure is a near-term institutional risk. Recommended posture: immediate governance and continuity intervention.

---

## Indicator Interpretation

- **High Governance Fragility** → focus on Sections 2, 5, 8.
- **High Trust Debt** → focus on Sections 3, 6, 8.
- **High Operational Memory exposure** → focus on Sections 1, 3, 4.

## Section-Level Findings

Each section produces a finding statement that combines:

- the score band,
- the two highest-risk responses in that section,
- the institutional implication,
- the recommended next action.

# 6. Assessment UX Flow

## Landing Page Structure

The landing page is structured as institutional content, not marketing copy.

### Above-the-Fold Elements
- Title: *Institutional Continuity Risk Assessment*
- Subtitle: *A structured diagnostic for organizations that take governance, continuity, and operational trust seriously.*
- Three-line description: what it measures, who it is for, what the output is.
- Single call to action: *Begin Assessment*.
- Procurement-safe credibility band: time required (15–25 minutes), structured methodology reference, anonymization commitment.

### Below-the-Fold Elements
- *What This Assessment Measures* — nine sections summarized in one line each.
- *Who This Is For* — labor organizations, healthcare institutions, public sector entities, federated associations, governance-heavy enterprises.
- *Methodology* — link to the doctrine architecture and scoring rubric.
- *Sovereignty and Privacy* — explicit statement about data handling, anonymization, and the participant's right to delete their submission.
- *Sample Output* — link to a sample executive summary.

### What the Landing Page Does Not Do
- No countdown timers.
- No "limited spots" framing.
- No gamification.
- No social proof carousels.
- No email-gating before the assessment is visible.

---

## Intake Flow

### Step 1 — Context Capture
- Organization type (labor / healthcare / public sector / federated association / enterprise / other).
- Approximate organization size band (under 50 / 50–500 / 500–5,000 / 5,000+).
- Role of respondent (executive leadership / operational leadership / governance committee / coordinator / other).

### Step 2 — Confidentiality Confirmation
- Clear statement: responses are confidential.
- Clear statement: aggregated, anonymized data may inform institutional benchmarks; individual responses are never published.
- Explicit consent checkbox.
- Right to receive the report without inclusion in benchmark dataset.

### Step 3 — Assessment Sections
- Sections are presented sequentially, not all at once.
- Each section begins with a one-paragraph orientation explaining what the section measures.
- Each question shows its 0–4 scale with anchor descriptions visible.
- A *not applicable* option exists for each question, with brief justification field.
- Saved progress is preserved automatically; respondents can pause and resume.

### Step 4 — Reflective Capture (Optional)
- At the end of each section, an optional open-text field: *Is there context about this section that the score does not capture?*
- These responses inform the report but are not scored.

### Step 5 — Report Delivery Preferences
- Delivery method (in-browser, downloadable PDF, both).
- Optional: organizational contact for follow-up discussion (never required).

---

## Scoring Experience

After submission:

1. A short calibration note: *Your responses are being analyzed against the Nzila Institutional Continuity framework.*
2. Composite score is displayed alongside the band label and a one-paragraph institutional interpretation.
3. The three cross-cutting indicators are displayed with their interpretive bands.
4. Section-level findings are presented in order of risk severity, not in section order.
5. Recommended next steps are presented as institutional actions, not vendor calls to action.

---

## CTA Progression

The CTA structure escalates in commitment level, never in urgency.

| Stage | CTA | Purpose |
|-------|-----|---------|
| Report delivered | Download full report | Standard delivery |
| 48 hours later | Optional: receive the Continuity Practitioner Briefing | Doctrine education |
| 14 days later | Optional: book a structured diagnostic review | Facilitated assessment |
| Quarterly | Reassessment invitation | Longitudinal benchmark contribution |

Every CTA is opt-in. No CTA implies scarcity, urgency, or social pressure.

---

## Follow-Up Nurture Logic

- **Cadence:** never more frequent than every 14 days.
- **Content discipline:** doctrine-grade or assessment-related only. No promotional content.
- **Opt-out:** single-click, no friction, no confirmation flow.
- **Reassessment:** the system tracks reassessment cadence and offers an optional annual reassessment to participants who have not opted out.

# 7. Output Report Architecture

The output report is the primary deliverable. It is structured as an institutional document.

## Report Sections

1. **Cover Sheet** — organization type, assessment date, respondent role, composite score, maturity band.
2. **Executive Summary** — 1 page. Composite finding, three highest-risk areas, recommended institutional posture.
3. **Composite Score Interpretation** — what the score means, what it does not mean, calibration against the maturity band.
4. **Cross-Cutting Indicator Analysis** — governance fragility, trust debt, operational memory.
5. **Section-by-Section Findings** — score, band, two highest-risk responses, institutional implication.
6. **Recommended Next Steps** — structured by priority, not by upsell logic.
7. **Methodology Appendix** — scoring methodology, question bank, weighting logic, doctrine alignment.
8. **Sovereignty and Data Handling Statement** — what was captured, how it is stored, how to request deletion.

## Report Tone

Reads like a governance review document. Avoids exclamation, urgency, and emotional language. Uses institutional vocabulary throughout.

# 8. Sample Executive Summary

> *Illustrative — a real assessment output will reflect actual responses.*

---

**Institutional Continuity Risk Assessment — Executive Summary**

**Organization Type:** Labor Organization
**Approximate Size:** 5,000–25,000 members
**Composite ICRA Score:** 47 / 100
**Maturity Band:** Fragmented Documentation
**Continuity Risk Level:** High

---

### Headline Finding

This organization has invested meaningfully in operational documentation but has not yet integrated that documentation into a continuity infrastructure. Knowledge is preserved in fragments — wiki pages, shared drives, individual files — but is not connected to governance, decision lineage, or institutional memory in a way that supports continuity under transition pressure.

The composite score of 47 places this organization in the *Fragmented Documentation* band, indicating that the foundations of operational discipline are present but the institutional integration that converts documentation into resilience has not yet been established.

### Cross-Cutting Indicators

- **Governance Fragility:** 64 (Elevated exposure). Governance decisions are made consistently in practice, but the rationale and lineage of those decisions is not systematically preserved.
- **Trust Debt:** 58 (Elevated exposure). Operational records exist but record integrity and reconstructability are inconsistent.
- **Operational Memory:** 71 (Elevated exposure). Significant institutional knowledge remains in individual memory, particularly among long-tenured stewards and coordinators.

### Three Highest-Risk Areas

1. **Onboarding Continuity (Section 4, score 38).** New coordinators require extended mentorship from specific individuals to reach competency. This creates compounding fragility as those individuals approach transition.
2. **Operational Traceability (Section 6, score 42).** Most operational activity does not produce a traceable record. Audit readiness depends on preparation rather than continuous evidence.
3. **Operational Dependency (Section 1, score 44).** A small number of individuals hold disproportionate operational knowledge, with no documented succession context for their roles.

### Institutional Implication

The organization is currently structured such that operational continuity depends materially on the continued presence of specific individuals. This is a normal condition for institutions at this maturity stage, but it is not a sustainable one. The next leadership transition, key staff departure, or restructuring event will create measurable continuity strain.

### Recommended Institutional Posture

A structured continuity program with three sequenced priorities:

1. **Operational Memory Capture** — systematic capture of institutional knowledge held by the most operationally central individuals, conducted as institutional hygiene rather than crisis response.
2. **Onboarding Systematization** — conversion of mentorship-dependent onboarding into a system-driven onboarding architecture.
3. **Traceability Foundation** — introduction of operational traceability infrastructure that generates evidence as a byproduct of normal operations.

Sequenced because operational memory capture establishes the substance, onboarding systematization establishes the transfer mechanism, and traceability infrastructure establishes the continuity backbone.

### Recommended Reassessment

Reassessment in 12 months. Organizations in the *Fragmented Documentation* band that implement a structured continuity program typically advance to *Structured Governance* (50–69) within 12–18 months.

# 9. Benchmark Readiness

## Benchmark Architecture

All anonymized ICRA submissions contribute to an evolving institutional continuity benchmark, structured by:

- organization type,
- organization size band,
- composite score distribution,
- section-level score distributions,
- indicator distributions,
- longitudinal progression for reassessing organizations.

## Benchmark Publication Cadence

- **Quarterly:** internal benchmark updates available to assessment participants.
- **Annually:** *State of Institutional Continuity* report, published publicly, drawing on benchmark data with full anonymization.

## Benchmark Integrity Standards

- Minimum sample size before any benchmark figure is published: 25 organizations within the segment.
- All published figures are aggregated; no individual organization is identifiable.
- Participants retain the right to withdraw their data from the benchmark dataset at any time.
- Benchmark methodology is documented and version-controlled.

# 10. Governance Constraints

The ICRA itself operates under the Nzila doctrine.

- **Sovereignty.** Participants own their responses and their report. They may request full deletion at any time, with deletion completed within 30 days.
- **Anti-surveillance.** The ICRA captures organizational posture, never individual behavior. It is not a workforce analytics tool.
- **Explainability.** Every score is traceable to specific responses through specific weights. Nothing is computed opaquely.
- **Anti-bureaucratic discipline.** The assessment is designed to take 15–25 minutes. It is not a 200-question survey.
- **Proportionality.** Recommendations scale to the institutional context — a 200-person nonprofit and a 50,000-member union receive recommendations calibrated to their reality.
- **Humility.** The ICRA produces a finding, not a verdict. Institutional context that the score cannot capture is acknowledged in every report.
