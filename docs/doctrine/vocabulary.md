# Vocabulary Intensity Ladder

Use the appropriate vocabulary level for the audience and context. Never lead with Level 3–4 unless the audience has already established alignment at a lower level.

| Level | Name | Used For |
|-------|------|---------|
| 1 | First Contact | Conferences, introductions, initial interactions |
| 2 | Procurement & Executive | Procurement, executive briefings, board discussions |
| 3 | Doctrine | Whitepapers, strategic narratives, category definition |
| 4 | Visionary | Long-term positioning, category creation (use sparingly) |

### Level 1 Examples
operational continuity, knowledge preservation, governance clarity, organizational resilience

### Level 2 Examples
institutional continuity, operational governance, evidence-native operations, trust infrastructure

### Level 3 Examples
continuity intelligence, sovereign federation, operational memory, governance replay

### Level 4 Examples
institutional continuity infrastructure, governed operational intelligence, sovereign operational systems

# Forbidden / Avoided Language

The following language should generally be avoided unless contextually necessary:

| Avoid | Prefer Instead |
|-------|---------------|
| tenant | organization |
| users | members, stewards, coordinators, staff, operators |
| AI magic | explainable intelligence |
| automation for automation's sake | (avoid entirely) |
| disruption / disruptive | (avoid startup framing) |
| move fast and break things | (incompatible with doctrine) |
| surveillance framing | (incompatible with sovereignty doctrine) |
| black-box | (use: opaque; then explain why that's a risk) |

Nzila language must remain: operationally serious, institutionally credible, governance-aware, and procurement-safe.

# Canonical Term Definitions

## Institutional Continuity

The ability for an organization to preserve operational capability, knowledge, governance integrity, and execution consistency across time, personnel transitions, restructuring, and organizational change.

**Pillar mapping:** Core (spans all 7 pillars)

---

## Operational Memory

The preserved intelligence of how an organization actually functions.

Includes: procedures, precedents, workflows, governance decisions, historical rationale, organizational relationships, and operational context.

**Pillar mapping:** Continuity, Evidence

---

## Continuity Intelligence

Systems and processes that preserve, structure, surface, govern, and operationalize institutional memory.

**Pillar mapping:** Continuity, Governance, Explainability

---

## Evidence-Native Architecture

An operational architecture where traceability, lineage, governance, and operational evidence are generated as a natural byproduct of system activity.

**Pillar mapping:** Evidence, Explainability, Trust

---

## Governance Replay

The ability to replay, inspect, validate, and audit operational and governance decisions.

**Pillar mapping:** Explainability, Evidence, Governance

---

## Sovereign Federation

A governance model where federated entities retain local operational autonomy while benefiting from shared infrastructure, policy alignment, visibility, and continuity systems.

**Pillar mapping:** Sovereignty, Federation

---

## Operational Drift

The gradual divergence between documented operations, intended governance, and actual institutional behavior.

**Pillar mapping:** Continuity, Governance

---

## Trust Infrastructure

Systems designed to preserve trust, reduce ambiguity, improve defensibility, maintain accountability, and increase organizational confidence.

**Pillar mapping:** Trust, Governance, Evidence

---

## Trust Debt

The accumulated institutional cost created by operational ambiguity, governance inconsistency, fragmented systems, untraceable decisions, opaque AI behavior, and institutional memory loss.

Trust debt compounds gradually. Most institutions do not notice it until transition failure, governance crisis, audit pressure, or institutional instability occurs.

**Pillar mapping:** Trust, Governance, Evidence

---

## Governance Fragility

The condition in which an organization becomes increasingly unable to sustain consistent, explainable, and person-independent governance as complexity increases.

**Pillar mapping:** Governance, Continuity

---

## Continuity Collapse

The operational failure state in which an organization loses the ability to maintain normal governance and operational function following personnel transition, restructuring, or institutional change.

**Pillar mapping:** Continuity, Governance

---

## Institutional Continuity Infrastructure

The category name for platforms and systems that help institutions preserve governance, operational memory, and organizational resilience over time — as opposed to platforms that optimize transactions, productivity, or communication.

**Usage:** Level 3–4 vocabulary. Use after alignment is established. Do not open with this term.

---

## Continuity-Aware Operations

An operational mode in which organizational memory is actively institutionalized, processes are documented and governable, and the organization is structured to survive key personnel transitions.

**Pillar mapping:** Continuity, Governance, Operational Memory

---

## Operational Sovereignty

An institution's ability to understand, govern, export, audit, and operationally survive independent of any single vendor, tool, or platform relationship.

**Pillar mapping:** Sovereignty

---

## Doctrine Debt

Used internally. The condition where a platform capability exists but has no traceable connection to a doctrine pillar. Indicates that either the capability needs doctrine justification or the doctrine needs to be extended.

**Usage:** Internal only. Not for external positioning.
