## 1. Closure of the First Collection

The documentation series previously developed under the working designation of the platform doctrine collection — including the authority, stewardship, signal, workspace, and governance doctrine papers, and the material informally cited as "Book VIII" — is hereby **closed as a collection**.

That collection is not withdrawn. It is designated the **Seed Corpus**: the body of practitioner writing, produced against a working national institution, from which the discipline of Institutional Engineering was first recognized as a distinct field. Seed Corpus documents retain their original identifiers, remain citable as historical sources, and shall not be renumbered into the present series. Where a Seed Corpus concept is carried forward, the IEBOK text restates it in the discipline's controlled vocabulary and becomes the citable formulation.

No new documents shall be added to the Seed Corpus.

## 2. The New Series

All subsequent work is organized as the **Institutional Engineering Body of Knowledge (IEBOK)**, structured in six levels:

| Level | Designation | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Foundations | Volume I — Institutional Engineering; Volume II — Institutional Architecture; Volume III — Institutional Systems |
| II | Institutional Sciences | Thirteen first-class sciences, Institutional Mechanics foremost |
| III | Applied Institutional Engineering | Domain implementation guides |
| IV | Executive Handbooks | Role-specific editions |
| V | Technical Standards | Normative specifications (IE-STD series) |
| VI | Reference Implementations | Documented complete implementations |

## 3. Citation Scheme

Citations take the form **IEBOK-{Level}.{Volume or item} §{section}**, for example:

- `IEBOK-I.1 §5.3` — Level I, Volume I, section 5.3
- `IEBOK-II.MECH §12` — Level II, Institutional Mechanics, section 12
- `IE-STD-004 §7.1` — Technical Standard 004, section 7.1

Section numbering is stable within an edition. Cross-edition citations must state the edition (e.g., *IEBOK-I.1 §5.3, 1.0*).

## 4. Versioning and Supersession

Every document in the series carries a semantic version (MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH) and a lifecycle state: *draft → working draft → public review → ballot → approved → published → superseded / withdrawn*. Published editions are immutable; corrections issue as errata, and changes of meaning issue as new editions that formally supersede their predecessors. Superseded editions remain permanently in the record with full provenance.

## 5. Language Conventions

The series uses requirement language in the ISO/IEC sense:

- **shall** — a binding requirement (normative documents only);
- **should** — a recommendation whose rejection requires documented rationale;
- **may** — a permitted option;
- **can** — a statement of possibility or capability.

Levels I–IV and VI are **informative** unless a passage is explicitly marked normative. Level V is **normative** throughout except where marked informative.

## 6. Authorship Standard

The series is written in a precise, neutral, engineering register. Motivational language, consulting idiom, and unnecessary neologism are excluded. A term may be introduced only when it names something no existing term names, and it must be defined in the volume that introduces it and registered in the discipline glossary.

## 7. Provenance Statement

The discipline originates in practice. Its first reference implementation — a national sport federation's institutional platform — predates its literature, and the literature is required to remain accountable to working implementations. No claim in this series should survive that cannot, in principle, be demonstrated in a reference implementation or falsified by one.
