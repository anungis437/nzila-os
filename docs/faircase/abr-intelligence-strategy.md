## 0. The Signal and Why It Matters

An audit of FPSLREB (Federal Public Sector Labour Relations and Employment Board) case data found that **only ~4% of cases carry explicit ABR (Anti-Black Racism) markings**.

This is not a minor calibration issue. It is a structural visibility failure with direct commercial, legal, and governance consequences.

Canada's federal public service employs approximately 300,000 people. Black employees are present across nearly every department, are subject to documented systemic discrimination patterns in hiring, promotion, and discipline, and represent a population with growing legal standing under the *Canadian Human Rights Act*, the *Employment Equity Act*, and emerging Anti-Racism Act frameworks across provinces.

If 4% of cases are explicitly coded as ABR — and all evidence suggests the actual incidence is orders of magnitude higher — then:

1. HR, legal, and labour relations functions are operating on fundamentally incomplete data.
2. Remediation programs are being designed against a distorted baseline.
3. Unions cannot advocate with precision because the case record obscures race.
4. Oversight bodies cannot enforce accountability because the data infrastructure doesn't produce it.
5. Institutions are carrying unreported legal exposure that has not been priced, disclosed, or addressed.

**FairCase's thesis:** The problem is not just unresolved cases. The problem is invisible cases. FairCase makes the invisible visible.

## 1. Interpreting the Signal Responsibly

### 1.1 What the 4% finding is

- A measurement of **explicit ABR labeling** in FPSLREB case records, not a measurement of the actual frequency of race-based workplace harm.
- A proxy for classification failure: the gap between what happens to workers and what gets recorded in institutional systems.
- Consistent with patterns documented by the Black-Led Advisory Committee (BLAC) reports, the Treasury Board Secretariat's GBA+ compliance reviews, and academic research on racialized complaint under-filing in Canada.

### 1.2 What the 4% finding is not

- It is not a claim that "only 4% of federal workers experience ABR." That would be an irresponsible inversion of the finding.
- It is not evidence that ABR is a minor issue in the federal public service. The opposite inference is supported by the data.
- It is not a fabricated statistic for sales purposes. It is a documented audit finding from Canada's principal federal labour adjudication body.

### 1.3 The responsible framing

> "The FPSLREB audit does not tell us that Anti-Black Racism is rare. It tells us that our systems are not built to see it. FairCase is built to see it — and to act on what it finds."

### 1.4 Why under-classification happens (the mechanism, not the accusation)

| Cause | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Complainant self-suppression | Racialized workers fear double retaliation — filing as ABR may trigger more scrutiny than filing as generic "harassment" |
| Intake design | Complaint intake forms at most institutions do not have ABR as a primary classification field |
| Investigator training gap | Most workplace investigators were not trained on ABR-specific factual patterns; they use general discrimination frameworks |
| Adjudicator pattern | Adjudicators apply the legal test available to them; without ABR-coded complaints, ABR-specific remedies are not triggered |
| Institutional incentive | Organizations facing ABR findings face stronger remedy obligations; ambiguous coding reduces institutional accountability pressure |

Understanding this mechanism is essential. It means the classification failure is **structural and systemic**, not merely administrative. FairCase's value proposition addresses the structural cause, not just the symptom.

**Why they buy it:**

### Module 2: Pattern and Exposure Dashboard

**What it does:**
Aggregates case data across an institution to surface ABR patterns that are invisible at the individual case level.

**Key views:**
- Department-level ABR incidence heat map
- Comparator benchmarks (sector peers, tribunal averages)
- Chronology clustering (spikes aligned with leadership changes, policy gaps, organizational events)
- Repeat-actor detection (managers, departments appearing in multiple ABR-pattern cases)
- "Dark matter" score: estimated proportion of ABR-related harm likely not captured in current case data
- Trending: classification rates improving or declining over time

**What the buyer gets:**
A governance dashboard that answers: "Where is the problem? Is it getting better? What are we missing?"

**Why they buy it:**
This is the board report and the audit response. No institution can credibly claim it is addressing ABR without this kind of institutional view.

### Module 3: Investigations Readiness Score

**What it does:**
Evaluates the quality and completeness of active investigations against ABR-specific standards. Flags gaps that create tribunal exposure before investigations close.

**Scored dimensions:**
- Intake quality (protected grounds documented, timeline captured, comparator context gathered)
- Evidence completeness (documents collected, witness statements structured, chain of custody)
- Procedural fairness record (notification, response opportunities, accommodation)
- Timeline compliance (days in each stage vs. SLA and tribunal expectation benchmarks)
- Remediation specification (remedy named, owner assigned, measurement criteria defined)

**What the buyer gets:**
A "readiness score" for every open investigation, with specific gap warnings before files close.

**Why they buy it:**
The most expensive outcome in ABR investigations is not finding the problem — it's closing an investigation improperly and having it reopened (or overturned) at tribunal. This module is tribunal-loss prevention.

## 4. Investigations Workflow Design

The analytics modules above diagnose. The investigations workflow treats.

### 4.1 FairCase Investigations Architecture

```
Intake → Classification → Mandate → Investigation → Closure → Remediation → Verification
   ↑                                                                              ↓
   └─────────────────────── Pattern Signal Feeds Back In ──────────────────────────┘
```

### 4.2 Workflow Stages

**Stage 1: Intake**
- Structured intake form with ABR-specific ground capture (mandatory, not optional)
- Identity vault: complainant identity stored with differential access controls
- Automated classification pre-score generated at intake
- Acknowledgment SLA timer starts

**Stage 2: Classification and Triage**
- Investigator reviews AI classification recommendation
- Override requires documented justification (creates accountability record)
- Priority tier assigned: P1 (immediate risk), P2 (standard track), P3 (monitoring)
- Case team assigned with need-to-know access controls

**Stage 3: Investigation Mandate**
- Investigation scope defined and recorded
- Evidence collection checklist generated (ABR-pattern-specific)
- Timeline documented with SLA milestones
- Complainant and respondent rights disclosure logged

**Stage 4: Active Investigation**
- Chronological evidence log with tamper-evident timestamps
- Role-scoped notes (investigator-only, legal-only, shared)
- Document versioning and chain of custody
- Readiness score surfaced continuously during investigation

**Stage 5: Findings and Closure**
- Dual-control closure: findings require second reviewer confirmation for high-severity ABR cases
- Finding categorization: substantiated / unsubstantiated / inconclusive with documented basis
- Remedy specification required before case can close
- Sealed evidence export for legal hold or tribunal proceedings

**Stage 6: Remediation**
- Remediation plan entered with owner, due date, evidence requirement
- Progress tracked with automated escalation on overdue items
- Completion verification gates (evidence-required, not self-reported)
- Pattern signal fed back into Module 2 analytics

### 4.3 The Dual-Control Principle

For any ABR case classified at High severity:
- Case closure requires two independent reviewers
- Severity downgrade requires documented justification and second sign-off
- Identity unmask requires compliance-officer authorization plus access log entry

This mirrors the evidence-grade protocols that make FairCase defensible at tribunal. It is also a selling argument: "Our platform protects your investigators as much as it protects the process."
