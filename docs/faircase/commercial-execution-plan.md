## 1. Safer Public Messaging Architecture

### 1.1 The problem with the current framing

The strategy document uses "4% finding" as a market anchor. The risk is a buyer hearing:
*"You have an ABR problem, and FairCase will expose you."*

That is not a buying trigger. That is a compliance department red flag.

The safe version of the same truth is: *"Your current data may not be giving you an accurate picture of where you stand. That creates risk — and we can help you understand your actual baseline."*

Same finding. Opposite emotional register. The buyer moves toward the conversation instead of away from it.

### 1.2 Messaging swap table

| Risky phrasing | Safe and equally strong replacement |
|---|---|
| "Your institution has an ABR problem" | "Most institutions find their classification data is incomplete — and that creates real exposure" |
| "Only 4% of cases are ABR-coded" | "Sector-level data suggests a significant classification visibility gap exists across Canadian institutions" |
| "FairCase finds ABR you've been missing" | "FairCase gives you a clear picture of what your case data is and isn't capturing" |
| "Classification failure" | "Classification gap" or "visibility gap" |
| "Unclassified ABR exposure" | "Protected grounds visibility risk" |
| "Detecting ABR patterns" | "Surfacing protected grounds signals in existing case records" |
| "Your investigators missed this" | "Standard investigation frameworks weren't designed to capture this — most aren't" |
| "ABR audit" | "Equity baseline review" or "classification readiness review" |
| "Tribunal-defensible record" | "Investigation-grade documentation" |

### 1.3 The governing tone principle

**FairCase is a governance partner, not an indictment engine.**

Every buyer conversation should feel like: *"We're going to help you understand what you have, and build the process to manage it well."*

Not: *"We found the thing you've been hiding."*

The FPSLREB 4% signal is always framed as a **sector-level observation** that creates a **sector-wide question** for every institution: *"What does our data look like?"* That question sells. The accusation doesn't.

### 1.4 The three-sentence public-safe version of the core claim

> "Sector-level data from Canada's federal labour adjudication system suggests that explicit Anti-Black Racism case classifications are rare relative to the documented presence of race-based workplace harm. Most institutions do not have a clear picture of their actual protected grounds case data. FairCase was built to close that gap — with intelligence tools, structured workflows, and reporting that institutions can act on and stand behind."

That is commercially strong, legally defensible, and does not overclaim.

## 2. Positioning Architecture

### 2.1 The two-layer brand

**Outer layer (door-opener):**
> **FairCase** — Equity Intelligence Platform

This is what appears on a LinkedIn post, a procurement registration, a conference badge. It is broad enough for any buyer in HR, legal, or equity to engage with.

**Core flagship module (mission anchor):**
> **ABR Intelligence** — Protected Grounds Visibility for Canadian Institutions

This is what appears in the proposal once a conversation is underway. It is specific, differentiated, and mission-anchored.

### 2.2 Why the two-layer matters

Some institutions are ready to engage explicitly with ABR. Others will engage with "equity intelligence" first and arrive at ABR through the diagnostic process. Both paths lead to the same product. The outer layer removes the barrier that prevents the second group from entering the conversation.

A union equity director will open with ABR from day one.  
A CHRO at a federal department may open with "classification governance."  
A General Counsel at a university may open with "protected grounds risk management."

All three are the same buyer. All three need the same product.

## 3. 15-Minute Buyer Demo Script

### 3.1 Format

Screenshare or printed PDF. Not a slide deck. The demo shows the product — not talking points about the product.

Three screens only. Each screen has one moment of genuine surprise for the buyer.

### 3.2 Pre-demo framing (2 minutes)

> "Before I show you the platform, let me ask you one question: If I asked you right now — across all the workplace complaints and HR investigations your organization handled in the last three years — how many of them involved a race or Anti-Black Racism component? Not how many were filed under that category. How many actually involved it?"

Pause. Let them answer.

> "Most HR and legal leaders we talk to say: 'We think it's more than what's in our system. But we can't prove it either way.' That's exactly what FairCase was built to address. Let me show you three things."

### 3.3 Screen 1: The Classification Scan (3 minutes)

**What to show:** The ABR Classification Intelligence module showing a simulated institutional scan — 2,400 historical cases processed over 90 days.

**Key callout:**

- Of 2,400 cases, 94 (3.9%) were explicitly coded with race or ABR.
- FairCase classification identified 287 cases (11.9%) with medium-to-high ABR signal confidence.
- Gap: 193 cases that may have a race component — but the institution's systems don't show it.

**The moment:** "That gap — between what's in your system and what's actually there — is your exposure. It's also your starting point for any meaningful equity program."

**Do not say:** "You have 193 cases you missed." Say: "These 193 cases are worth a structured review. You may close most of them on review. But you need to have that conversation — before anyone else does."

### 3.4 Screen 2: The Pattern Dashboard (5 minutes)

**What to show:** The department-level heat map. Same simulated institution. Five departments shown.

**Key callout:** Department 3 has 4× the ABR-signal density of the institutional average. Department 5 has been generating a rising trend over 24 months — aligned with a leadership transition 18 months ago. Department 1 is clean.

**The moment:** "This view does not exist anywhere in your current HR systems. Your HR case tool shows you individual cases. FairCase shows you patterns. Those are not the same thing — and patterns are what boards ask about."

**Selling line:** "This is the view your equity officer has been trying to build in Excel for three years. It takes us 30 days to generate from your existing data."

### 3.5 Screen 3: The Board Report Preview (3 minutes)

**What to show:** The Equity Intelligence Reporting Suite — the draft annual ABR accountability report, French and English, with the institutional classification summary, pattern analysis, and remediation completion rate.

**Key callout:** The report includes: total cases by protected ground, classification trend (improving/declining), investigations closed by outcome, remediation actions and completion rate, comparator benchmark against sector average.

**The moment:** "This report can be generated on demand. It is reproducible — every quarter, every year, same structure. It is audit-ready. And it doesn't depend on whether your equity officer had time to build it."

**Ask:** "If you ran this across your case data for the last three years, what do you think you'd find? We can answer that question — free, in 30 days, with no contract. That's our diagnostic offer. You see the findings. You decide what to do with them."

### 3.6 Demo close (2 minutes)

> "I want to be clear about what we're not doing. We're not here to tell you your institution is failing. Most institutions are doing what they can with the tools they have — and those tools weren't designed for this. We're giving you the tools that were. The diagnostic is how we start. You see your data. No obligation."

Leave behind: one-page diagnostic offer sheet (see pilot package document).

## 5. Founder Outreach Campaign

### 5.1 Principles

Michel's personal brand is the commercial asset. He is not an SDR. Every outreach message should read as if a thoughtful peer reached out — not as if a sales sequence was triggered.

Rules:

- Never pitch in the first message
- Lead with a specific observation about the recipient's institution or role — not a generic compliment
- Reference real institutional context (tribunal decision, published report, CBA clause, public commitment)
- Offer value before making any request
- Make the diagnostic ask a service offer, not a sales close

### 6.4 Path to $250K ARR: the critical actions

**The six decisions that determine whether the conservative case holds:**

1. **Run 3+ diagnostics by end of M3.** The pipeline does not build without diagnostic volume. Every month of diagnostic delay is a month of ARR delay downstream.

2. **Convert the CAPE relationship in M4.** CAPE is the fastest-path account. A signed contract with Canada's professional federal employees union is the credibility unlock for every subsequent public sector conversation.

3. **Do not discount pilots to close faster.** The $12,000 pilot fee is not a barrier — it is a commitment signal. Free pilots attract uncommitted buyers and create conversion problems. Hold the fee.

4. **Build the reference story by M7.** The pilot 1 case study — even anonymized, even brief — is the asset that converts the next five diagnostics into pilots. Without it, the M7–M9 pipeline stalls.

5. **Have the TBS conversation by M3.** Even if Treasury Board takes 12–18 months, starting that conversation in M3 means it can potentially land in Q4 of Y1. Every month of delay is a month added to that already-long cycle.

6. **Price Enterprise correctly from day one.** When the first multi-department or multi-institution buyer appears, the Enterprise tier must be positioned clearly at $84K+. Underpricing the first Enterprise deal sets a precedent that compresses every subsequent deal.
