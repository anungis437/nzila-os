# Doctrine Governance Scorecard

> **Status:** Canonical governance · **Layer:** Measurement and trend · **Inherits:** all governance documents in this directory

This document defines the **scorecard** by which doctrine adherence is measured across products, surfaces, releases, AI capabilities, and stakeholder-facing artifacts.

It is not a productivity metric and not an engineering KPI. It is the institutional measurement of how reliably Nzila's running systems express its doctrine.

The scorecard supports trend reading, surfacing of drift, prioritization of remediation, and external evidence of governance discipline.

---

## 1. Posture

The scorecard:

- Measures **doctrine adherence**, not feature output
- Surfaces **drift**, not blame
- Supports **stewardship**, not ranking
- Is itself **governance-safe** — no individual scoring, no covert resolution, no productivity framing

A scorecard that drifted into individual scoring would itself be a doctrine violation.

---

## 2. Scoring Model

The scorecard uses a four-band qualitative model:

- **Excellent** — doctrine fully expressed; no concerns
- **Acceptable** — doctrine adequately expressed; minor improvements possible
- **Warning** — doctrine partially expressed; remediation required
- **Doctrine Violation** — doctrine breached; immediate remediation required

Bands are determined by review evidence, not by composite calculation. There is no single overall number.

---

## 3. Evaluation Dimensions

Each evaluated subject (product, surface, release, AI capability, narrative artifact) is scored across the dimensions below.

### 3.1 Continuity Alignment
Does the subject preserve and express continuity doctrine? Multi-dimensional posture rendering, signal taxonomy fidelity, posture trajectory honesty.

### 3.2 Governance Legitimacy
Does the subject preserve human authority? Authority framing presence, override path legitimacy, governance forum reviewability.

### 3.3 Calmness
Does the subject meet calmness floors? Density, motion restraint, layout stability, urgency framing discipline.

### 3.4 Anti-Surveillance Posture
Does the subject avoid individual behavioral resolution? Aggregation stance honored, restricted-class handling, audit-visibility appropriateness.

### 3.5 Explainability
Are AI, analytic, and recommendation outputs explainable? Assumption/horizon/uncertainty rendering, rationale availability, generation indication.

### 3.6 Executive Cognitive Safety
Do executive-touching elements respect cognitive thresholds? Density, pacing, dashboard restraint, alerting limits.

### 3.7 Deployment Legitimacy
Was the subject deployed under deployment governance? Sequencing, pacing, reversibility, stakeholder visibility.

### 3.8 Operational Legitimacy
Does the subject preserve role-bearer and stakeholder legitimacy? Pilot discipline, isolation, role-centering.

### 3.9 Stakeholder Trust
Does the subject strengthen — or at minimum not degrade — trust across affected stakeholder populations?

### 3.10 Narrative Alignment
Where the subject has external surfaces, do they align with [investor and procurement narrative alignment](investor-and-procurement-narrative-alignment.md)?

---

## 4. Subject Types

### 4.1 Product Score
Aggregated across product surfaces, releases, AI capabilities, and narrative artifacts within a horizon.

### 4.2 Surface Score
Per-surface evaluation against design governance, executive cognitive standards, and visibility enforcement.

### 4.3 Release Score
Per-release evaluation against deployment governance, doctrine compliance verdicts, and post-release observation.

### 4.4 AI Capability Score
Per-capability evaluation against the AI governance regime.

### 4.5 Narrative Artifact Score
Per-artifact evaluation against narrative alignment.

### 4.6 Cross-Product Score
Periodic evaluation of cross-product invariant adherence and vocabulary consistency.

---

## 5. Cadence

Scorecards are produced at:

- Per release — release governance scorecards close release events
- Per quarter — surface, AI, and narrative cadence audits
- Per half-year — product-level and cross-product audits
- On-demand — when drift or incident warrants

Cadence may tighten in response to drift; it does not loosen for convenience.

---

## 6. Outputs

A scorecard produces:

- Per-dimension band assignment with cited evidence
- Cited remediations for any non-Excellent band
- Trend assessment versus prior scorecard
- Escalation list for Warning and Doctrine Violation bands
- A reviewable record entered into the governance trail

Scorecards are not press materials. They are stewardship instruments.

---

## 7. Use of Scorecard Outputs

Scorecard outputs feed:

- Remediation prioritization
- Architecture review agendas
- Doctrine governance forum reviews
- [Doctrine operationalization readiness review](doctrine-operationalization-readiness-review.md)
- External governance evidence where appropriate (procurement, certification, regulator)

Scorecards do not feed:

- Individual performance evaluations
- Marketing surfaces
- Compensation
- Ranking of products or teams

The scorecard is structurally protected from misuse.

---

## 8. Anti-Patterns

- **Composite collapse** — combining bands into a single score for convenience
- **Individual attribution** — naming individuals in band assignments
- **Cosmetic remediation** — closing remediations without addressing the underlying doctrinal cause
- **Cadence laxity** — extending cadence to defer hard findings
- **Evidence absence** — bands assigned without cited evidence
- **External weaponization** — using scorecards as competitive marketing material

---

## 9. Discipline

The scorecard's authority comes from consistency, evidence, and protection from misuse. A scorecard that becomes a tool of pressure becomes a doctrine violation in its own right.

Used correctly, it is the instrument by which Nzila reads its own continuity posture.
