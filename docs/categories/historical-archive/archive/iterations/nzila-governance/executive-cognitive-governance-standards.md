# Executive Cognitive Governance Standards

> **Status:** Canonical governance · **Layer:** Executive surface standards · **Inherits:** [executive-cognitive-governance.md](../nzila-ip/executive-cognitive-governance.md), [institutional-design-governance.md](institutional-design-governance.md)

This document specifies the **measurable standards** that govern executive surfaces — those surfaces that support consequential governance acts, board-relevant judgment, and the daily continuity reading executive role-bearers must perform.

The premise is doctrinal: executive cognition is a constrained institutional resource. Surfaces that consume it without doctrinal cause damage governance.

These standards are enforced at design review, doctrine compliance review, architectural review, and end-to-end validation.

---

## 1. What Counts as an Executive Surface

An executive surface is any surface whose primary stakeholder is:

- A continuity-accountable principal (CEO, executive director, accounting officer, head of governance, chief continuity owner)
- A board, oversight, or governance forum
- A regulator-facing summary surface
- A senior role-bearer with consequential authority over continuity

Surfaces that occasionally show executive content but are not primary executive surfaces inherit these standards proportionally.

---

## 2. Density Thresholds

Executive surfaces are bound by tighter density budgets than general surfaces.

| Standard | Floor |
|----------|-------|
| Distinct interactive regions on primary view | bounded; small set |
| Distinct information classes on primary view | bounded; small set |
| Concurrent state indicators | bounded; doctrinally minimal |
| Concurrent affordances | bounded; supports primary act, no more |
| Tile count on primary dashboard | bounded; each tile doctrinally justified |

Exceeding any threshold without doctrinal justification is a governance defect.

---

## 3. Pacing Standards

- Executive surfaces refresh at **governance cadence**, not data cadence
- Real-time-style feeds are absent
- Updates between governance cycles are summary-shaped, not stream-shaped
- Time-to-posture (from open to first comprehension of continuity posture) is short and bounded
- Time-to-act (from posture to governance act) is short and predictable

Pacing that mirrors operational dashboards is off-doctrine.

---

## 4. Calmness Metrics

- Color palette: bounded; conservative
- Motion: state-transition only; no decorative or attention-seeking motion
- Notification arrivals on executive surfaces: bounded by notification governance
- Urgency framing: only where doctrinally justified by continuity stake
- Surface stability: layout does not shift under live updates

Calmness on executive surfaces is a procurement-grade property.

---

## 5. Readability Standards

- Type hierarchy expresses continuity hierarchy
- Reading paths are linear where comprehension matters
- Quantitative content is paired with interpretive context
- Multi-dimensional posture is rendered legibly without composite reduction
- Tabular content is bounded; long lists are not primary surfaces

Readability is reviewed not by aesthetic but by *cognitive cost to read accurately*.

---

## 6. Hierarchy Standards

Hierarchy on executive surfaces:

1. Continuity posture
2. Stake and exposure
3. Signal and evidence
4. Interpretation
5. Governance act surface

This order is invariant. Surfaces that elevate signals above posture, or acts above interpretation, are restructured at review.

---

## 7. Dashboard Restraint

Executive dashboards must satisfy, before approval:

- Each tile expresses a doctrinally coherent continuity concern
- Each tile has a stated governance purpose
- Tile inclusion is paced — additions require doctrine compliance review
- No tile presents a composite continuity score
- Tiles do not compete through motion, color saturation, or urgency framing
- Dashboard density respects threshold (§2)

Dashboards that fail any criterion are reduced or restructured.

---

## 8. Alerting Limits

- Alert classes on executive surfaces are bounded and doctrinally defined
- Alert arrival rates are bounded; sustained breach triggers governance review
- Alert acknowledgment is meaningful, not click-through
- Alerts do not surface restricted-class individual signals
- Alert escalation is visible to the affected stakeholder

Alert chaos on executive surfaces is a categorical doctrine violation.

---

## 9. Escalation Visibility

- Every escalation surface identifies originator, route, and authority
- No covert escalation
- Escalation cadence is bounded
- De-escalation paths are equally legitimate
- Escalation history is reviewable in governance forums

Escalation visibility is a trust contract with affected stakeholders.

---

## 10. Standards Enforcement

These standards are enforced at:

- Design review (per surface)
- Doctrine compliance review (per feature)
- Architectural review (per platform change)
- End-to-end validation (density, cadence, notification audit)
- Post-release audit (sustained breach detection)

A breach that survives all five layers is treated as a top-tier governance defect.

---

## 11. Anti-Patterns

- Real-time feeds on executive surfaces
- Composite continuity scoring
- Motion or color used to compete for attention
- Notification streams replacing summary cadence
- Dashboards constructed for breadth rather than for governance support
- Click-through acknowledgment without governance act
- Covert escalation
- Tabular data dumps as primary executive surface

---

## 12. Discipline

Executive cognitive governance is the surface most often eroded by reasonable-sounding requests: "just one more tile", "just a small alert", "just a real-time feed for one team".

These standards are the institutional answer. They are not negotiable on convenience.
