# Doctrine Drift Governance

> **Status:** Canonical · **Layer:** Meta-doctrine · **Purpose:** Prevent erosion of institutional IP integrity

Doctrine drift is the slow, often invisible erosion of conceptual integrity that occurs when:

- Vocabulary mutates under pressure from sales, engineering, or marketing
- Product behavior diverges from doctrinal intent without explicit review
- Lower-priority doctrines accumulate exceptions to higher-priority ones
- Implementation patterns leak upward and reshape doctrine
- New hires inherit only the surface artifacts (terms, screens) without the underlying philosophy

Doctrine drift is the **single most dangerous failure mode** for an institution whose moat is doctrine.
This document defines how it is detected, prevented, and corrected.

---

## 1. Doctrine Review Process

### 1.1 Triggers
Doctrine review is mandatory when any of the following occur:

- A product feature appears to require a doctrinal exception
- A new vertical, customer segment, or market is being entered
- Terminology conflicts emerge across products
- An external party (investor, regulator, partner) proposes redefinition
- Cross-product audit surfaces inconsistency
- A new doctrine document is proposed
- A material modification to existing doctrine is proposed

### 1.2 Review Cadence
- **Quarterly:** terminology + cross-product consistency review
- **Semi-annual:** full doctrine integrity audit
- **Annual:** doctrine vs. competitive landscape review (does Nzila's category boundary still hold?)
- **Ad hoc:** triggered reviews per §1.1

### 1.3 Review Outputs
Every review produces:

- A signed doctrinal record (kept in institutional memory)
- A list of accepted clarifications
- A list of rejected proposals with rationale
- A list of implementations requiring remediation
- A drift-risk register update

## 2. Terminology Governance

Doctrine vocabulary is **load-bearing**. Terms are not interchangeable.

### 2.1 Terminology Discipline
- Each canonical term has exactly one definition, in exactly one document
- Synonyms are explicitly enumerated and bound to the canonical term
- Marketing copy must use canonical terms or explicitly licensed paraphrases
- Engineering identifiers (types, modules, env vars) must echo canonical terms where they describe doctrinal concepts

### 2.2 Forbidden Substitutions
The following substitutions constitute drift and must be rejected:

| Forbidden | Doctrinal Term |
|-----------|----------------|
| "AI" replacing "institutional intelligence" | Institutional intelligence |
| "automation" replacing "governance-safe coordination" | Governance-safe coordination |
| "predictions" replacing "interpretive surfacing" | Interpretive surfacing |
| "users" replacing "stakeholders" or "role-bearers" | Role-bearers / stakeholders |
| "dashboard" replacing "operational reading surface" (where doctrinal) | Operational reading surface |
| "score" replacing "continuity reading" | Continuity reading |
| "alert" replacing "continuity signal" | Continuity signal |
| "monitoring" replacing "continuity visibility" | Continuity visibility |

### 2.3 Term Introduction
New terms enter doctrine only via review. They must:

- Carry a precise definition
- Be distinguishable from existing terms
- Be consistent with higher-priority doctrine
- Have a stated lifecycle (provisional, canonical, deprecated)

## 3. Continuity Philosophy Enforcement

Every doctrinal update is checked against:

- Does it preserve continuity-centered framing?
- Does it preserve human governance authority?
- Does it preserve explainability?
- Does it preserve modernization-without-destabilization?
- Does it avoid surveillance and productivity-extraction framing?

Failure on any axis → rejection by default.

---

## 4. Governance-Safe Language Enforcement

### 4.1 Banned Framings
- "Replace humans"
- "Eliminate bottlenecks" (when "bottleneck" denotes a person)
- "Predictive enforcement"
- "Behavioral analytics" (without explicit governance framing)
- "Performance scoring of individuals"
- "Disrupt the institution"

### 4.2 Preferred Framings
- "Augment institutional understanding"
- "Reduce coordination friction"
- "Surface continuity signals for human review"
- "Support governance-safe interpretation"
- "Modernize without destabilizing"

### 4.3 Tone Discipline
Doctrine and product copy must read as **institutional**, not entrepreneurial. Strategic calmness is the default register (see [executive-cognitive-governance.md](executive-cognitive-governance.md)).

## 5. Modernization Compliance Audits

Each product release passes a compliance pass that verifies:

- No new feature contradicts doctrine
- No copy change violates terminology governance
- No UX pattern violates role-centered architecture
- No integration violates governance-safe intelligence
- No metric violates continuity metrics philosophy

Audit results are filed under institutional memory and surface in release records.

## 6. Cross-Product Doctrine Audits

Performed semi-annually across the entire Nzila product portfolio. Outputs:

- Inconsistency map (which products diverge from canonical doctrine)
- Remediation plan with ownership
- Risk-weighted prioritization (highest priority: drift in doctrine layers 1–3)

## 7. Institutional Consistency Reviews

Beyond product code, consistency is enforced across:

- Sales decks
- Procurement responses
- Investor materials
- Public website
- Conference talks
- Partner enablement
- Hiring and onboarding materials

External materials that drift from doctrine are corrected; recurrent drift triggers a process review.

## 8. Prohibited Implementation Patterns

The following are categorically prohibited regardless of perceived business value:

- **Opaque scoring of individuals** based on continuity signals
- **Unreviewable automated escalation** to executives or external parties
- **Surveillance dashboards** framed as "intelligence"
- **Behavioral nudging** disguised as governance
- **Black-box interpretations** without human override paths
- **Feature flags that silently change governance behavior**
- **Vendor lock-in patterns** that compromise continuity portability
- **AI outputs presented as authoritative judgment** rather than interpretive aids

## 9. Doctrinal Deviation Escalation

When deviation is detected:

1. **Containment** — affected implementation is paused or feature-flagged off
2. **Review** — convene doctrine review with all affected stakeholders
3. **Resolution** — either remediate the implementation or, in rare cases, formally update doctrine
4. **Record** — file a doctrinal incident record in institutional memory
5. **Pattern check** — examine whether the deviation indicates a systemic risk (e.g., commercial pressure, hiring gap, training gap)
6. **Prevention** — update review processes, training, or tooling to prevent recurrence

---

## 10. Drift-Risk Register

A living register tracks:

- Known pressures on doctrine (commercial, technical, regulatory)
- Recent near-misses
- Verticals or products with elevated drift risk
- Terminology under stress
- External actors attempting redefinition

The register is reviewed at every governance review.
