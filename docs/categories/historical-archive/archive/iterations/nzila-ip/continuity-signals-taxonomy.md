# Continuity Signals Taxonomy

> **Status:** Canonical · **Layer:** Operational doctrine · **Inherits:** Continuity Ontology, Governance-Safe Intelligence

This document is the **formal classification system** for the signals through which an institution's continuity field becomes legible. The taxonomy is exhaustive in structure and extensible in instances. It is the shared vocabulary across all Nzila products that surface continuity-relevant material.

A **signal** is observable or inferable institutional material that carries information about the continuity field. Signals are interpretive inputs, never authoritative on their own.

---

## 1. Classification Axes

Every signal is classified along **eight orthogonal axes**:

1. **Visibility** — observable / inferred
2. **Origin** — operational / strategic
3. **Sensitivity** — governance-sensitive / governance-neutral
4. **Audience** — executive-level / role-specific / cross-cutting
5. **Criticality** — continuity-critical / continuity-relevant / continuity-adjacent
6. **Temporality** — acute / sustained / chronic / latent
7. **Substrate** — process / memory / authority / relational / strategic / reputational
8. **Aggregation Stance** — individually meaningful / aggregate-only / categorically off-limits-individually

The eighth axis is doctrinally critical: it preserves the prohibition on individual behavioral exploitation.

---

## 2. Visibility Classes

### 2.1 Observable Signals
Signals derivable directly from institutional records, events, or artifacts. Examples (illustrative, implementation-agnostic): decisions delayed past stated cadence; documented succession gaps; cross-team handoff failures; recurring escalation concentration.

### 2.2 Inferred Signals
Signals derived through interpretation of patterns, absences, or composites. Examples: silence around topics historically discussed; reduced participation patterns in forums where it previously held; vocabulary divergence in cross-team artifacts.

> **Inferred signals carry higher interpretive risk and require stricter explainability and reviewability.**

---

## 3. Operational vs. Strategic Signals

### 3.1 Operational Signals
Pertain to the daily and weekly conduct of institutional activity. Surface to roles whose center-of-gravity addresses operations.

### 3.2 Strategic Signals
Pertain to the longer-horizon trajectory of the institution: posture, positioning, leadership, mission integrity. Surface to roles whose center-of-gravity addresses strategy.

Signals may carry both classifications; routing must respect both.

---

## 4. Governance-Sensitive Signals

A signal is **governance-sensitive** when its interpretation could legitimately influence governance decisions. Such signals require:

- Stricter routing
- Stricter access controls
- Stricter retention
- Mandatory traceability
- Explicit interpretive framing

Governance-sensitive signals are **never** surfaced as urgency triggers; they are surfaced as deliberation material.

---

## 5. Audience Classifications

### 5.1 Executive-Level Signals
Routed to roles holding institutional authority. Calibrated for executive cognitive governance (see [executive-cognitive-governance.md](executive-cognitive-governance.md)): low frequency, high interpretive density, framed for deliberation.

### 5.2 Role-Specific Signals
Routed to roles whose specific exposure profile demands the signal. Subject to role-centered architecture constraints.

### 5.3 Cross-Cutting Signals
Surface across multiple roles. Routing follows continuity exposure mapping; concurrent surfacing is sequenced to prevent interpretive collision.

---

## 6. Continuity-Criticality

### 6.1 Continuity-Critical
Signals whose mis-interpretation or non-surfacing materially threatens continuity. Subject to the strongest reviewability and routing standards.

### 6.2 Continuity-Relevant
Signals that materially inform continuity reading but are not, individually, decisive.

### 6.3 Continuity-Adjacent
Signals that enrich interpretation but are unlikely to alter governance decisions on their own.

This classification governs prioritization — never urgency theatrics.

---

## 7. Temporal Classes

| Class | Definition | Interpretive Stance |
|-------|------------|---------------------|
| **Acute** | Discrete, time-bounded condition | Address now; record outcome |
| **Sustained** | Persistent condition over weeks-months | Watch for trend; weigh sequencing |
| **Chronic** | Long-standing condition embedded in operation | Address structurally, not reactively |
| **Latent** | Condition not yet manifest but plausibly emerging | Monitor; prepare scenarios |

Temporal misclassification is a common source of governance miscalibration: chronic conditions treated as acute produce thrash; acute conditions treated as chronic produce neglect.

---

## 8. Substrate Categories

Each signal pertains to one or more institutional substrates:

- **Process substrate** — workflow, procedure, coordination
- **Memory substrate** — documented, embodied, embedded, relational memory
- **Authority substrate** — governance lines, decision rights, escalation
- **Relational substrate** — trust, networks, partnerships
- **Strategic substrate** — direction, positioning, mission
- **Reputational substrate** — external perception, standing

Cross-substrate signals (e.g., authority + memory) are particularly important; they often indicate compounding degradation.

---

## 9. Aggregation Stance

This axis enforces governance-safe intelligence at the data level.

### 9.1 Individually Meaningful (Restricted Use)
A small set of operational signals that are meaningful at the individual role-bearer level (e.g., succession gap for a named role). Use is bounded to legitimate continuity purposes; never repurposed for performance assessment.

### 9.2 Aggregate-Only
Signals that are interpretable only at the team, function, or institutional level. Individual-level surfacing is prohibited.

### 9.3 Categorically Off-Limits Individually
Signals that, regardless of analytical convenience, must never be surfaced at the individual level. Includes anything that would constitute individual behavioral scoring, attention surveillance, or covert performance assessment.

> **Doctrine treats aggregation stance as inviolable. No commercial or operational pressure may relax it.**

---

## 10. Signal Families

The taxonomy organizes specific signals into families. Each family maps to continuity dimensions.

### 10.1 Fragmentation Indicators
- Tooling divergence patterns
- Process variation across structurally similar units
- Vocabulary divergence in shared artifacts
- Cross-unit handoff failures
- Parallel decisions on the same matter

### 10.2 Onboarding Degradation Indicators
- Documented vs. lived process gaps surfaced by recent entrants
- Time-to-effective-contribution trends
- Vocabulary mismatches between cohorts
- Onboarding artifact age and currency
- Departures during early tenure

### 10.3 Operational Instability Indicators
- Decision deferral frequency
- Decision reversal frequency
- Coordination meeting growth without throughput growth
- Recurring ad-hoc resolution of recurring matters
- Failure of routine handoffs

### 10.4 Governance Friction Indicators
- Escalation concentration on small role sets
- Decisions made informally that should be formal
- Decisions made formally that should be informal
- Governance forum agenda saturation
- Strategic items recurrently displaced by reactive items

### 10.5 Escalation Concentration Indicators
- Inbound decision volume per authority-bearing role
- Diversity of escalation sources per role
- Cycle time of escalation resolution
- Re-escalation patterns

### 10.6 Dependency Accumulation Indicators
- Single-point-of-knowledge incidence
- Vendor concentration in critical paths
- Tool concentration in critical paths
- Person-dependent recurring decisions

### 10.7 Institutional Fatigue Indicators
- Sustained governance bandwidth saturation
- Reduced quality of strategic deliberation
- Increased reactive-to-strategic ratio
- Workforce engagement decline in interpretive forums

### 10.8 Stabilization Indicators
- Vocabulary alignment across formerly divergent units
- Decision rhythm restoration
- Memory substrate refresh
- Authority clarity recovery
- Reduced concurrent transformation load

### 10.9 Leadership Continuity Exposure Indicators
- Interpretive frames anchored to a single leader
- Relational bridges leader-specific
- Memory embodied in leadership roles without transfer
- Pending decisions deferred to a known transition
- Workforce trust patterns leader-anchored

---

## 11. Signal Hygiene

Every signal surfaced by a Nzila product must carry, explicitly or accessibly:

- Its classification across all eight axes
- Its source material
- Its interpretive frame
- Its time horizon
- Its uncertainty
- Its routing rationale
- Its retention policy

Signals lacking hygiene are noise and must not be surfaced.
