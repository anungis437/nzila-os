# Role-Centered Continuity Architecture

> **Status:** Canonical · **Layer:** Architectural doctrine (Priority 4) · **Inherits:** Continuity Ontology, Governance-Safe Intelligence, Institutional Risk Philosophy

This doctrine defines how Nzila products organize around **roles**, not features, modules, or workflows. The role is the unit of design. Every surface, signal, sequence, and interaction is governed by the role's continuity exposure, cognitive load, governance authority, and operational center-of-gravity.

This is the architectural layer at which doctrine becomes experiential. It is the layer most prone to drift under product pressure and must be defended accordingly.

---

## 1. Role Gravity

**Role gravity** is the natural pull a role exerts on the institution: the decisions it makes, the interpretations it must produce, the trust it must hold, the continuity it must safeguard.

Role gravity is not measured by org-chart seniority. A long-tenured operations lead may carry more institutional gravity than a recently appointed executive. Architecture must respect actual gravity, not nominal authority.

Design implication: **surfaces are organized around the gravity of the role, not the volume of features available.** A high-gravity role with few interactions outranks a low-gravity role with many.

---

## 2. Operational Center-of-Gravity

Every role has an **operational center-of-gravity**: the small set of concerns that, when addressed correctly, allow everything else to follow. This center is:

- **Specific** to the role
- **Narrower than the role's full scope**
- **Continuity-relevant**
- **Stable across implementation changes**

Architecture identifies the center for each role and ensures that the **first surface a role encounters is centered on it**, not on the totality of available functionality.

A role's primary view answers, in order:

1. What requires my interpretive attention right now?
2. What is the continuity posture I am responsible for?
3. What governance acts are mine alone to perform?
4. What can I defer without continuity cost?

---

## 3. Progressive Sophistication

Sophistication is offered, not imposed.

- **First encounter:** the minimum surface necessary to discharge the role's center-of-gravity
- **Sustained use:** richer interpretive material as the role-bearer's literacy grows
- **Specialist depth:** advanced interpretive frames available on request, never volunteered

This is the inverse of conventional enterprise UX, which tends to expose maximum surface area immediately and rely on the user to filter. Doctrine inverts this: the system filters, the role-bearer escalates depth.

---

## 4. Cognitive Load Governance

Cognitive load is treated as an institutional resource. Doctrine requires:

- **Per-surface load budgets** — every surface has a maximum interpretive load
- **Per-role load budgets** — across all surfaces, the role's daily interpretive load is bounded
- **Load attribution** — when a surface adds load, the doctrinal justification is recorded
- **Load reduction reviews** — periodic audits that surface load creep
- **Load asymmetry awareness** — high-gravity roles are protected from incidental load that should fall to lower-gravity roles

Cognitive overload is a continuity threat: an overloaded role-bearer interprets less, defers more, and ages governance friction.

---

## 5. Invisible Orchestration

Underneath every role surface is significant orchestration: data assembly, signal interpretation, scheduling, escalation routing, governance compliance. Doctrine requires this orchestration to be **invisible by default**.

- The role does not see infrastructure
- The role does not navigate orchestration topology
- The role is not asked to "configure" continuity
- The role is offered the **outcome of orchestration**, not its mechanics

When orchestration becomes visible, it is because it must be — for trust, audit, or troubleshooting — not because the system has leaked.

---

## 6. Continuity-Safe UX

UX is continuity-safe when:

- It does not introduce urgency the institution cannot govern
- It does not surface signals as judgments
- It does not nudge role-bearers toward predetermined actions
- It does not make absence (of activity, of response) into evidence
- It does not gamify governance acts
- It maintains tonal consistency with institutional gravity

UX that is acceptable in a consumer or productivity product may be **categorically unacceptable** in an institutional one.

---

## 7. Stakeholder Continuity Exposure

Every role has a **continuity exposure profile**: the dimensions of the continuity field that depend on this role's interpretation, decision, or memory.

Architecture maps this exposure explicitly:

- Which continuity dimensions does this role guard?
- Which signals must reach this role and no other?
- Which signals must reach this role first?
- Which signals must never reach this role (e.g., raw individual-behavior data)?

Routing follows exposure mapping, not org-chart adjacency.

---

## 8. Experiential Governance

Doctrine treats the **experience itself as a governance instrument**. The way a role encounters the institution shapes:

- Its interpretive frame
- Its sense of authority
- Its trust in the system
- Its continuity-preserving instincts
- Its willingness to use governance authority well

Therefore experience design is governance design. A poorly designed experience can degrade governance even when every individual feature is correct.

---

## 9. Continuity-Centered Operational Routing

When work, signals, or decisions must be routed:

- Routing reads the **continuity field**, not just role assignments
- Routing avoids concentration on already-overloaded roles
- Routing respects governance authority lines
- Routing is **traceable** — the routed-to party can see why
- Routing is **revocable** — misrouting is correctable without governance cost

Routing is therefore a governance act, not a logistical one.

---

## 10. Operational Calmness Systems

Calmness is a deliberate architectural property. It is achieved by:

- Defaulting to quiet surfaces
- Refusing to surface every available signal
- Sequencing interruptions instead of multiplexing them
- Using strong typographic and informational hierarchy
- Designing for **slow rereading**, not only fast scanning
- Treating notification as a governance decision, not a notification system feature

Calmness is the experiential expression of continuity. Loud systems destabilize. Calm systems stabilize.

---

## 11. Anti-Patterns

The following patterns are categorically rejected:

### 11.1 Module Sprawl
Multiplying modules to mirror feature inventories rather than role centers-of-gravity. Symptom: a navigation tree grows to mirror engineering structure, not role need.

### 11.2 Orchestration Overexposure
Forcing role-bearers to understand orchestration topology to perform their role. Symptom: configuration screens for things the role should never need to configure.

### 11.3 Enterprise Fatigue
Defaulting to dense, joyless, opaque enterprise idioms because "that's what enterprise software looks like." Symptom: high-gravity roles avoid the system.

### 11.4 Dashboard Overload
Maximizing visible metrics on the assumption that more is better. Symptom: dashboards that no one reads.

### 11.5 Governance Anxiety
UX that makes role-bearers feel watched, judged, or one-step-from-failure. Symptom: workforce engagement declines after rollout.

### 11.6 AI-Centric UX
Foregrounding AI features as if they were the product. Symptom: the chat surface is the most prominent element on the page.

### 11.7 Continuity-Breaking Complexity
Adding sophistication that costs more in interpretive load than it returns in continuity value. Symptom: power users emerge as gatekeepers; non-specialists disengage.

---

## 12. Architectural Defaults Implied by This Doctrine

- One primary view per role, centered on its operational center-of-gravity
- Quiet, generous information density
- Clear naming aligned with doctrinal vocabulary
- Conservative use of color, motion, and notification
- Surfaces that read well at a glance and reread well in depth
- Explicit, visible governance authority indicators
- Always-available "why am I seeing this" affordance
