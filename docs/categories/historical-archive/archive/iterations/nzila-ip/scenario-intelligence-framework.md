# Scenario Intelligence Framework

> **Status:** Canonical · **Layer:** Architectural doctrine (Priority 6) · **Inherits:** All higher-priority doctrine

This framework defines Nzila's approach to **scenario intelligence**: the disciplined modeling of plausible institutional futures, transitions, and stress pathways for the purpose of human interpretation and governance decision-making.

Scenario intelligence is **not predictive AI**. The distinction is doctrinal and load-bearing.

---

## 1. Continuity Scenario Modeling

A **continuity scenario** is a coherent, traceable, human-reviewable depiction of how a defined set of conditions could unfold across the institution's continuity field over a defined horizon.

A scenario is not a prediction. It is **interpretive material** for governance.

A scenario is well-formed when it:

- States the conditions it assumes
- States the horizon it covers
- Identifies the continuity dimensions it touches
- Surfaces signals to watch for
- Identifies decision points within human authority
- Names plausible alternative scenarios

Scenarios that lack these properties degrade to noise.

---

## 2. Governance Friction Simulation

Governance friction simulation models how decisions, escalations, and authority interactions would unfold under varied conditions. It is used to:

- Identify likely concentration points
- Identify likely deferral patterns
- Identify governance bandwidth shortfalls
- Identify where memory loss would compound

Simulation outputs are **interpretive aids for governance design**, not directives.

---

## 3. Onboarding Continuity Simulation

Onboarding simulation models how a new role-bearer (or cohort) would encounter the institution under current conditions. Outputs include:

- Where memory gaps would surface
- Where vocabulary mismatches would appear
- Where authority disorientation is likely
- Where trust would need to be reformed
- Where existing fragmentation would be inherited

Onboarding simulation is one of the highest-leverage continuity instruments because onboarding is where institutional renewal succeeds or fails.

---

## 4. Leadership Transition Modeling

Leadership transitions are the largest discrete continuity stressors most institutions face. Transition modeling examines:

- Which interpretive frames depend on the departing leader
- Which relational bridges are leader-specific
- Which memory substrates are leader-embodied
- Which decisions the institution has been deferring to the transition
- Which workforce trust patterns are leader-anchored

Modeling is performed **before** transitions when possible, and **during** transitions to support stabilization.

---

## 5. Fragmentation Propagation

Propagation modeling traces how a local fragmentation event (a divergent process, a new tool, a vocabulary split) is likely to spread under current conditions. It identifies:

- The most likely propagation paths
- The likely time-to-systemic-effect
- The roles most exposed to second-order effects
- The intervention points where containment is still cheap

Propagation modeling is preventive: it makes containable fragmentation visible before it compounds.

---

## 6. Continuity Stress Pathways

A continuity stress pathway is a chain of conditions, signals, and effects that, if unaddressed, would weaken the continuity field. Pathways are modeled to:

- Make compounding effects visible
- Distinguish acute pathways from chronic ones
- Identify the cheapest intervention point in each pathway
- Surface pathways that cross conventional organizational boundaries

---

## 7. Stabilization Sequencing

Stabilization sequencing is the ordered identification of stabilization actions such that each action is well-positioned to succeed and does not undo a prior action. Sequencing rules:

- Restore vocabulary before restoring rhythm
- Restore rhythm before restoring memory substrates
- Restore memory substrates before scaling new processes
- Restore authority clarity before introducing new tools
- Allow recovery intervals between sequenced actions

Skipping or compressing sequencing typically produces apparent progress followed by regression.

---

## 8. Institutional Memory Disruption

Memory disruption modeling examines how memory loss would propagate under varied conditions:

- Departure of memory-embodying personnel
- Failure of documented memory systems
- Reorganization that severs memory networks
- Vendor changes that strand historical context
- Leadership turnover that resets interpretive frames

Outputs surface where memory **redundancy** is needed and where **transfer** must precede transition.

---

## 9. Operational Degradation Patterns

Doctrine recognizes recurring degradation patterns. Common ones include:

- **Escalation concentration spiral** — single roles become escalation funnels, fatigue, deferral grows, friction compounds
- **Vocabulary drift cascade** — small terminology splits deepen across teams, eventually severing interpretation
- **Tool sprawl entropy** — divergent tooling fragments memory, then process, then authority
- **Renewal failure pattern** — onboarding degrades, new hires inherit weaker frames, next renewal degrades further
- **Reactive governance pattern** — governance bandwidth consumed by reactive matters, strategic governance atrophies
- **Trust withdrawal pattern** — small trust violations accumulate; workforce withdraws interpretive cooperation

Recognizing pattern membership lets institutions intervene at the pattern level, not the symptom level.

---

## 10. Scenario Intelligence vs. Predictive AI

This distinction is **load-bearing** and must remain intact across all communications, products, and procurement conversations.

| Scenario Intelligence (Nzila) | Predictive AI |
|---|---|
| Surfaces interpretive material for human review | Outputs predictions for action |
| Traceable to inputs and assumptions | Often opaque |
| Bounded to a stated horizon and conditions | Often presented as universal |
| Refuses individual prediction | Frequently centers individual prediction |
| Sequenced for governance use | Optimized for accuracy metrics |
| Strengthens human authority | Often displaces human authority |
| Outputs include explicit uncertainty | Often conceals uncertainty |
| Designed to be reviewable by non-specialists | Frequently requires specialist review |

> **Scenario intelligence is institutional interpretation infrastructure. It is not predictive AI. Where AI is used, it is used in service of interpretation, never as a replacement for it.**

---

## 11. Scenario Hygiene

Every scenario, simulation, or modeling output produced by Nzila products must:

- Carry its assumptions and horizon visibly
- Carry its uncertainty visibly
- Identify the human authority responsible for interpretation
- Be reviewable in its construction
- Be revisable as conditions change
- Be retired when its conditions no longer hold

Scenarios that persist beyond their useful life become mis-information.

---

## 12. Use Pattern

Scenarios are most useful when:

- Considered alongside, not instead of, current readings
- Used to test governance preparedness, not to drive governance action
- Used to surface unexamined assumptions
- Used in deliberation rather than substituted for it

Scenarios are least useful — and most dangerous — when treated as predictions, when over-trusted, or when used to defer decisions that present conditions already require.
