# Cora Second-Consumer Selection Matrix

**Status:** evidence-qualified comparison; no second consumer selected.
Candidate scores are evidence-bounded discovery aids, not endorsements,
probabilities of acceptance, source permissions, or a decision. The
[Evidence Ledger](CORA_SECOND_CONSUMER_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md) contains the
candidate-specific facts and primary sources; the
[Discovery Gaps](CORA_SECOND_CONSUMER_DISCOVERY_GAPS.md) specifies the minimum
facts still needed for the top two classes.

## Scoring method

Each criterion has an individual `score` from 1 to 5, an `evidence_basis`,
`confidence`, and `unresolved_dependency`. Scores indicate fit for proving
bounded, neutral, cross-institutional evidence reuse; 5 is more favorable. A
high score never offsets missing authority, permission, recipient acceptance,
or operator control. Confidence measures support for that score, not
probability of future agreement.

There is **no weighted or aggregate score**. `NOT_EVIDENCED` means the
reviewed material did not establish a fact; it is not proof that a practice
does not exist. The scores are not findings about unnamed institutions.

### Criteria

1. Institutional independence from certifier
2. Real decision consequence
3. Recurrence
4. Evidence overlap with the authoritative organic evidence
5. Producer/operator reconstruction burden that might be avoided
6. Decision-authority clarity
7. Rights feasibility
8. Measurement feasibility
9. Graph yield
10. Neutrality / low capture risk
11. Time-to-proof
12. Repeatability beyond the first institution

For criterion 10, a higher score means lower assessed risk for a bounded,
neutral proof—not an assertion about an institution's conduct. For criterion
5, a higher score requires evidence of existing burden that could actually
be reduced.

## Penalties and selection gate

No class may be selected by strategic attractiveness, score, institutional
prestige, ease of access, or an assumed relationship. Before selection, the
candidate must have:

1. a named, institutionally independent receiver and its current requirement;
2. an accountable receiver decision authority and its evidence acceptance
   standard;
3. evidence that the same source-authoritative reference can be reused without
   operator reconstruction, with provenance, scope, and context preserved;
4. measured recurrence and an explicitly bounded operator-burden baseline;
5. exact source/provider rights and operator/recipient permissions for the
   intended method, purpose, retention, correction, and onward use;
6. recipient-controlled decisions, rejection/defer capability, and a
   requirement-relative disposition; and
7. observable success measures without unauthorized collection or a central
   source-data copy.

Failure to establish any gate leaves the consumer unresolved or disqualifies
that candidate. The contract's measurable proof thresholds remain controlling.
