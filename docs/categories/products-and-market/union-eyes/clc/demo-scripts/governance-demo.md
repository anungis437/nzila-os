# Governance Demo Script
## Union Eyes — CLC Convention · May 2026

**Persona:** David Okafor, Governance Officer, CUPE Local 4279  
**Duration:** 10–12 minutes  
**Audience:** Governance officers, compliance managers, internal auditors, constitutional committee members

---

## Purpose

Show a governance-oriented stakeholder that Union Eyes:
- **preserves and enhances governance oversight** — not replaces it
- **makes explainability a structural feature**, not an afterthought
- **supports audit-ready evidence** without manual assembly
- **doesn't require trust in black-box decisions** — rationale is always visible

Your job is to make them feel like Union Eyes *makes their job easier*, not *replaces their judgment*.

## Demo Flow

### Step 1 — Open with Framing (1 min — verbal only)

**Do not open the platform yet.**

Say:

> "Governance officers have one foundational concern when a new institutional system is introduced: does it preserve explainability, or does it introduce a black box? If I can't explain a recommendation to a constitutional committee, I can't defend it.
>
> Union Eyes was designed with that constraint as a first principle. Every output in this system has visible rationale. Let me show you what that looks like in practice."

---

### Step 2 — Governance Overview Landing (1 min)

**Click path:** Dashboard → Governance Overview (auto-lands here as David)

**What to show:**
- The governance experience landing — focus on the framing
- Governance posture summary, continuity coherence, review state

**What to say:**
> "David lands here — not at a grievance list, not at member data. He lands at governance framing. What's the institutional coherence state? What governance reviews are pending or active?"

---

### Step 3 — Trust & Explainability (3 min)

**Click path:** Trust & Explainability

**What to show:**
- Explainability indicators
- Rationale visibility features
- Governance review structure

**What to say:**
> "This is the foundational governance surface. Any intelligence output in Union Eyes — any recommendation, any continuity signal — comes with its rationale visible here. There's no 'the system suggests this.' There's always: 'Here's what the system observed, here's why it generated this signal, and here's who reviewed it.'"

**Pause on any visible rationale block and point to it:**
> "That's explainability by design. Not added later. Structural."

**Trust moment to emphasize:**
> "The system can't make governance decisions. David makes governance decisions. Union Eyes gives David better context with which to make them."

---

### Step 4 — Continuity Signals (2 min)

**Click path:** Continuity Signals (or from nav)

**What to show:**
- Organizational continuity indicators
- Transition signal flagging
- Governance alert posture

**What to say:**
> "Governance officers care about continuity fragility — where does institutional memory live, and where is it concentrated in ways that create succession risk? This surface makes those signals reviewable on a standing basis — not just when someone leaves."

**Ask:**
> "In your current governance review process, how do you assess knowledge concentration risk? Most organizations answer that question informally, if at all."

---

### Step 5 — Audit & Evidence (2 min)

**Click path:** Audit & Evidence

**What to show:**
- Evidence review interface
- Governance evidence exports (if available)
- Audit trail structure

**What to say:**
> "Here's the governance evidence layer. Everything that flows through Union Eyes — decisions, assignments, reviews, case outcomes — is auditable. Governance officers can run standing evidence reviews. You don't need to request exports from a technical team; the audit architecture is part of the governance experience."

**If asked about export formats:**
> "Exports are structured for governance review — not raw database dumps. They're designed to accompany constitutional committee reviews."

---

### Step 6 — Pilot Governance Protocol (2 min — verbal + brief screen)

**Click path:** Navigate to `/en-CA/trust` (new tab)

**What to show:**
- Trust & Governance marketing surface
- Pilot governance framework language
- CLC context CTAs

**What to say:**
> "The pilot itself has a governance protocol. It's not a technology deployment — it's a governed institutional experiment. Your governance committee reviews at each phase. You retain stop-authority at any point. Nothing scales without your sign-off."

**Then say:**
> "That's what I mean by governance-safe. The platform serves the governance function. The governance function doesn't serve the platform."

## Questions to Ask Them

- "How does your current governance review process document rationale for institutional decisions?"
- "If a constitutional committee asked you to defend a recommendation from your current tools, how easy would that be?"
- "What's your standing cadence for continuity risk review?"

| Area                          | Why                                                     |
|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| AI assistant / automation     | Creates black-box anxiety — never frame as AI           |
| Member personal case data     | Not relevant to governance demo; keep level appropriate |
| Workflow automation features  | Technical detail — breaks governance narrative          |
| Metric dashboards (raw)       | Governance cares about rationale, not metric volume     |

---

## Recovery Flow If Interrupted

**"How does this handle constitutional challenges?"**
> "The audit architecture is designed with exactly that in mind. Every recommendation trace is exportable and human-reviewable. Nothing in Union Eyes creates a constitutional obligation — it informs your governance committee's deliberations."

**"Who controls the explainability outputs?"**
> "Your organization does. The governance officer configures what reviews are scheduled, what is auditable, and who has visibility. The platform serves your governance structure, not the other way around."

**"Is this subject to privacy legislation?"**
> "Yes — and that's built in. Data residency, access controls, and privacy layer architecture are part of the security evidence package. David would review that through the audit surface before any deployment."
