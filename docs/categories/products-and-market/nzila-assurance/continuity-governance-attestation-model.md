# Continuity Governance Attestation Model

> **Status:** Canonical assurance · **Layer:** Continuity attestation · **Inherits:** [../nzila-ip/continuity-ontology.md](../nzila-ip/continuity-ontology.md), [../nzila-governance/continuity-safe-deployment-governance.md](../nzila-governance/continuity-safe-deployment-governance.md), [institutional-certification-framework.md](institutional-certification-framework.md)

This document formalizes the **continuity governance attestation model**: the standardized attestations Nzila produces to demonstrate that continuity safety is preserved across deployment, routing, isolation, onboarding, modernization, and operational coherence.

Attestations are the artifacts buyers, auditors, and stewards consult when they need an authoritative statement — backed by evidence — that continuity is safeguarded.

---

## 1. Posture

Continuity governance attestation:

- Is **scope-bounded** — every attestation declares its scope explicitly
- Is **evidence-cited** — claims point to artifacts in the [evidence pipeline](governance-evidence-pipeline-architecture.md)
- Is **standing-revisable** — attestations are reviewed at cadence, not held perpetually
- Is **honest about partials** — partial attestation is named partial
- Stays **anti-surveillance-safe** — attestation never surfaces individual behavioral material

---

## 2. Attestation Standards

### 2.1 Continuity-Safe Deployment
Attestation that a deployment honored:
- Release manifest discipline
- Sequencing and pacing rationale
- Reversibility validation
- Stakeholder visibility actions
- Environment isolation
- Post-deployment continuity-posture observation

### 2.2 Continuity-Safe Routing
Attestation that routing layer honored:
- Role and stakeholder population gating
- Pilot-scope structural enforcement
- Cross-isolation refusal
- Visibility policy resolution per signal
- Authority requirements per consequential surface

### 2.3 Stakeholder Isolation
Attestation that isolation between stakeholder populations is preserved:
- Identity-layer attribute enforcement
- Routing-layer rejection of cross-isolation traversal
- Surfacing-layer refusal to render out-of-scope data
- Audit trail of refusals
- Cross-isolation contract test outcomes

### 2.4 Institutional Stabilization
Attestation that change introduced into an institution did not destabilize operating equilibrium:
- Continuity posture trajectory through the change window
- Stakeholder communication record
- Incident and rollback record
- Pacing adherence
- Recovery interval observation

### 2.5 Onboarding Continuity
Attestation that onboarding (new role-bearer, new institution, new pilot) preserved continuity:
- Role-canonical first-encounter surface present
- Doctrinal vocabulary used consistently
- Calmness floors honored
- Authority framing visible
- Stakeholder isolation honored from day one

### 2.6 Governance-Safe Modernization
Attestation that modernization steps were continuity-safe:
- Sequence rationale reviewed
- Each step earned the next
- Reversibility validated at each step
- Stakeholder trust monitored
- Pilot discipline preserved

### 2.7 Operational Coherence
Attestation that operational rhythm remained coherent:
- Governance forum cadence honored
- Notification governance ceilings respected
- Executive surface refresh cadence governance-aligned
- Cross-product vocabulary consistency
- Continuity surfaces accessible and current

---

## 3. Attestation Scopes

Attestations are issued at any of the following scopes:

| Scope | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| Per deployment | Single release event evidence |
| Per release | Multi-deployment release window evidence |
| Per environment | Environment-bound continuity posture evidence |
| Per pilot | Pilot-bound discipline evidence |
| Per organization | Institution-bound continuity governance evidence |
| Per product | Product-wide continuity governance evidence |

Scope is declared, not implied.

---

## 4. Attestation Structure

Every attestation includes:

- **Subject** — what is attested
- **Scope** — bounded scope of the attestation
- **Standard** — which attestation standard from §2 is being asserted
- **Verdict** — *Met*, *Partially Met*, *Not Met*
- **Evidence citations** — artifacts from the evidence pipeline supporting the verdict
- **Reviewer attestation** — by role
- **Window** — time window the attestation covers
- **Expiry** — when standing review must refresh

Attestations missing any field are not assurance attestations.

---

## 5. Issuance Procedure

1. Scope and standard declared
2. Evidence assembled from the pipeline
3. Verdict determined per dimension
4. Reviewer attests evidence sufficiency
5. Attestation signed and retained
6. Standing review schedule registered

A claimed attestation that does not appear in the retained, signed record is not an attestation.

---

## 6. Review and Expiry

Attestations are reviewed at:

- Their declared expiry
- Any material change to subject or scope
- Any drift indicator surfacing in the relevant domain
- Any incident bearing on the attestation
- Procurement, certification, or regulator request

Review may sustain, downgrade, or revoke. Sustained C4+ certification depends on sustained attestation refresh discipline.

---

## 7. Anti-Patterns

- **Perpetual attestation** — issuing attestations without expiry
- **Scope creep** — claiming attestation against subjects beyond declared scope
- **Verdict inflation** — *Met* claimed where evidence supports *Partially Met*
- **Cited absence** — citing evidence that does not exist or has been withdrawn
- **Attestation bundling** — collapsing distinct standards into a single attestation to inflate apparent coverage
- **Marketing extraction** — using attestations in promotional surfaces in ways that erode register

---

## 8. External Posture

Continuity attestations are first-class artifacts in:

- Procurement evidence packs
- Certification submissions
- Regulator engagements
- Investor and partner diligence
- Standing readiness reviews

They are presented as institutional artifacts, not as marketing claims.

---

## 9. Discipline

The attestation model's authority depends on the refusal to issue attestations beyond what evidence supports, and the discipline to refresh, downgrade, and revoke when evidence does not sustain.

This model is the substrate of continuity-grade institutional standing.
