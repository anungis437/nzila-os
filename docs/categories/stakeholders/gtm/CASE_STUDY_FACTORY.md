## Why This Matters

In a trust-heavy, relationship-driven market like labour organizations:

- A named peer reference beats any product feature
- A specific outcome ("reduced arbitration prep time by 40 hours per case") beats a generic claim
- A case study that a buyer can share internally is worth more than a pitch deck they'll delete

One strong case study per vertical tier can unlock an entire segment.

---

## When to Start a Case Study

Start the case study process **at pilot kickoff**, not at the end. The framing:

> "As part of how we operate, we'd like to document outcomes from this pilot. Everything goes through your review and you approve what's published. There's no obligation to publish anything you're not comfortable with. But if the results are strong, having a written record helps both of us."

Get this agreement verbally at kickoff. The written sign-off comes later.

## Step 2: Post-Pilot Interview (30 minutes)

Schedule the interview within 2 weeks of pilot end, while outcomes are fresh. The executive sponsor is the preferred interviewee. The pilot admin is useful for the operational detail.

### Interview Questions

**Context:**

1. "Before Union Eyes, walk me through how you handled a typical grievance from filing to resolution."
2. "What was the most frustrating part of that process for your team?"
3. "What triggered you to look for a new approach?"

**During the pilot:**
4. "What surprised you most about how the platform worked in practice?"
5. "Was there a specific moment where you thought 'this actually works'?"
6. "Were there any parts that didn't work as expected?" (Include this — it makes the case study credible)

**Outcomes:**
7. "What changed for your team after 60 days on the platform?"
8. "How has the evidence quality changed for your grievance cases?"
9. "What would you tell a colleague at another union who asked if it was worth trying?"
10. "Is there a number that captures the impact — hours saved, cases resolved faster, SLA compliance rate?"

## Step 3: Metrics Framework

Pull from `docs/union-eyes/pilot-kpis.md`. For every case study, aim to quantify at least 3 of the following:

| Metric | How to Express It |
|--------|-----------------|
| SLA compliance rate | "[X]% of cases met SLA targets, up from [baseline]" |
| Cases processed | "[X] cases tracked over 60 days, 0 lost in handoff" |
| Response time | "Avg response time reduced from [X] to [Y] days" |
| Evidence packs | "[X] court-ready evidence packs generated automatically" |
| Steward adoption | "[X]% of stewards active on platform within 30 days" |
| Staff time saved | "Estimated [X] hours per month freed from manual tracking" |
| Arbitration readiness | "Evidence trail available on demand; previously assembled manually per case" |

If they can't provide baseline numbers, use directional language: "Significantly reduced," "Eliminated manual assembly," "No longer dependent on email chains for case history."

---

## Step 4: Quote Extraction

From the interview, extract 2–3 usable quotes. A good quote is:

✅ Specific ("The evidence pack used to take 3 hours to assemble per case — now it's generated automatically")  
✅ From a named role (even if anonymized: "Executive Director, provincial union, 8,000 members")  
✅ Surprising or counter-intuitive ("We expected a learning curve. There wasn't one.")  

❌ Generic ("This has been really helpful for our team")  
❌ Product-focused ("The platform has great features")  
❌ Vague ("We're satisfied with the results")

## Step 6: Vertical-Specific Reuse

Once you have a case study from a specific segment:

| Segment Won | Use It With |
|-------------|------------|
| Large local union | Other large locals; provincial bodies to show proof of scale |
| Professional association | Other professional associations in same sector |
| Provincial union | National body to demonstrate federation-readiness |

**Adaptation rule:** When sending to a different segment, edit the opening 2 sentences to reflect their context. The results table stays the same.

Example:

- Original: "A large local union in Ontario with 4,500 members..."
- For professional association audience: "A professional association with comparable governance structure and member representation requirements..."

---

## Publication Rules

1. **No publication without written sign-off.** Email confirmation counts.
2. **Org name optional.** Many orgs will agree to an anonymized or sector-level version.
3. **Quotes require specific sign-off** if attributed to a named individual.
4. **Review published case studies every 6 months** — if a customer has churned, remove or update.
5. **Case studies with real metrics > brand-name logos.** Don't hold out for a famous-name customer if a lesser-known org has better data.
