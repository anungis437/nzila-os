## How to Use This Document

Every prospect before outreach should be mapped to a segment here. The segment determines:

- Which pilot tier to lead with
- Approximate sales cycle
- Likely objections
- Expansion potential

If a prospect does not fit any segment, it is not a qualified prospect.

## Union Eyes — Segment Profiles

---

### Segment UE-1: Large Local Union

**Definition:** A single union local with 500–5,000 direct members, a staff of 5–30, and an active grievance caseload.

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Org size** | 500–5,000 members |
| **Staff using the platform** | 5–25 (stewards, LROs, admin, officers) |
| **Pain profile** | Grievances tracked in spreadsheets or email. Steward handoffs drop cases. No leadership visibility into caseload or SLA status. Evidence for arbitration assembled manually, late. |
| **Buying trigger** | Recent arbitration loss due to evidence gap. New executive director pushing for operational modernization. Membership complaint about transparency. |
| **Budget likelihood** | Medium-High — locals with dues income can absorb $30K–$60K/year without board struggle |
| **Decision speed** | 2–6 weeks — executive director + secretary-treasurer typical committee |
| **Urgency** | High when triggered by an event (arbitration outcome, grievance backlog, member pressure) |
| **Sales cycle estimate** | 3–8 weeks from first contact |
| **Easiest entry point** | Discovery pilot or Controlled pilot. Position as "low-risk 60-day test." |
| **Expansion potential** | SaaS Year 1 → Federation module if they have sub-locals → custom reporting |
| **Close probability** | High (65–75%) when there is an identified internal champion |

**Perfect profile:** CUPE Local 79 (Toronto). OPSEU local with recent arbitration issues. ONA regional with SLA problems.

---

### Segment UE-2: Provincial Union (Mid-Tier National Body)

**Definition:** A provincial-level union body with 5,000–50,000 members, centralized operations, and distributed locals or chapters.

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Org size** | 5,000–50,000 members |
| **Staff using the platform** | 20–100 (multi-department: grievance team, admin, research, field reps) |
| **Pain profile** | Multiple simultaneous grievance streams with no unified system. Reporting to a national body or provincial executive. Risk of compliance failures in collective agreement administration. Inconsistent SLA adherence across chapters. |
| **Buying trigger** | Audit or compliance review. Change in leadership with a modernization mandate. National federation pushes standardization. |
| **Budget likelihood** | High — budget authority exists; procurement process exists but is manageable |
| **Decision speed** | 6–12 weeks — executive director + board committee + potentially an RFP |
| **Urgency** | Moderate — tends to be strategic, not reactive |
| **Sales cycle estimate** | 6–14 weeks from first contact |
| **Easiest entry point** | Controlled pilot with a specific department or chapter. Frame as "de-risked, scoped, 60-day proof." |
| **Expansion potential** | Very high — provincial SaaS → national federation expansion → integration with HR systems |
| **Close probability** | Medium-High (50–65%) with strong champion |

**Perfect profile:** BCGEU. OPSEU full organization. OECTA provincial. ONA.

### Segment UE-3: National Labour Body

**Definition:** A national-level umbrella body or major national union (CUPE, PSAC, CLC, Unifor) with 50,000–700,000+ members.

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Org size** | 50,000–700,000+ members |
| **Staff using the platform** | 50–500+ |
| **Pain profile** | Federation-level visibility and reporting across dozens to hundreds of locals. No unified system. Inconsistent evidence and governance standards across the organization. Political complexity around technology adoption. |
| **Buying trigger** | Governance initiative at the national level. Technology committee review. A few progressive locals already using a system and pushing for federation-wide rollout. |
| **Budget likelihood** | High — substantial dues revenue — but budget authority is more diffuse |
| **Decision speed** | 12–24 weeks — full procurement, legal review, union democracy process for major technology decisions |
| **Urgency** | Low to medium — strategic conversations, not reactive purchases |
| **Sales cycle estimate** | 12–24 weeks from first qualified contact |
| **Easiest entry point** | Start with a single local or division as reference customer. Position national body as the long-term expansion, not the first sale. |
| **Expansion potential** | Highest in portfolio — multi-year federation contracts can represent $200K–$1M+ ARR |
| **Close probability** | Low-Medium (25–40%) in first approach; rises dramatically with existing local reference customer |

**Strategy note:** Do not target national bodies directly in Year 1. Win their locals first. Then the national body conversation becomes easier.

### Segment UE-4: Professional Association

**Definition:** A member-governed professional body (nurses association, teachers federation, legal association) with similar governance and representation needs but outside the traditional union structure.

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Org size** | 5,000–100,000 members |
| **Staff using the platform** | 10–50 |
| **Pain profile** | Member advocacy, discipline cases, or grievance-equivalent workflows that have no purpose-built system. Heavy evidence and documentation requirements. Board accountability pressure. |
| **Buying trigger** | Regulatory scrutiny. Member complaint surge. Board requesting better governance reporting. |
| **Budget likelihood** | High — professional associations often have strong dues income and can move faster than unions |
| **Decision speed** | 4–8 weeks — executive director + governance committee |
| **Urgency** | Moderate — tends to be driven by regulatory calendar or board cycle |
| **Sales cycle estimate** | 4–10 weeks |
| **Easiest entry point** | Controlled pilot. Position Union Eyes as "member representation and case governance platform" — not "union grievance tool." |
| **Expansion potential** | High — member portal, reporting tools, AI assistance are all high-value upsells |
| **Close probability** | High (60–70%) — often faster decision-making than unions |

**Perfect profile:** ONA. CRNBC. Law Society of Ontario. Canadian Nurses Association.

---

### Segment UE-5: Emerging / Non-Traditional Member Organization

**Definition:** Any member-governed organization (cooperative, advocacy body, sector council) that manages representation workflows without being a traditional union.

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Org size** | 1,000–20,000 members |
| **Pain profile** | Same structural problem as unions: member advocacy, evidence-sensitive cases, no system. |
| **Buying trigger** | Growth pressure. Board accountability. Compliance initiative. |
| **Budget likelihood** | Variable — depends heavily on dues/funding model |
| **Sales cycle estimate** | 4–12 weeks |
| **Close probability** | Medium (40–55%) — more variance than traditional unions |

**Strategy:** Treat as opportunistic. Do not pursue as primary ICP in Year 1.

## Flow — Segment Profiles

---

### Segment FL-1: SMB Operations Team (20–200 employees)

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Org size** | 20–200 employees |
| **Pain profile** | Disconnected quoting, invoicing, and inventory systems. Manual reconciliation. No single operational view. |
| **Buying trigger** | Outgrowing spreadsheets. Failed QuickBooks integration. Shopify operational chaos at scale. |
| **Budget likely** | $5K–$20K/year (SaaS) + implementation |
| **Sales cycle** | 2–6 weeks |
| **Close probability** | High (55–70%) with strong demo |
| **Expansion path** | AI pricing → advanced reporting → integration pack |

---

### Segment FL-2: Regulated Workflow Buyer (financial services, compliance-sensitive SMB)

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Org size** | 50–500 employees |
| **Pain profile** | Needs audit trail on operational workflows. Current tools don't provide defensible records. |
| **Buying trigger** | Regulatory audit. Insurance or contract requirement for process documentation. |
| **Budget likely** | $20K–$80K/year |
| **Sales cycle** | 6–12 weeks |
| **Close probability** | Medium-High (50–65%) |
| **Expansion path** | Audit hardening → compliance reporting → integration with existing GRC tools |

---

### Segment FL-3: Association Operations Team

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Org size** | 500–50,000 members |
| **Pain profile** | Member management, event coordination, and invoicing run on separate tools. No operational backbone. |
| **Buying trigger** | Leadership change. Membership growth. Board accountability requirement. |
| **Budget likely** | $15K–$60K/year |
| **Sales cycle** | 4–10 weeks |
| **Close probability** | Medium (45–60%) |
| **Expansion path** | Member portal → payment automation → reporting |

## ICP Disqualification Criteria

Do not spend time on a prospect if:

- US-based and unwilling to use Canadian cloud hosting
- < 50 members with no realistic grievance caseload
- Active RFP underway and you haven't been invited (you won't win late)
- No executive sponsor identified after 2 conversations
- Governance culture is explicitly anti-technology ("we do this in person")
- Budget cycle closed and no path to emergency spend
