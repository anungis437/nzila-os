# Why Union Eyes: The Case for a Purpose-Built Union OS

## The Tools Unions Use Were Not Built for Unions

The average union local uses 6–9 separate tools to manage operations: a spreadsheet for grievance tracking, a shared drive for case files, email for member communications, SurveyMonkey for elections, paper ballots for ratification votes, Zoom for town halls, and someone's personal Gmail for steward coordination.

None of these tools were designed for labour administration. None of them understand what a Step 3 is. None of them know the difference between a ULP and a grievance. None of them can tell a rep that Case #47 is past the contractual deadline.

The consequences are measurable:

- Cases get lost in email inboxes
- Deadlines are missed because no one has visibility
- Arbitration prep takes 3× longer than it should
- Institutional knowledge walks out the door with every departing rep
- Members lose confidence when they can't get status updates

## What Purpose-Built Looks Like

Union Eyes was designed with one customer in mind: the rep managing 40 active files while also running a meeting, answering member messages, and trying to file a grievance before the 5-day contractual clock runs out.

Every feature was built around the actual workflow of Canadian labour representation:

**Intake → Investigation → Filing → Arbitration → Outcome**

Not a generic CRM. Not a project management tool with union labels bolted on. A system that knows the stages of a grievance, tracks contractual timelines automatically, surfaces pattern data across cases, and produces arbitration-ready documentation in one click.

## Why Not Build It In-House?

Larger federations have attempted in-house tools. The results are consistent: 18–36 months of development, $400K–$1.2M in cost, a tool that covers intake and tracking but nothing else, and ongoing maintenance burden that falls on staff who have other jobs.

Union Eyes offers the same outcome — plus case intelligence, governance tools, election administration, member communications, and a Canadian-hosted security posture — for the cost of a single staff position's first three months.

## Why the AI Is Different

Union Eyes uses AI in one specific way: to help reps see patterns they couldn't see manually.

When 40 grievances come in over 18 months, no human can easily see that 60% of them originated in the same department, peak in February after performance reviews, and are being adjudicated by the same manager. Union Eyes surfaces that pattern automatically — not to replace the rep's judgment, but to make it sharper.

Every AI output is advisory. Every recommendation shows its sources. Every decision requires human confirmation. This is not ChatGPT for unions — it's pattern detection trained on labour data, designed to reduce the information asymmetry between management and the grievance table.

## The Validation

Union Eyes emerged from direct partnership with Canadian union locals. The features were not invented by a software team. They were built from conversations with grievance officers, shop stewards, and union executives who described exactly what they needed and what they couldn't get from any existing tool.

The pilot organizations are not beta testers. They are co-designers. Their feedback drives the roadmap.
