# Continuity-Safe AI Governance

> **Status:** Canonical governance · **Layer:** AI domain governance · **Inherits:** [governance-safe-intelligence.md](../nzila-ip/governance-safe-intelligence.md), [scenario-intelligence-framework.md](../nzila-ip/scenario-intelligence-framework.md), [executive-cognitive-governance.md](../nzila-ip/executive-cognitive-governance.md), [continuity-metrics-philosophy.md](../nzila-ip/continuity-metrics-philosophy.md)

This document specifies the **AI governance regime** for any AI capability — assistive, generative, retrieval, scoring, summarization, recommendation, scenario, or otherwise — that touches a Nzila surface, signal, or workflow.

It exists because AI is the surface most likely to drift away from doctrine: through opacity, through false precision, through quiet authority displacement, through covert behavioral resolution, through productivity-coercive framing.

This regime is **non-optional** and **categorical**.

---

## 1. Posture

Nzila's AI posture is:

- **Interpretive, not assertive** — outputs surface interpretation, with assumptions, horizons, and uncertainty
- **Authority-preserving** — final judgment, prioritization, and consequential decision remain human responsibilities
- **Aggregation-respecting** — restricted classes are never resolved to individuals
- **Continuity-safe** — outputs serve continuity preservation, not productivity extraction
- **Governance-legible** — every consequential output is explainable in governance forums
- **Restrained** — capability is exposed only as doctrinal purpose justifies

AI capability without doctrinal purpose is removed.

---

## 2. Acceptable AI Exposure

AI capability may be exposed at a Nzila surface only when:

1. The capability serves a stated continuity, governance, or interpretive purpose
2. The capability satisfies the explainability minimums (§3)
3. The capability satisfies the human oversight requirements (§4)
4. The capability does not produce, depend on, or enable any prohibited automation (§5)
5. The capability has passed AI governance review (§7)
6. The capability is exposed under a doctrine compliance review verdict
7. The surface respects the calmness, density, and authority framing standards in [institutional-design-governance.md](institutional-design-governance.md)

Exposure outside these conditions is a doctrine violation.

---

## 3. Explainability Minimums

Every consequential AI output surfaced to a role-bearer or executive must include:

- **Assumptions** — what the output presupposes
- **Horizon** — the time/scope range within which the output is meaningful
- **Uncertainty** — visible expression of confidence, range, or qualification
- **Inputs** — the class and provenance of the inputs used
- **Limits** — what the output does not address

Explainability is not a tooltip. It is a primary surface element.

Where the capability is generative, the surface additionally indicates that the output is generated and identifies the human authority responsible for adoption.

---

## 4. Human Oversight Requirements

Human oversight is required for:

- Every recommendation that touches consequential governance
- Every prioritization that affects role-bearer or stakeholder population
- Every escalation
- Every interpretive output presented as a basis for decision
- Every cross-stakeholder summarization

Oversight requirements include:

- The human authority is identified by role on the surface
- The human authority can pause, override, or reject the output without friction
- The override path is structurally equivalent in legitimacy to the AI path
- Override events are recorded and reviewable

Oversight may not be deferred to "user discretion" without doctrinal grounding.

---

## 5. Prohibited Automation

The following are categorically prohibited. Their proposal triggers a DOCTRINE VIOLATION at AI governance review.

- **Black-box scoring** — scoring of individuals, populations, or governance events using inputs, weights, or methods that are not explainable in governance forums
- **Behavioral manipulation** — outputs designed to influence behavior through framing, urgency, or selective surfacing without doctrinal justification
- **Coercive analytics** — analytics that pressure role-bearers or populations toward speed, output, or compliance metrics
- **Autonomous governance decisions** — automation that executes consequential governance acts without identified human authority
- **Unreviewable recommendations** — recommendations whose basis cannot be reconstructed for review
- **Individual behavioral resolution** — any pipeline, model, or surface that resolves restricted classes to individuals
- **Covert escalation** — automated escalation paths invisible to the affected stakeholder
- **Surveillance scoring** — productivity, attendance, sentiment, or similar individual-behavioral scores
- **Undisclosed generation** — generated content presented without indication of generation
- **Adaptive opacity** — capability that becomes more opaque under stress, scrutiny, or workload

---

## 6. Anti-Surveillance Requirements

AI subsystems must, by construction:

- Refuse restricted-class individual resolution at every layer
- Honor aggregation stance in retrieval, ranking, and summarization
- Preserve role and stakeholder isolation in inputs and outputs
- Avoid retention of input that exceeds the doctrinal retention horizon
- Avoid silent learning from interactions whose retention or use is not explicitly governed

Models, retrievers, and prompts that drift toward individual behavioral resolution are remediated or removed.

---

## 7. AI Governance Review Procedure

An AI governance review is required when:

- A new AI capability is introduced at a Nzila surface
- A material change is made to model selection, retrieval, ranking, prompting, or post-processing
- A change is made to inputs, retention, or learning posture
- A change touches authority framing or oversight surface
- A change crosses product or stakeholder isolation boundaries

Review inputs:

1. Capability description and stated continuity/governance purpose
2. Inputs, provenance, retention, and aggregation handling
3. Model and method, with explainability assessment
4. Output schema with assumption/horizon/uncertainty fields
5. Authority and oversight surfaces
6. Failure modes and worst-case continuity outcome
7. Surveillance assessment
8. Reversibility and rollback

Review verdict feeds the doctrine compliance review.

---

## 8. Escalation

Escalation is required when:

- A capability is proposed for which acceptable exposure is unclear
- A capability would establish precedent affecting other surfaces
- A capability sits near the surveillance boundary
- A capability proposes a doctrinal exception
- A capability surfaces in regulated contexts (legal, clinical, governance, compliance)

Escalations route to architectural doctrine review and, where required, the doctrine governance forum.

---

## 9. Anti-Patterns This Regime Prevents

- **AI everywhere** — capability exposure for showcase reasons
- **Magic outputs** — high-confidence surfaces that hide assumption and uncertainty
- **Authority erosion** — quiet displacement of human responsibility into model outputs
- **Productivity scoring** — individual behavioral resolution dressed as analytics
- **Covert agentic behavior** — agents acting on consequential surfaces without identified human authority
- **Demo-grade AI in production** — capability that has not crossed governance, exposed to real stakeholders

---

## 10. Discipline of This Regime

AI is the most expressive surface a system can offer. That expressiveness is a governance liability when uncoupled from doctrine.

This regime is the coupling. It is enforced at compliance review, at architecture review, at design governance, at deployment governance, and in code.

Drift here erodes Nzila's institutional standing faster than drift anywhere else.
