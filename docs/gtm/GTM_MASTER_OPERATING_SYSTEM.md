## 1. GTM Thesis

NzilaOS is a governed multi-product SaaS platform. The commercial strategy is sequential, not parallel.

**We enter the market through Union Eyes** — the one product that is fully built, audit-hardened, and solving a real problem no one else is solving well: governed representation operations for labour unions.

The thesis in one paragraph:

> Labour unions are evidence-sensitive organizations running on spreadsheets and email. There is no purpose-built governed SaaS for their core work. Union Eyes solves that problem today, with a deployment model designed for organizations that can't afford to get it wrong — managed cloud, org isolation, audit trails, and a mandatory pilot before any long-term commitment. The sales motion is founder-led, trust-first, and proof-heavy. We do not need volume. We need signal, conversion, and reference customers.

We win by being the only credible vendor in a specific lane, not by being the broadest platform in a crowded market.

## 4. Sales Motion Model

**Model: Founder-Led, Trust-First, Proof-Heavy**

This is not a product-led growth motion. It is not an inbound SaaS motion. It is a deliberate, high-trust, consultative sale to a relationship-driven market.

Characteristics:

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Deal sourcing** | Founder-led outreach, warm intros, conference presence |
| **First meeting** | Readiness briefing — 30 min, no demo yet, understand their process |
| **Demo** | Shown only after discovery — tailored to their workflow, not a generic walkthrough |
| **Proposal** | Written pilot scope agreement — specific, not a slide deck |
| **Close motion** | Pilot agreement signed, environment provisioned, metrics locked |
| **Expansion motion** | Mid-pilot check-in → renewal proposal at Week 6 of Controlled pilot |

The sale is not a software demo. It is a trust conversation about whether their governance operations can be improved and whether they're willing to test that claim under controlled conditions.

### Sales Cycle Estimates by Tier

| Buyer Segment | Expected Cycle | Complexity Driver |
|---------------|---------------|------------------|
| Local union (< 500 members) | 2–4 weeks | Fast decision, small committee |
| Provincial union / large local | 4–8 weeks | Executive committee approval |
| National body (CUPE, CLC, PSAC) | 12–24 weeks | Procurement process, legal review |
| Professional association | 4–8 weeks | Board approval, budget cycle |

**Implication:** Start with mid-size local and provincial unions for first signed agreements. National bodies are long-term plays.

## 5. Founder-Led Sales Operating Rules

1. **Every week, send at least 5 outreach messages.** Not 5 LinkedIn likes. 5 real outreach attempts.
2. **Every week, complete at least 1 discovery or readiness briefing call.** Pipeline dies without call volume.
3. **Never pitch before you've listened.** First call = their workflow, their pain, their team. Not a demo.
4. **Proposal within 24 hours of demo.** Momentum dies after 48 hours.
5. **Follow up 3 times before marking paused.** Most deals require 3–5 touches. One email is not a sales process.
6. **Never discount without a reason.** Design partner discount is the only standing exception (20% off Year 1 SaaS, first 3 orgs).
7. **Every signed pilot = a case study commitment built in.** Ask on Day 1, not at the end.
8. **No custom builds without a signed scope.** Interest in a feature is not a commitment to build it.
9. **Pipeline visibility weekly.** If you haven't updated the command center, the pipeline doesn't exist.
10. **Lost deals are data.** Log every decline with a reason. Pattern recognition is a commercial asset.

## 9. Decision Rules

| Situation | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| Prospect wants a feature we don't have | Log in Tab 5 (Product Requests). Do not commit. Evaluate after 2+ requests for same thing. |
| Prospect asks for free trial | No. Offer a paid Discovery pilot. This signals seriousness. |
| Prospect wants multi-year contract without a pilot | Pilot is mandatory. No exceptions. Offer a 30-day Discovery to accelerate. |
| Prospect needs integration with a specific system | Scope it first, price it separately. Never bundle unknown scope into a fixed pilot fee. |
| Two equally strong prospects — which to prioritize? | The one with an identified internal champion. No champion = long cycle. |
| Prospect goes quiet after demo | 3 follow-ups over 10 days. Then pause and log reason. |
| Inbound inquiry arrives | Treat as priority 1. Respond same business day. |
| Prospect wants to negotiate price down | Design partner discount is the only standing lever. Beyond that, scope reduction, not price reduction. |
| Pilot is struggling mid-execution | Escalate to weekly executive check-in. Do not wait for the pilot to fail. |
| Pilot completes but org is undecided | Deliver the outcome report within 5 days. Schedule a decision call within 2 weeks. |

## 10. When to Hire First GTM Staff

**Do not hire GTM staff until all three conditions are true:**

1. At least 3 SaaS customers paying recurring revenue
2. Founder has a documented, repeatable sales process (this document + call recordings/notes)
3. The bottleneck is demonstrably capacity, not product or market fit

**What to hire first:**

- **Customer Success Manager** — someone who can run pilots, capture case studies, and own onboarding. Not a seller. This unlocks founder time for pipeline.
- Hiring a seller before CS creates a leaky bucket — they'll sign pilots you can't deliver.

**Expected timing:** Q4 2026–Q1 2027 if base case holds.

## 12. Signals to Re-Evaluate

If any of the following persist past 60 days:

- No calls booked after 30+ outreach attempts
- Calls happening but no proposals requested
- Proposals sent but no pilots signed after 3+ proposals
- Active pilot but no expansion conversation by Week 6

**Response:** Do not build more product. Do a post-mortem on the outreach approach, discovery questions, and proposal quality. The problem is almost certainly positioning or process, not product.
