## I. Product doctrine

Union Eyes shall provide a governed environment for authorized institutional oversight, independent review, procedural accountability, compliance assessment, evidence preservation and corrective-action verification.

The governance function must be capable of reviewing institutional conduct without becoming the institution's general executive, operational administrator, financial manager, grievance representative or technical system operator.

The following five powers remain independent:

1. Authority to monitor or oversee.
2. Authority to obtain specific evidence.
3. Authority to investigate and issue findings.
4. Authority to require, recommend or refer corrective action.
5. Authority to enforce or certify a binding outcome.

None follows automatically from the others. Every power requires an effective institutional mandate and a valid resource-specific, purpose-specific authorization.

## II. Frozen navigation

Exactly seven primary destinations:

1. **Today** — assigned reviews, urgent exceptions, evidence requests, risk alerts and decisions requiring attention.
2. **Mandates & Policies** — current governing instruments, authorized mandates, office terms, delegations and policy lifecycles.
3. **Reviews & Complaints** — protected intake, independent assignment, investigations, findings, reconsideration and referrals.
4. **Privacy & Records** — information rights, incidents, disclosure restrictions, classification, retention and legal holds.
5. **Democratic Integrity** — election procedures, voting safeguards, member-rights protections, quorum and procedural challenges.
6. **Audit & Evidence** — oversight work, assurance records, findings, evidence provenance, exports and corrective-action verification.
7. **Risk & Continuity** — institutional risks, exceptions, monitoring, leadership transitions and unresolved governance obligations.

Documents, investigation interviews, correspondence, review tasks and corrective-action assignments shall be contextual to these destinations. Personal profile, language preferences, privacy and account security remain outside primary navigation. No additional primary navigation shall be introduced without canonical review.

## IV. Three governance ownership boundaries

### A. Customer-union governance
Each union, local, national organization, council or federation retains authority over its applicable governing instruments, member processes, institutional decisions and internal records. The institution's officers, committees, members and independent reviewers act according to their actual legal and organizational mandates.

### B. Independent oversight
A reviewer may hold a specific institutional mandate to assess conduct, evidence, election integrity, finance, privacy or governance procedures. Such an appointment must be recorded with its scope, effective dates, source of authority, conflicts, permissible actions, disclosure limits and review rights. Independence from the actor under review is mandatory wherever the governing procedure requires it.

### C. Nzila / Union Eyes platform governance
Nzila's own security, privacy, software governance, corporate governance, platform controls and reserved-matter arrangements remain distinct. Platform compliance and technical security staff do not automatically become governance officers of customer unions. Customer-union officers do not automatically administer platform corporate governance. System-context database access must never serve as an implicit cross-institutional governance delegation.

## V. Authority and confidentiality

Every protected action must evaluate: authenticated actor; active institution or legal entity; verified current oversight appointment; governing policy, resolution or delegated authority; valid review purpose; assigned review or investigation; resource-specific evidence grant; information classification; conflicts and recusals; applicable temporal restrictions; cross-institutional disclosure authority; revocation and expiry; independent certification or enforcement requirements, where applicable.

A general governance role, seniority level, system administrator permission or software entitlement is never sufficient to disclose restricted records. Audit visibility does not imply document-content visibility. Access to an institutional decision does not imply access to the underlying privileged case. Reviewers may be authorized to inspect evidence without possessing authority to change it.

## VI. Independent review lifecycle

The following stages are distinct:

1. Concern, complaint or finding received.
2. Identity and submission authority validated.
3. Jurisdiction and review scope assessed.
4. Independence and conflicts assessed.
5. Reviewer assigned and authorized.
6. Evidence requested and securely obtained.
7. Interviews or investigation steps conducted where permitted.
8. Evidence assessed and reasons recorded.
9. Finding or recommendation proposed.
10. Authorized decision, certification or referral issued.
11. Corrective action assigned to the responsible authority.
12. Action performed and evidenced.
13. Independent verification conducted where required.
14. Complainant and appropriate stakeholders informed.
15. Matter closed, appealed, reopened or transferred under valid procedures.
16. History preserved for authorized institutional continuity.

A finding is not automatically a binding decision. A recommendation is not automatically an order. An action marked completed by its owner is not independently verified. An allegation must not be displayed as an established fact before the required process establishes it.

## VII. Protected complaints and ethical independence

Complaints against officers, stewards, financial managers, governance officials or other institutional personnel require protection from conflicted actors. The person implicated in a complaint must not automatically receive access to the allegation, identify a protected complainant, control the investigator, dismiss the matter or alter the evidence. Independent review may require an external reviewer or designated alternate authority. Retaliation risk, confidentiality, procedural fairness, accessibility, response rights and reviewable reasoning must be incorporated into applicable procedures. The system must not automatically render conclusions on legal duties, misconduct or liability.

## VIII. Democracy and election integrity

Election oversight must distinguish: election authority and approved procedure; eligible electorate; nomination eligibility; candidate and election-officer conflicts; notice and access requirements; voter identity verification; ballot secrecy, when applicable; single valid participation under election rules; ballot integrity and controlled tabulation; preliminary results; challenges, recounts and corrections; independent certification; official publication and retention.

Individual ballots, identifiers, cryptographic artifacts and vote records must not be exposed in ways that compromise ballot secrecy or reveal protected voting choices. Organizational elections, ratifications, union meeting votes and Nzila corporate reserved-matter votes are distinct domains. No UI, database record or unit test alone certifies a lawful institutional election.

## IX. Privacy and information rights

Privacy workflows must begin by establishing the correct institution, capacity, jurisdiction, activity, applicable law and accountable decision-maker. The architecture must accommodate PIPEDA and relevant provincial privacy law, including Québec's Law 25 framework where applicable. The legal tests, notification obligations, exceptions and deadlines must derive from verified authoritative sources and appropriately reviewed institutional policy. Never use hardcoded affected-person counts or generalized clock deadlines as the sole determinant of breach reportability.

A privacy incident must distinguish: detection; containment; investigation; information classification; harm-risk assessment; applicability and legal review; decision about required notification or reporting; actual dispatch; recipient acknowledgement where supported; mitigation; records and follow-up. A data-subject request must distinguish receipt, identity verification, scope, lawful exemptions, response authorization, delivery and completion.

Classification-policy CRUD is not a breach-management engine and is not a DSAR workflow. Where an operational legal assessment is absent, require human review and display an appropriately qualified status rather than a definitive legal conclusion.

## X. Evidence, retention and privilege

Evidence must preserve provenance, source identity, collection authority, classification, integrity and chain-of-custody information as applicable. Review access must remain separate from modification and disclosure rights. Legal holds, preservation notices, retention exceptions, version history and approved disposition require governed lifecycles. Counsel privilege, WorkAid professional confidentiality, protected complaints and sensitive medical information require separately authorized evidence scopes. A legal hold must prevent incompatible deletion or mutation where the hold applies. An exported evidence package cannot disclose information the requesting reviewer is not entitled to receive.

## XI. Audit and remedial accountability

The governance experience must support independent verification that an identified deficiency was actually corrected. The remediation lifecycle distinguishes: finding issued; response requested; responsible authority designated; plan accepted or returned; action in progress; owner reports completion; evidence submitted; reviewer verifies effectiveness; finding closed or reopened. An owner cannot automatically certify the effectiveness of their own remediation. Audit evidence must remain retrievable under valid controls, including after personnel transitions. Missing records, expired sources, failed audit endpoints and unavailable evidence must not be represented as clean findings.

## XII. External and cross-institutional relationships

The architecture must accommodate: CAPE/ACEP governance and independent review; CUPE local and National relationships; CLC and affiliated institutional governance; LIUNA OPDC/CECOF-style independent legal entities; volunteer-led local governance; external counsel and independent investigators; WorkAid professional engagements; financial trustees and auditors; regulatory or statutory oversight where applicable.

National, regional and federation status does not automatically authorize raw access to local confidential records. WorkAid's professional engagement, clinical/medical information, Clio files and filing validity remain under independent professional control. Union-side referral decisions and data-sharing compliance may be reviewed within an expressly authorized governance mandate.

## XIII. AI and automated assurance

AI may assist with: identifying missing procedural evidence; summarizing authorized records; detecting inconsistent policy application; drafting review questions; highlighting potential risk; organizing governance histories.

AI shall not independently determine misconduct, certify legal compliance, impose sanctions, invalidate votes, close complaints, authorize sensitive disclosure or replace an independent reviewer. Every material AI-assisted output must preserve source scope, provenance, uncertainty and accountable human disposition. No unsupported surveillance or scoring of members, employees or union officials.

## XIV. Continuity and succession

Governance continuity must preserve open complaints, investigations, policy obligations, privacy incidents, audit findings, election challenges, corrective actions, governing instruments and independent evidence. Departure, suspension or replacement of a reviewer must cause appropriate mandate reassessment and obsolete-access revocation. Successors acquire only validated review mandates and specifically permitted records. No personal credential or privileged disclosure grant transfers automatically with an office title.

## XV. Operational acceptance

Every enabled capability must identify: UI entry point; authenticated reviewer and institution; governing source of authority; effective review mandate; resource-level disclosure rules; server-owned workflow; persisted review state; audit and evidence trail; actual communication or notification status; negative authorization proof; native browser acceptance; exact candidate provenance.

Capability dispositions: OPERATIONAL, LIMITED, UNAVAILABLE, BLOCKED or NOT_APPLICABLE. Source inspection, synthetic tests, explanatory pages and screenshots cannot independently establish OPERATIONAL.

## XVI. Change control

Frozen elements: the seven primary navigation destinations; the G01–G30 capability ceiling; separation of platform and customer-union governance; the five independent oversight powers; review independence and conflict controls; protected complaint and appeal pathways; democratic confidentiality and certification boundaries; privacy-law source requirements; evidence, legal hold and disclosure boundaries; independent remediation verification; institutional autonomy and WorkAid separation; human-controlled AI; succession and evidence-based release standards. Changes require explicit canonical review.
