# Union Eyes — Meeting Booker Pack

**For:** Founder-led outbound  
**Use when:** Prospect replies, books a call, or you're doing a warm intro follow-up

---

## 10 Opener Lines

Use one of these to open a call or initiate a first real conversation (email, LinkedIn, phone). Adapt to your voice.

1. **Steward load hook:**  
   "I want to understand how your stewards are actually managing their current caseload — not the official process, the real one."

2. **Scale hook:**  
   "You're managing collective agreement rights for [X] thousand workers — tell me what the biggest administrative friction point looks like on a bad week."

3. **Post-legislation hook (ONA / NSNU / CUPE 4400):**  
   "The legislative pressure your members have been under has been significant. I'm curious — has that changed what your stewards are dealing with day to day?"

4. **Healthcare restructuring hook (MNU / SUN / HSA BC):**  
   "Since [Shared Health / SHA / NS Health Authority] consolidated, how has your grievance intake changed? Are you managing more cases with the same team?"

5. **Arbitration credibility hook:**  
   "Your arbitration record is strong. I want to understand what your advocates are spending their time on before a hearing — because that's usually where we find the friction."

6. **Dual-employer hook (NBNU / CUPE 503):**  
   "Running a bilingual local with two employer relationships means your team is managing twice the context on every case. How are you handling that documentation today?"

7. **Multi-unit hook (CUPE 3902):**  
   "With five units in rolling bargaining cycles, your administrative team is essentially never not managing something. What does the most resource-intensive part of that look like?"

8. **Federal procurement hook (CAPE-ACEP):**  
   "Federal unions have more formal grievance adjudication requirements than most. I want to make sure the tool fits your documentation standards — can we walk through a few of your actual case types?"

9. **Cost of the status quo hook:**  
   "If a steward is spending 4 hours building a case file that should take 45 minutes — what's the dollar value of that across your whole steward network in a year?"

10. **Direct ROI hook:**  
    "We've found that a local your size saves roughly 50 hours per month in steward administration time. At a fully loaded rate of $65/hour, that's $3,250/month — more than the pilot costs. Does that math make sense to stress-test?"

## Objection Replies

### "We're already handling it."

> "That's great — and I'd expect that from a well-run local. My question is just whether 'handling it' is taking more time than it should. Can you tell me how your stewards are tracking active cases right now? I'm not trying to replace a working system — I want to understand whether there's a faster version of it."

---

### "We don't have the budget for something like this."

> "Completely fair. The pilot is structured to answer that question before you commit. $12,000 CAD for 90 days — if it doesn't generate enough time savings to justify the full licence, you don't proceed. We've designed it so the ROI case is made within the first 30 days. Would it help if I put a rough number on what your current admin hours are costing you annually?"

---

### "Our members are skeptical of technology."

> "That's a legitimate concern and I hear it from a lot of union executives. Here's what I'd say: Union Eyes isn't sold to members — it's a tool for stewards and staff. The member-facing part is optional and designed to feel like a web portal, not an app. But more importantly, the ROI for the union comes from steward time savings, not member adoption. Can I show you the steward workflow specifically?"

---

### "We had a bad experience with a vendor before."

> "I appreciate you being direct about that. Can I ask what went wrong? I want to understand whether it's the category of tool or a specific implementation failure — because how we structure the pilot is designed specifically to de-risk that. If we can't make it work in 90 days with 20 steward accounts, we haven't earned a full deployment."

---

### "Our IT department needs to approve everything."

> "Of course — and we're set up for that. The procurement checklist I can send you covers data residency (Canadian servers), security posture, access controls, and integration requirements. Your IT team can review it before we go any further. Want me to send that today so you can see whether we even pass the gate?"

---

### "We're not looking to change our process right now."

> "That makes sense — change management is real. I want to be clear: this isn't a process change. It's a documentation upgrade. Your stewards keep doing what they do; it just happens in a system instead of email. But if the timing genuinely isn't right, I'm not going to push. When would be a better moment to revisit this — six months from now? After your next round of negotiations?"

---

### "Can you just send me information?"

> "Absolutely — but I'd rather spend 10 minutes showing you the actual tool than sending a PDF that summarizes it. A product walkthrough will tell you more in 10 minutes than a deck does in 30. Can I book 15 minutes this week? If it's not a fit after that, you'll know immediately."

---

### "We're a small local, we can't afford this."

> "I hear that — and NSNU and NBNU are both smaller organizations that we've looked at specifically because the efficiency gains are actually proportionally larger for a smaller team. Less redundancy to absorb the admin drag. Let's figure out what 'small' means for your caseload — if you have fewer than 200 active grievances at any time, I'll tell you honestly whether the ROI math works."

## "Send Me the Deck" Reply

Just a heads up: the deck covers what Union Eyes does, but the ROI calculator is where most local executives stop and say 'that's actually the case for doing this.' Take a look at that section first. I'll follow up in two days to see if the numbers resonated — and if they do, let's book 15 minutes."

## "Not Now" Nurture Reply

> "No problem — I figured it might not be the right moment. I'll check back in at the start of [Q3 / after your next round of bargaining / after the new year]. In the meantime, if anything changes on your end — a new grievance administration challenge, a new staff member who needs tools, a budget cycle opening up — I'd love to hear about it. I'll make a note to reconnect in [month]."

*Set a calendar reminder. Follow up exactly when you said you would.*

---

## "We Already Have a System" Pivot Reply

> "That's helpful to know. Can I ask what you're using? I'm not assuming we're better — I genuinely want to understand what you have before saying anything else. What I'd want to know is: does your current system track individual grievance chronologies linked to each member's file? And does it give your stewards a self-serve case status view? Those are the two things we hear most often that existing tools don't do well for unions specifically. If yours handles both of those, I'd want to learn from it."

*Goal: build curiosity about the gaps, not compete head-to-head. Let them sell themselves on the gap.*

---

## Quick Call Close

When you have them on a call and need to close to a next step:

> "Based on what you've told me, I think a pilot makes sense to explore. Here's what I'd suggest: I send you the pilot outline tonight — it's one page — and the procurement checklist so your team can do a quick gate review. If both of those clear, we can be live in 14 days. Would that be a reasonable next step?"

---

## Demo Prep — 3 Questions to Ask Before Any Demo

1. "What does a typical grievance intake look like for your stewards today — email, form, phone, something else?"
2. "Who in your organization is the primary person who'd use this day-to-day?"  
3. "What would a successful 90-day pilot look like from your perspective — what would you need to see to feel confident moving forward?"
