## 1. Core Brand Story

Most founders don't have a strategy problem. They have a **Monday morning problem**.

Twelve tabs open. Three half-finished spreadsheets. A Notion doc nobody updates. A team Slack that doubles as a to-do list. And the quiet, constant question every owner asks themselves at 6:47am:

> *"What am I supposed to be doing this week — and is it actually moving the business forward?"*

WeekOne was built for that moment.

It's not another project tool. It's not a CRM. It's not a dashboard you have to feed.
It's a **weekly operating rhythm** — focus, money, risks, growth — assembled into one calm surface, so an owner can sit down for fifteen minutes on Monday and walk out knowing exactly what matters, what's at risk, and what's next.

We believe small teams deserve the same operational clarity that big companies pay six-figure consultants for. WeekOne is that clarity, productized — pre-loaded with the structure, prompts, and AI assistance that turn chaos into cadence.

**One week at a time. One source of truth. One brain that doesn't drop things.**

## 3. Five Emotional Pain Narratives

Each one is a story arc, not a feature complaint. Use these in ads, email, sales decks, and onboarding.

### Pain 1 — The Sunday Dread
*"Every Sunday night I open my laptop, look at twelve tabs, and feel the week slipping before it's even started. I don't know what to focus on, only that there's too much."*
**WeekOne reframe:** Sunday becomes a 10-minute setup. Monday becomes execution.

### Pain 2 — The Spreadsheet That Lies
*"I built a beautiful spreadsheet six months ago. Nobody updates it. I don't trust the numbers anymore — and I'm making decisions on vibes."*
**WeekOne reframe:** Live, structured, lightweight. The numbers stay true because the system is built around how you actually work.

### Pain 3 — The Quiet Cash Panic
*"I think we're fine. I'm probably fine. I'll check the bank account in a second. I'm definitely fine."*
**WeekOne reframe:** Money pillar shows runway, inflows, outflows, and what to chase — without forcing you to become a bookkeeper.

### Pain 4 — The Team That Can't Read Your Mind
*"My team is talented but they keep asking me what's important. I'm the bottleneck. I am the org chart."*
**WeekOne reframe:** A weekly source of truth your team can read in two minutes. You stop being the human Slack channel.

### Pain 5 — The Tool Graveyard
*"I've tried Notion, Asana, ClickUp, Airtable, Trello, three CRMs and a journal. They all start great. They all end abandoned."*
**WeekOne reframe:** WeekOne is opinionated. It doesn't ask you to design a system. The system is the product.

## 4. Five Customer Personas

### Persona 1 — "Maya, the Solo Founder"
- **Stage:** Year 1–2, $0–$200K ARR, no team yet.
- **Pain:** Doing everything. Forgetting half of it. No structure to her week.
- **Trigger to buy:** A bad week where she missed an invoice and lost a lead in the same 72 hours.
- **Message that lands:** *"Run your week like a real company — even if it's just you."*

### Persona 2 — "Daniel, the Owner-Operator"
- **Stage:** 5–25 employees, services or trades, $500K–$5M revenue.
- **Pain:** Spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, gut feel. No visibility into what's actually working.
- **Trigger to buy:** Cash got tight one month and he didn't see it coming.
- **Message that lands:** *"Stop running your business from memory."*

### Persona 3 — "Priya, the Agency Lead"
- **Stage:** 4–15 person creative/marketing agency.
- **Pain:** Client work eats internal work. The agency itself never gets a strategy session.
- **Trigger to buy:** Lost a great client because internal ops were a mess.
- **Message that lands:** *"Your agency deserves the same rigor you sell your clients."*

### Persona 4 — "Tomás, the Lean Nonprofit Director"
- **Stage:** 3–10 person nonprofit, grant-funded.
- **Pain:** Reporting to a board, a funder, and a team — with no operating cadence.
- **Trigger to buy:** Quarterly board prep that took 11 hours of scrambling.
- **Message that lands:** *"One weekly rhythm. Board-ready in fifteen minutes."*

### Persona 5 — "Renée, the Growing Service Business Owner"
- **Stage:** Clinic, studio, or boutique consultancy. 8–30 staff. Stable revenue, foggy growth.
- **Pain:** She knows the business is plateauing. She doesn't know which lever to pull.
- **Trigger to buy:** A competitor lapped her on something she should've seen.
- **Message that lands:** *"Find the one move that compounds — and actually do it."*

## 5. Service + SaaS Hybrid Offers

The moat is **the rhythm**. Sell the rhythm in three forms:

### Tier A — WeekOne **Self-Serve** (pure SaaS)
The product. Onboarding flow, weekly cadence, AI prompts, dashboards.
*For:* solo founders, lean teams who want to install the discipline themselves.

### Tier B — WeekOne **Setup Sprint** (SaaS + done-with-you)
A one-time, fixed-fee 2-week onboarding led by a WeekOne strategist. Outcome: your first 90 days of focus, money, risks, and growth installed inside the product, with your team trained.
*For:* owner-operators and agencies who want it working in 14 days, not 14 weeks.

### Tier C — WeekOne **Operator** (SaaS + fractional ops)
Monthly retainer. A WeekOne operator joins your Monday review, keeps the system honest, surfaces risks early, and packages weekly insights for you and your team.
*For:* nonprofits, growing service businesses, and founders who know they'll never run the cadence consistently alone.

> **Why this works:** Self-serve creates the funnel. Setup Sprint creates the lighthouse customers. Operator creates the recurring relationship that makes churn nearly impossible.

## 6. Pricing Psychology

**Principles:**
- **Anchor on the cost of chaos**, not on competitor SaaS pricing. (One missed invoice. One lost client. One bad hire.)
- **Three tiers. Middle tier wins.** Always design for the middle to be the obvious choice.
- **No "free forever."** Free trials, yes. A free tier teaches the wrong behavior — that the rhythm is optional.
- **Annual = relief, not discount.** Frame annual billing as *"set it and forget it for a year"* — not *"save 20%."*

### Suggested Structure

| Plan | Price | Positioning Line |
|---|---|---|
| **Solo** | $29 / mo | *"For founders running the whole show."* |
| **Team** ⭐ | $89 / mo (up to 10 seats) | *"For small teams who want one source of truth."* |
| **Operator** | from $1,500 / mo | *"WeekOne + a fractional operator who runs the rhythm with you."* |

**Setup Sprint:** $2,500 one-time. Position as: *"The fastest way from chaos to cadence."*

**Psychological levers to use on the pricing page:**
- "Cancel anytime. We'd rather you stay because it works."
- "Most founders save 4–6 hours in their first week."
- "Less than one missed invoice. Less than one bad Monday."

## Brand Voice Quick-Reference

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Speak like a calm operator | Speak like a hype-driven SaaS startup |
| Name the feeling first, the feature second | Lead with feature lists |
| Use short, declarative sentences | Use jargon, AI buzzwords, "synergy" |
| Respect the reader's intelligence | Talk down or over-explain |
| Show the Monday, not the dashboard | Show 14 charts in the hero image |

**One sentence to govern all copy:**
> *Write like you're texting a smart friend at 7am on Monday — and you want them to feel calmer by 7:01.*
