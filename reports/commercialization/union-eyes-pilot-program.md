# Union Eyes — Standardized Pilot Program

**Version:** 1.0 (June 2026)
**Audience:** Local unions, hospital locals, municipal locals, education locals, and small federated organizations.
**Positioning:** Product-led pilot deployment, not consulting-led custom delivery.

---

## 1. Pilot Overview

### 1.1 Purpose

The Union Eyes Pilot Program is a bounded, measurable, 90-day operational deployment that moves a prospect from stakeholder confidence to production decision.

The pilot is designed to answer one question:

**Can this organization materially improve continuity, coordination, and governance clarity using Union Eyes under real operating conditions?**

The pilot is not a feature exploration sandbox. It is an implementation with clear entry criteria, scope lock, success thresholds, and exit paths.

### 1.2 Expected Outcomes

By the end of the pilot, the organization should have:

1. A live organizational workspace configured for real governance workflows.
2. Tracked operational activity (cases, updates, role-based interactions) sufficient for evaluation.
3. A continuity and adoption scorecard based on measured behaviors, not anecdotal impressions.
4. A month-3 decision package: proceed to annual engagement, extend under constrained scope, or close with no-fit.

### 1.3 Ideal Customer Profile

The best fit for this standard pilot shape:

1. **Organization profile:** one primary local organization with a clear internal sponsor.
2. **Scale profile:** up to ~200 members in the pilot scope and up to 5 worksites.
3. **Sector profile:** healthcare, municipal/public service, education, and adjacent coordinated local operations.
4. **Operational profile:** existing workflow friction (manual tracking, fragmented records, inconsistent handling, weak auditability).
5. **Readiness profile:** willingness to run a 90-day program with named accountable roles.

### 1.4 Not-a-Fit Criteria

Do not run this standard pilot when any of the following are true:

1. Multi-employer or multi-federation complexity requiring cross-org topology in pilot 1.
2. No executive/steward sponsor who can allocate time and decisions.
3. Procurement/legal posture that cannot support pilot DPA and scope lock within decision timeline.
4. Expectation that pilot includes enterprise-scale customization or long feature commitments.
5. Requirement for broad finance persistence, public APIs, or out-of-scope platform modules before proving operational fit.

## 2. Pilot Structure (Month 0–3)

The pilot is intentionally staged.

## Month 0 — Kickoff (Qualification + Activation)

### Deliverables

1. Confirmed pilot scope lock (in-scope / excluded capabilities).
2. Completed pilot intake and readiness assessment.
3. Signed pilot agreement and deployment prerequisites (DPA, named roles, access readiness).
4. Initial implementation plan and milestone calendar.

### Meetings

1. Sponsor alignment call.
2. Kickoff workshop (governance + continuity outcomes + operational priorities).
3. Technical activation session (account, roles, workspace readiness).

### Artifacts

1. Pilot intake package.
2. Readiness assessment summary.
3. Scope lock annex.
4. Pilot execution calendar.

### Responsibilities

**Union Eyes**
1. Run intake review and readiness scoring.
2. Define pilot milestones and onboarding checklist.
3. Prepare deployment and activation checklist.

**Customer**
1. Assign accountable sponsor and pilot lead.
2. Confirm target users and role ownership.
3. Provide core onboarding data needed for activation.

---

## Month 1 — Deployment (Controlled Rollout)

### Deliverables

1. Pilot workspace activated in pilot environment.
2. User invitations, role assignments, and baseline configuration completed.
3. First operational workflows executed (initial case and update activity).

### Meetings

1. Deployment activation call.
2. Weekly operating review (Week 1–4 cadence).

### Artifacts

1. Onboarding checklist completion snapshot.
2. Initial deployment health report.
3. Week-4 pilot telemetry summary.

### Responsibilities

**Union Eyes**
1. Execute bootstrap and environment activation.
2. Validate onboarding checklist completion.
3. Monitor friction indicators and resolve activation blockers.

**Customer**
1. Complete role assignment and access validation.
2. Drive first-wave user participation.
3. Confirm operational use cases are tracked in the workspace.

---

## Month 2 — Operational Use (Evidence Building)

### Deliverables

1. Sustained usage across targeted stewards/officers.
2. Pilot metrics trendline (adoption, engagement, usage, effectiveness, progress).
3. Mid-pilot friction remediation and workflow stabilization.

### Meetings

1. Weekly operations review.
2. Mid-pilot executive review.

### Artifacts

1. Mid-pilot health scorecard.
2. Friction and adoption interventions log.
3. Updated milestone completion report.

### Responsibilities

**Union Eyes**
1. Run pilot metrics instrumentation and trend reporting.
2. Surface champions and risk cohorts.
3. Recommend concrete interventions tied to success criteria.

**Customer**
1. Maintain active usage discipline.
2. Execute agreed interventions.
3. Confirm leadership review attendance and decisions.

---

## Month 3 — Assessment (Decision Window)

### Deliverables

1. Pilot success review package.
2. Exit-path recommendation (successful / partial / no-fit).
3. If successful: conversion-ready annual engagement pathway.

### Meetings

1. Final pilot review with sponsor and pilot lead.
2. Commercial decision meeting.

### Artifacts

1. End-of-pilot scorecard with outcomes against targets.
2. Lessons learned and continuity recommendations.
3. Commercial next-step pack (if proceeding).

### Responsibilities

**Union Eyes**
1. Publish final pilot assessment.
2. Present clear recommendation and next-step options.
3. Prepare conversion package where fit is validated.

**Customer**
1. Validate pilot outcomes against business goals.
2. Decide exit path within agreed decision window.
3. Confirm next-step agreement or closeout.

## 3. Pilot Success Criteria

The standard pilot uses measurable operational criteria mapped to existing Union Eyes scoring dimensions.

### 3.1 Core Outcome Domains

1. **Operational adoption**
   - Active steward/officer participation rate.
   - Repeated workflow usage across the pilot period.

2. **Case and documentation discipline**
   - Cases tracked in-system versus ad hoc channels.
   - Evidence and updates maintained with traceability.

3. **Governance and visibility improvement**
   - Leadership-level visibility into pilot operations.
   - Reduction of ambiguity in process status.

4. **Continuity improvement**
   - Better retention and handoff of organizational knowledge.
   - Reduced dependency on individual memory/workarounds.

5. **Execution progress**
   - Milestones completed as planned.
   - Time-to-first-value and week-over-week stabilization.

### 3.2 Metrics to Avoid (Vanity)

Do not evaluate pilot success on:

1. Raw feature clicks.
2. Session duration alone.
3. Total users invited without active usage.
4. Marketing engagement metrics unrelated to operations.

### 3.3 Success Threshold Posture

For each pilot, define target thresholds at kickoff for:

1. Minimum active users.
2. Minimum tracked operational volume.
3. Minimum milestone completion rate.
4. Minimum governance review confidence.

A pilot is successful when those thresholds are met with stable usage behavior, not one-time spikes.

## 4. Pilot Exit Paths

### A. Successful Pilot

**Definition**
- Core thresholds met.
- Operational behavior sustained.
- Leadership confirms clear value and continuity uplift.

**Next Steps**
1. Move to annual engagement pathway.
2. Finalize subscription/engagement structure.
3. Expand scope in phased rollout sequence.

### B. Partial Success

**Definition**
- Material progress but one or more threshold domains under target.
- Value is visible but not yet durable.

**Next Steps**
1. Short, bounded extension plan with explicit remediation targets.
2. Narrowed scope and focused intervention on weak domains.
3. Decision checkpoint at extension close.

### C. No Fit

**Definition**
- Core thresholds not met and intervention path is not justified.

**Next Steps**
1. Structured pilot closeout with evidence summary.
2. Data export and continuity handoff package.
3. Option to re-engage under revised conditions in future.

---

## 5. Product-Led Guardrails

To preserve product-led delivery and avoid consulting drift:

1. Keep pilot scope lock standard.
2. Use existing readiness and health scoring surfaces; do not invent bespoke scorecards per deal.
3. Restrict custom feature commitments during pilot.
4. Use a fixed pilot cadence and artifact set for every customer.
5. Route all commercial decisions through explicit month-3 exit paths.

The pilot is a product conversion mechanism. Services support adoption; they do not replace the product as the value driver.
