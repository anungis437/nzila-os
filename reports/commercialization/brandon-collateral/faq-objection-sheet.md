# Union Eyes — FAQ / Objection Sheet

Audience: Skeptical stakeholders and internal reviewers
Date: 2026-06-02

---

## 1. "We already have Union365. Why do we need this?"

Union Eyes is not positioned as a rip-and-replace pilot.
The pilot validates whether steward and leadership workflows can run with better continuity, visibility, and follow-up discipline using a bounded operational layer.

---

## 2. "Is this replacing our existing systems?"

No. The pilot is scoped to operational workflow improvement and decision support.
Replacement decisions, if any, are separate and only considered after pilot outcomes are validated.

---

## 3. "How much work is required from our team?"

Expected effort is structured:
1. Week 0 kickoff and scope lock.
2. Named pilot lead and sponsor.
3. Weekly review cadence.
4. Timely feedback on friction and blockers.

The model is designed to be practical for an active local team, not a heavy transformation project.

---

## 4. "What data is needed to start?"

Initial pilot intake requires:
1. Core organization profile.
2. Priority workflows/challenges/goals.
3. Basic onboarding and role setup details.
4. Any hard constraints for compliance and operations.

No broad data migration commitment is required for pilot start.

---

## 5. "How is success measured?"

Success is measured using objective dimensions:
1. Fit, readiness, revenue, strategic value, risk, and overall opportunity.
2. Operational adoption and activity progression.
3. Week-over-week execution signals and month-3 assessment.

The decision is evidence-based, not narrative-only.

---

## 6. "What happens after 90 days?"

Three outcomes:
1. Successful -> convert to annual engagement with full pilot fee credit-forward.
2. Partial -> constrained extension with explicit remediation targets.
3. No fit -> structured closeout and clean exit.

---

## 7. "Are we locked into a long contract after pilot?"

No automatic lock-in is assumed by the pilot itself.
The pilot is a bounded decision phase designed to validate fit before annual commitment.

---

## 8. "What if our team encounters friction during the pilot?"

Friction is expected and treated as signal.
The weekly pilot cadence includes friction detection, intervention tracking, and course correction.

---

## 9. "How does this help leadership, not just stewards?"

Leadership gets:
1. Better lifecycle visibility.
2. Structured status and risk signals.
3. End-of-pilot decision package based on observable outcomes.

---

## 10. "Why run this now?"

Running a controlled pilot now gives decision-makers evidence quickly while risk remains bounded by scope and timeline.
Deferring preserves current friction without generating operational proof.
