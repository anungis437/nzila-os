## 4. ROI, Payback, and Break-Even

Primary metrics for board approval:
1. ROI percentage = (net annual benefit / total year-1 cost of ownership) x 100
2. Payback period (months) = (total year-1 cost of ownership / total expected annual value) x 12
3. Break-even value threshold = total year-1 cost of ownership

Interpretation:
1. If payback is under 12 months and risk posture improves, conversion is financially and operationally justified.
2. If payback is over 12 months, proceed only with explicit remediation assumptions and tighter scope.

---

## 5. Scenario Analysis (Conservative to Upside)

### Conservative case

Assumptions:
1. Only 30% to 40% of projected time recovery realized.
2. Minimal continuity-risk events avoided.
3. Slower adoption curve through first half-year.

Use when:
1. Sponsor wants strict downside stress test.

### Base case

Assumptions:
1. 50% to 70% of projected workflow efficiency gains realized.
2. Measurable follow-up reliability and continuity improvements.
3. Adoption stabilizes by end of quarter two.

Use when:
1. Pilot signals align with expected operating cadence.

### Upside case

Assumptions:
1. Strong adoption and durable weekly usage.
2. Material reduction in coordination drag and preventable delays.
3. Expanded benefit across adjacent leadership workflows.

Use when:
1. Pilot exceeds target in adoption, activity, and risk trend metrics.

---

## 6. Pilot-to-Conversion Financial Gate

Proceed to annual conversion only if at pilot close:
1. Adoption trend is stable or improving.
2. At least two of three value levers show measurable movement.
3. Base-case payback is within 12 months.
4. Sponsor and pilot lead confirm operating-fit confidence.

Do not convert yet if:
1. Value movement is mostly anecdotal.
2. Governance and follow-up discipline are not improving.
3. Measured friction remains concentrated in Tier 1 essentials.
