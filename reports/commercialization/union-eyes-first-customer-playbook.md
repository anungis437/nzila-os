# Union Eyes — First Customer Playbook (CUPE 4373 Motion)

**Prospect context:** CUPE local profile (single hospital local, ~200-member scale), successful demo completed, decision intent before end of month, competitive pressure active.

---

## 1. Commercial Objective

Convert demo confidence into a signed bounded pilot without introducing ad hoc consulting scope.

The first-customer objective is not maximizing pilot price.
The objective is proving repeatable pilot conversion with measurable product outcomes.

---

## 2. Why This Prospect Fits Now

### Validated from conversation and platform constraints

1. Prospect has seen an operational environment and can imagine day-to-day use.
2. Prospect asks deployment and decision questions (not abstract feature questions).
3. Organizational size aligns with existing controlled pilot scope discipline.
4. Existing pilot infrastructure already supports this motion.

### Practical fit check

1. One local organization profile.
2. Hospital operations context with continuity/governance needs.
3. End-of-month decision timeline creates urgency and commercial clarity.

---

## 3. Offer Shape (Modified gamma)

### Pilot Engagement Structure

1. **Fixed price**
2. **90-day bounded scope**
3. **Includes kickoff workshop**
4. **Includes deployment activation**
5. **Includes onboarding and weekly operating reviews**
6. **Includes month-3 success assessment**

### Risk-reduction clause

**100% of pilot fees are credited toward the first annual engagement if they proceed.**

This clause should be presented as a confidence and alignment mechanism.

### Why not alpha now

At this stage (successful demo, zero signed pilot references), the priority is conversion and referenceability, not aggressive anchor pricing.

---

## 4. Intake Package for CUPE 4373

Use this as the official intake set sent to the prospect immediately.

## A. Organization and Sponsorship

1. Local name and number.
2. Primary contact (name, role, email, phone).
3. Executive sponsor (name and role).
4. Current member count.
5. Employer structure (single employer or multi-site).

## B. Operating Context

6. Province/territory.
7. Sector classification.
8. Any immediate labor cycle events affecting timeline.
9. Current workflow tools/systems.
10. Top three current operational friction points.

## C. Pilot Goals

11. Top three pilot outcomes required for success.
12. Which workflows are highest priority in month 1.
13. Required visibility for leadership review.
14. Any non-negotiable compliance/privacy constraints.

## D. Deployment Readiness

15. Day-to-day pilot lead.
16. Estimated number of active users during pilot.
17. Data readiness for onboarding.
18. IT/security review requirements.
19. Constraints on implementation timeline.

## E. Commercial and Decision

20. Decision deadline confirmation.
21. Budget posture (range or guidance).
22. Approval stakeholders and sequence.
23. Competing options under evaluation.
24. Conditions that would trigger no-go decision.

---

## 5. 90-Day Execution Blueprint

## Month 0 (Week 0)

1. Confirm scope lock and success criteria.
2. Confirm pilot agreement terms and credit-forward clause.
3. Finalize implementation calendar and participants.

## Month 1 (Weeks 1–4)

1. Activate pilot workspace.
2. Complete onboarding checklist.
3. Launch first real workflows.
4. Weekly adoption/friction review.

## Month 2 (Weeks 5–8)

1. Expand operational use to target users.
2. Run mid-pilot scorecard review.
3. Execute remediation actions for weak metrics.

## Month 3 (Weeks 9–12)

1. Final success review.
2. Decide exit path: proceed, partial extension, or no-fit closeout.
3. If proceed: convert to first annual engagement with full pilot fee credit.

---

## 6. Communication Cadence (Prospect-Facing)

### Immediate (Day 0)

Message:
- confirm readiness to proceed
- share pilot intake URL
- lock 30-minute discovery call window

### Day 1–3

1. Intake completed.
2. Discovery call held.
3. Proposal issued.

### Day 4–7

1. Handle objections and alignment clarifications.
2. Confirm decision pathway and timing.

### Week 2 onward

1. Sign pilot.
2. Trigger deployment and onboarding.

---

## 7. Proposal Template (First Customer)

Every proposal should include the same sections:

1. Executive summary (problem + why now).
2. Pilot scope and exclusions.
3. 90-day timeline.
4. Success criteria and scorecard approach.
5. Implementation and support model.
6. Pilot commercial terms.
7. Credit-forward conversion clause.
8. Decision and next-step timeline.

This should be generated from intake/readiness data, not manually re-authored each time.

---

## 8. Pilot Agreement / SOW Checklist

The agreement package should define:

1. Scope boundaries.
2. Roles and responsibilities.
3. Data/privacy terms.
4. Support and escalation expectations.
5. Success review process.
6. Exit paths and conversion clause.
7. Change-control rules (avoid consulting drift).

---

## 9. Conversion Paths at Month 3

### A. Proceed

1. Apply 100% pilot-fee credit to annual engagement.
2. Activate first annual plan.
3. Publish rollout expansion plan.

### B. Partial

1. Define bounded extension conditions.
2. Re-test specific success criteria.
3. Reopen conversion decision at extension close.

### C. No Fit

1. Deliver closeout report.
2. Complete data and knowledge handoff.
3. End engagement cleanly.

---

## 10. First-Customer Success Definition

For this first customer, success means:

1. Signed pilot agreement within the target decision window.
2. Pilot activation with on-time onboarding completion.
3. Measured operational usage and continuity improvements by month 3.
4. Clear commercial decision with either conversion or disciplined no-fit closeout.
5. A reusable reference motion for the next two pilot prospects.

This is the bridge from promising demo to repeatable commercialization.
