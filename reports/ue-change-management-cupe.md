# Union Eyes Change Management For A CUPE Pilot

## Core Principle

The first live CUPE deployment should be framed as a controlled operating change, not a feature launch.

## Why

1. The product already contains both strong and weak paths.
2. Adoption risk is political and procedural, not only technical.
3. The pilot succeeds if staff trust the workflow and members trust the handling of their cases.

## Change Model

1. Phase 1: steward-assisted intake and case handling.
2. Phase 2: controlled member self-serve rollout after the intake and upload rewiring is complete.
3. Phase 3: broader admin and reporting exposure after denial-path and cross-org tests are proven.

## Governance Cadence

1. Daily check-in during the first two weeks.
2. Weekly union-platform review on case throughput, failures, and member complaints.
3. Formal decision gate before expanding beyond the first local.

## Metrics To Watch

1. Case submission success rate.
2. Time to assignment.
3. Time to first acknowledgment.
4. Evidence export success rate.
5. Privacy or access complaints.

## Red Flags That Trigger Rollback

1. Member submission or upload failures.
2. Cross-org or wrong-role access anomalies.
3. Missing audit trail entries for pilot-critical actions.
4. Operators using legacy paths because the approved path is unclear.
